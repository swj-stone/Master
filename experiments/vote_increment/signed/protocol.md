# Signed coalition voting on development sources

Use all six legacy prepared tasks at seed 42 in the first screen. All are previously opened development sources. On each task use the native 100-round XGBoost policy, CPU thread 12, original fit split, separate voting and validation partitions, and refit the final model on all development rows. The direct comparator uses native mean split Gain, with its usual decreasing order. Give all three selectors the same cutoff fractions, same four-at-most proposal evaluations including the full reference, and same validation guard: accept the first (narrowest) support whose validation AUC or R² lies within .005 of the full reference. Each positive evidence distribution nominates widths at half, three quarters and nine tenths of its mass, deduplicated, plus full width. The exploratory controls differ only by the supplied score vector. Keep accuracy/MAE and actual individual timing information.

The first supervised game uses small, medium and intact randomly restricted decision trees. Features can be substitutes in one subspace and complements in another. For each split, the vote is the signed improvement in squared score on disjoint vote rows from replacing its parent's fitted response by its child's. Average by feature inclusion opportunities at that size and normalize by the total absolute stratum weight. The minimum and the mean aggregate exactly the same signed scores. For the control, apply the identical support-mass rule to the fitted XGBoost Gain. Record all diagnostic tree fits separately from downstream refits; a cheap refit is still a fit. Never use outer utility to steer any individual job.

For each comparator the decision counts a source when both held primary losses ≤.01, and (a) strictly fewer features, or (b) fewer actual fits and faster whole pipeline with width within max(1,.05 × baseline width). Also give stricter speed+width, corresponding accuracy/MAE and true times without claiming help from timing noise alone. Subsequent prespecified changes to the game and the widths remain exploratory. A repeated win on any opened dataset is not independent replication.

The second game compares two fitting landmarks for each voting row, one nearby in response and one farther away. Each column's signed margin is the distance to the farther response minus its distance to the nearer response, after fitting-sample marginal rank transforms. A coalition casts a positive vote when its total margin is positive, a negative vote on an ordering reversal and half credit at a tie. Column utility is the change in contest utility on entering the coalition. Compute the empty and intact-minus-one marginal exactly; at middle cardinality approximate the margin of the other columns by a Gaussian with its exact without-replacement mean and variance. This avoids fitting auxiliary trees. Pool uniformly drawn and nearest-landmark comparisons. The acceptance procedure, direct control and outer analysis stay fixed.

In the third experiment pair ballots price the fitted native model's own score. Approximate TreeSHAP contributions must reconstruct every raw model margin on the sampled voting rows within numerical tolerance. Pair positives and negatives within each class for a ranking game on centered class margins. Estimate the signed chance that including a feature changes a pair from incorrect to correct, or conversely, with panels at approximately one eighth, one half, and seven eighths of all other inputs. In regression use analytic expected changes in squared error on the additive SHAP sum. The first width covers 75% of positive vote mass, followed by the geometric mean of that width and full width and their arithmetic mean, then full width. This increases recovery range for concentrated vote masses after very small prefixes failed to meet the guard in the initial experiment. Apply the same width policy to Gain and the mean. Record the direct baseline anew since the search policy changed.

The fourth variant uses the native margin ballots to nominate deletions. Each surviving candidate pays an opportunity cost equal to the largest available positive ballot of another column multiplied by its squared Spearman correlation with that candidate. Retire only the lowest negative net column, recomputing every strongest surviving substitute after retirement. This is a proxy for substitution, without a claim that correlation suffices for exact refit invariance. Signed minima and means use precisely the same input, shrinkage and dynamic retirement rule. Within the resulting survivor set use ordinary native Gain order. Probe widths covering 50%, 85% and 100% of that set, then full width. If the survivor set is intact, use the ordinary Gain 50%, 85%, 95%, and full widths. Native direct Gain enters without an upstream voting screen, and uses the same cap and refit guard. Include a matched correlation-only Gain ablation in secondary analysis, scoring the Gain share as a single stratum.
