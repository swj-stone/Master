# Changing the masking predictor and the receiving learner

We retained the original XGBoost Gain anchors, feature rankings, 216 half-width candidates, proposal rows, restoration families and tolerances. Ten tasks contributed 30 folds; a second set of six wider tasks contributed 16. The existing logistic experiment already included the first panel, but used boosted trees to nominate every candidate. Here a 100-tree random forest also fitted the masking predictor on all training features. Each forest candidate could then be guarded and refitted by a fresh forest, the archived boosted-tree receiver, or the archived logistic receiver. XGBoost nominations were also paired with all three receivers. The forest settings and contrasts were recorded at `19b3096` after the earlier tasks had been studied. All new nominations, forest guard probabilities, outer probabilities and masked panel metrics were sealed at `07a4431` before scoring the forest outcomes. [Design](../../configs/context_review/forest_cross.json) · [Prediction lock](../../thesis-paper/evidence/forest_cross/prediction-lock.json)

The vote takes the minimum across restorations of the number of approving panels. The continuous margin takes the minimum across the same restorations of the third-largest normalized margin among the four panels. The two rules give identical accept/reject decisions at their corresponding thresholds on all 216 candidates for each masking learner. They can rank proposals differently. On the forest's first panel, 274 of 302 within-fold candidate pairs tied on vote count. The margin separated 265 of those ties, and reversed none of the strict count orderings. On the wider panel it separated 274 of 278 count ties among 370 pairs and reversed 13 strict count orderings. This shows a real loss of numerical distinctions on both cohorts. Many distinct numerical scores could still produce an equally good or worse nominee. [Fold counts](../../thesis-paper/evidence/forest_cross/score-collisions.csv)

Entries give task-weighted percent of anchor columns saved within outer tolerance after one guarded nomination. The guard compares predictions against the receiver's own anchor. On the ten tasks with forest masking and forest refits the continuous-minus-ordinal difference was +3.44 percentage points, with a 95% paired whole-task bootstrap interval of [−4.67, 15.00]. Dry Bean gained 50.00 points within its three folds, while Steel lost 15.56; the remaining eight tasks contributed zero difference. The mean breach rate rose from 3.33% to 10.00%. On the six wider tasks the gain was +5.49 points [0.00, 11.05], from Ozone and Semeion, with unchanged 16.67% mean breach rate. An unrestricted uniform nominee had mean guarded utility of 4.79% and 8.21% on the two panels. The forest full margin exceeded this lottery by 1.54 points [−2.67, 6.54] and 5.56 points [−3.05, 13.83], respectively. For the primary forest/forest pairing, candidate-level concordance averaged informative folds within source, then weighted contributing sources equally. Among first-panel candidates with mixed outer outcomes, the margin scored .678 and the integer vote .543 (10 folds, 5 tasks, 113 passing/failing pairs); the difference was .134 [−.061, .319]. On the wider panel they scored .728 and .543 (11 folds, 5 tasks, 128 pairs), difference .185 [.086, .284]. When the candidate had to pass the guard and the outer test, the wider comparison became .678 versus .475 (8 folds, 5 tasks, 94 pairs). The original Gain ranking scored .476 and .449 for the two outer-pass comparisons. For wide joint passage it reached .599. These scores concern mixed slates, while delivered-utility averages include all planned folds. Each interval is descriptive and conditions on the sampled tasks, fitted models and fixed candidate draws. [Concordance results](../../thesis-paper/evidence/forest_cross/rank-means.csv) · [Source variation](../../thesis-paper/evidence/forest_cross/rank-tasks.csv)

In the forest/forest pairing the single empty-context margin achieved the full margin's guarded utility on each of 46 folds. On the first panel the point and full rules breached on different folds despite the same overall breach rate. Point, ordinal and full nomination required 126, 189 and 760 unique masked predictions on the ten tasks, and 125, 366 and 1,334 on the wider tasks. Their measured aggregate nomination times were 0.47, 0.66 and 2.75 seconds in the first panel, versus 0.47, 1.40 and 5.27 seconds in the second. These clocks exclude fitting the common masker, original Gain screening and receiver fits. The complete diagnostic run fitted 46 masking forests and 524 support-specific receiving forests in roughly 114 seconds. [Costs by fold](../../thesis-paper/evidence/forest_cross/costs.csv)

The experiment changes the mask-scoring model and the reduced-model learner while retaining the original candidate generator. One seed determines the forest in each fold. The comparisons cover the original median-removal mechanism and the stated half-width candidate pools. Neither changing the learner nor refitting all candidates estimates performance under other generators or independently drawn forests.

The standalone evidence replay uses the sealed probabilities and transcripts. From the thesis checkout run `./thesis-python.ps1 -m experiments.context_review.forest_replay`. The input-based audit additionally needs the prepared source matrices and runs with `./thesis-python.ps1 -m experiments.context_review.forest_audit`. It independently fits each forest on its own imputed reduced matrix, checks the saved probabilities, and compares weighted sklearn metrics after reconstructing every distinct forest mask.
