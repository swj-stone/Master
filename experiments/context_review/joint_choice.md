# Paired candidate decisions under one reassignment of outer rows

The historical archive contains 1,283 unordered pairs of distinct, equally wide supports on the same fold. Given a common reassignment, each candidate and its anchor are scored against the same outer labels. A candidate passes if its AUC and accuracy reach 97% of that anchor's values, with the historical slack of (10^{-12}). The payoff is saved anchor width multiplied by passage. We condition on both candidates being delivered because these 704 proposals lack a common, separately recorded deployment guard. For an application with a known guard, a rejected candidate's saving is zero in every world.

For each pair we consider the original information available when choosing the candidate, then a diagnostic observer who also sees both candidates' empty-restoration outputs and their common anchor's empty-restoration output at each outer position. A single permutation preserves all observed outputs. With neither outer query, all four joint passage patterns are attainable for every pair. With the shared point queries the sharp gap is fixed on 1,220 pairs, varies between zero and one strict direction on 57, and allows both strict directions on six. Of the 1,220 fixed gaps, 926 are ties and 294 have a strict winner throughout their admissible worlds. These are overlapping pairs of stored supports. They give no estimate of the likelihood of a win on a new sample.

The [pair records](../../thesis-paper/evidence/joint_choice/pairs.csv) identify the dataset, fold, zero-based proposal indices, retained width, anchor width, attained patterns, and utility-gap bounds. Each `world_xy` indexes one complete permutation in [witnesses.zip](../../thesis-paper/evidence/joint_choice/witnesses.zip) for that fold. A value of −1 indicates an unattainable pattern. The first bit belongs to candidate `a`.

The [attribution table](../../thesis-paper/evidence/joint_choice/attribution.csv) traces every pair through three sets. First take the Cartesian product of the two candidates' individual states under their own point queries. Then give both candidates the union of their point queries and take the Cartesian product of their attainable marginal states under that observation. Finally require one assignment for both candidates. Projecting each of these nested sets onto the equal-width utility difference separates changes due to revealing another query from changes due to the common assignment.

Of the 1,220 fixed gaps, 1,218 already follow from two individually invariant decisions. The extra queries narrow the state and gap sets for three of the remaining 65 pairs. Two become singletons, (4,6) and (6,8) on SPECTF fold 2. In the third pair, (3,14), the second candidate becomes fixed while the first still varies. After giving both candidates all the point queries, nine of the 1,283 pairs still have both marginal bits varying. Enforcing a single common pairing excludes a state on five of these nine pairs. In (4,7) and (4,9) this removes a simultaneous failure, with the three utility differences still attainable. In (4,8), (7,8), and (8,9) it eliminates one strict ordering. None of the five becomes a singleton because of coupling alone. All eight records with a changed joint set across these steps come from SPECTF fold 2.

The search evaluates gap numerators exactly as integers. It expresses binary or multiclass AUC using twice the tied midranks, preserving the integer class counts and pooled score distributions. The reference and candidate move together. For development-only observations, candidate-specific archived assignments, small assignment subproblems and a linear relaxation supply proposed common permutations. The final witness check requires class-count preservation, exact integer passage of both candidates, and agreement with independent scoring of the archived probabilities. The relaxation contributes no exclusion certificates.

A candidate whose decision was certified invariant with its own point queries remains invariant when the other candidate's queries are also supplied. Where those individual certificates do not settle the pair, we enumerate label allocations within joint output classes. The search accumulates four integer gaps. Coordinatewise remaining bounds may rule out an outcome or establish invariant decisions, and are never used on their own to witness a pair of passes. The independent check directly scores the permutations with scikit-learn. It exhausts every relevant assignment for each gap not already settled by the witnessed states and the marginal certificates.

For the matched example we selected the first pair in dataset, fold and proposal index order where both passage bits varied but only one strict ordering was attainable. We combined it with the first pair sharing its first candidate, fold and width that permitted both strict orderings, then enumerated the observation class for all three simultaneously. In SPECTF fold 2, proposal indices 4, 7 and 8 share three columns. For their joint observer the three mixed-label code groups permit 80 label allocations. Applying the same procedure to the entire nine-candidate width-five slate leaves eight label allocations. All allocations in that larger observation scheme have candidate 4 passing and candidate 8 failing.
