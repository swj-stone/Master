# Does a minimum vote add value to a Gain shortlist?

The original nomination pool contains the prefix of the $w$ highest-ranked features of the anchor, plus up to seven other $w$-column supports. Gain ranks the individual features by their mean training split gain, breaking feature ties by index. The deployed Gain policy selects the prefix. Ranking the candidate sets by the sum of their retained feature ranks has the same unique winner. Indeed, for sorted one-based ranks $r_1<\cdots<r_w$, each $r_i\ge i$, with equality in the sum only if $r_i=i$ for every $i$. Thus a random or continuous secondary ordering always leaves the rank-prefix nominee unchanged.

We independently reconstructed all 46 fitted XGBoost models from the fitting rows and confirmed the complete ordering in each archived receipt. We also summed each candidate's actual retained mean split gains before inspecting the receiver outcomes for this comparison. On all 35 folds with a choice, the raw gain sums had a single maximum on the recorded slate. On eleven singleton-anchor folds there was no deletion candidate. These checks distinguish genuine ties in the model score from ties introduced by deliberately grouping candidates.

For a graded comparison we use the best half of the candidate **sets** under the original Gain order. On a slate of $m>0$ sets, the first $\lceil m/2\rceil$ remain. Fractions one quarter, three quarters and one are sensitivity controls. Pool widths were fixed before calculating these contrasts. Inside the restricted pool choose uniformly, choose by the point margin, restrict uniform choice to candidates tied for the largest minimum vote, or maximize point margin within that same top-vote group. The half-width always refers to retained columns. The shortlist fraction refers to candidate sets. Each deployment receives one candidate guard on each actionable fold and reverts to the corresponding learner's anchor after rejection. Expected returns under lotteries average those actual guarded outcomes. The earlier candidate-specific refits were already known when this secondary comparison was formulated. [Protocol](../../configs/context_review/gain_increment.json)

The timing replay reconstructs the original one-thread voter on the fitting sample and measures nomination after the common anchor and slate are available. Each policy starts with an empty cache. Point and vote use the same median replacement and four-panel bank as the original methods, with only the original panel read for point. To sample a uniform vote tie exactly, a scan can skip the rest of a candidate's contexts only when the running count falls strictly below the best known count, or reaches its absolute lower bound of zero. To use point as the tie-breaker, visit candidates in descending point order and prune any that cannot strictly improve the best count. The counted units are distinct masked feature-set predictions, including masked reference sets. A singleton shortlist needs none. Reported wall times exclude the common model fit, construction of the anchor and the guard.

For a uniform draw among a set of candidates, each possible nominee contributes its recorded guarded return with equal weight.

Adding the vote before point changed one nomination in the ten-task panel and none on the wider panel. Both learners gained 1.33 percentage points of effective compression on the first panel (paired 95% percentile interval [0.00, 4.00]), attributable to Seeds. The XGBoost breach rate stayed unchanged. Logistic breaches fell by 3.33 points [−10.00, 0.00]. Identical nominations on the wider panel fix both differences at zero on these splits.

Adding the vote before uniform choice changed the nomination distribution on four folds of each panel. Both receivers gained 0.67 points [0.00, 2.00] on the ten tasks. On the wider panel the XGBoost gain of 2.73 points [0.00, 8.19] came from Ozone. For logistic refits the Ozone gain was partly offset by SECOM, yielding +1.36 points [−2.08, 6.15]. The corresponding breach differences were −0.83 [−2.50, 0.00] on the ten tasks for both receivers, −1.39 [−4.17, 0.00] for wide XGBoost and −6.94 [−15.28, 0.00] for wide logistic. Each interval uses 30,000 whole-task paired resamples, conditional on the fixed data and guarded outcomes. With one contributing source, many resamples omit every gain. When the Gain group was reduced to the best quarter, adding the vote before point left average effective compression unchanged on all four comparisons. Expanding it to three quarters or the full slate produced +2.61 points only on wider logistic refits. On the full slate with uniform tie-breaking, the vote changed effective compression by +0.67 and +0.33 points for the ten-task tree and logistic refits and by +2.42 and +3.32 on their wider counterparts. These sensitivity analyses reuse the same outcomes. [All shortlist widths](../../thesis-paper/evidence/gain_increment/means.csv)

On the ten-task panel the half-slate point rule used 68 distinct masked predictions (0.061 s total across folds), and vote plus point used 96 (0.074 s). On the wider panel the counts were 71 (0.093 s) and 168 (0.286 s). Uniform choice used zero predictions, while vote plus uniform used 96 and 170 (0.080 s and 0.302 s). Independent caches were used in a single timing pass on one machine. These measurements concern nomination only. Each candidate chosen on an actionable fold pays the same one-candidate guard budget.
