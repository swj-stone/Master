# Changing the anchor, candidate neighborhood and receiving model

The original transport experiment uses a training-derived tree Gain anchor, proposes subsets of half its width and measures deletions using a held-out guard. The alternate pipeline uses univariate ANOVA F scores on fitting rows, keeps the top half of the original columns and drops one quarter of this anchor, rounding the number dropped down with a minimum of one. Its candidate pool contains the F-ranked prefix and as many as seven distinct one-column exchanges around that prefix. The masking predictor, guard learner and final learner all use 100-tree random forests. All candidates share their fold's width. The data sources, split assignments, training-stage imputation, bootstrap weights, metric tolerances and tail-restoration construction follow the original panels. These design changes jointly alter the decision problem. [Fixed protocol](../../configs/context_review/filter_neighborhood.json)

The method sampled 138 candidate subsets on the ten-task panel and 128 on the six wider tasks. Both panels retained every split and every candidate. The narrower panel's anchors ranged from 2 to 21 columns and the wider panel's anchors from 36 to 428. The designs were committed at `e395a58` and the nominees and predictions at `62af88b` before scoring the new slates. The same previously studied data sources contribute to both pipeline comparisons.
