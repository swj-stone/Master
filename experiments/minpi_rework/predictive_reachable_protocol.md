# Secondary post-training reachable inputs in frozen forests

This secondary study follows inspection of structural widths on the 20 wide RF jobs. Their outer scores are still sealed. The earlier seven-source study's outcomes have already been released, so its 35 jobs are a retrospective replay for this question. No new source family is introduced. The target is a fixed forest under first-unavailable-split truncation, using its entire stored structure including branches absent from calibration rows. Compute C(S) as the union of the required feature sets H along every structural root-to-split prefix for which H is contained in S. It describes features whose tests can actually be executed. Constant-response or redundant split computations may exist, so it need not give the smallest functionally equivalent forest.

For all 20 wide jobs use the registered MinPI and MDI ranking paths. For all 35 earlier jobs also use pooled, intact and static-ablation ranking paths. Compare each original selected support and its canonical form at the original all-size and clean acceptance guard. Independently recompute all candidate widths on the entire path, including zero and p, and minimize canonical width over all feasible depths. Within one path break ties by the calibration worst-size excess then the deepest feasible depth. Across paths compare MDI alone, MinPI alone and the MDI-plus-partner portfolios (including pooled, intact and static where available). The published nominal winner and the new canonical winner each retain their own tie rules. For canonical portfolios break equal widths by the smaller all-size calibration risk then by the MDI path. Report changes from both the original portfolio and its canonicalized support. In the wide panel separately identify changes due solely to tie policy. Treat MDI and every partner symmetrically.

Build all 55 forecast receipts and hash predicted arrays and structural supports before opening new wide outcomes. Verify raw arrays for S versus C(S) on the acceptance sample and outer feature rows, and compare availability risk for every coalition size on acceptance rows. Record actual numerical residuals, chosen method, path depth, physical and nominal widths, clean prediction scores and paired effects per source with source-level uncertainty. Report limitations, widths of the full teacher and null examples. Structural closure brings no guarantee that a newly refitted forest will return the same model; keep refit questions separate from fixed-forest equivalence.
