# Independent referee report for the exact triplet minPI study

This report identifies earlier manuscripts by their recorded digests. The current manuscript is covered by the [revision review](revision-review.md).

## Pre-draft review, 16 November 2026

**Question under review.** Given a fixed collection of labelled training triplets, can a dense feature screen integrate the majority-game marginal exactly at the three specified peer counts, and does the resulting ranking justify its cost at a fixed retention rate? The mathematical calculation is independently checkable. Claims about feature recovery, downstream accuracy and elapsed time need separate observations.

**Formal checks.** I read the full source of `paper/minpi/formal/Pivotal.lean` and compiled it with Lean 4.35.0. Indexed peer subsets are enumerated by include/exclude recursion, with multiplicity retained when distinct subsets yield identical profiles. I verified the statements for profile multiplicity, cardinality normalization, and positive, negative and abstention boundary increments. The compiler reported only the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The formalization covers these five combinatorial ingredients. It does not cover the floating-point implementation, witness sampling, or prediction after refitting. The Python summation correctly excludes the focal sign and sums only boundary-margin profiles; calling it exact refers to deterministic integration of the finite coalition expectation, up to floating-point error.

**Evidence for the contribution.** For a local selection problem, the closest controls are F, a raw hit/miss update, and the same-witness signed vote and size-mean ablations. Marginals computed from the majority game are distinct from the binary retraining quota in Shi and Zhao, and from the conditional-importance minimum in MinShap. Use these distinctions when naming the method. A general interaction-discovery, lossless-screening or redundancy-certificate claim has no proof here. [Original MinPI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33885) · [MinShap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15107)

**Runtime accounting.** `witness_margins` constructs near hits and misses via a query-by-reference distance matrix with an inner dimension equal to the feature count. After the witness matrix is available, a three-dimensional count need not be computed for every column. Give separate dependence on query and reference counts, witness count, number of tiers, maximum cardinality and final ordering. A fixed retention rate sets the compression mechanically. An equal-width baseline can differ in quality and time, whereas it cannot differ in compression. For the historical compact filter, state whether timing includes a discarded sizing or reranking phase; avoid using such overhead as evidence for the pivotal formula.

**External prediction replay.** I recomputed AUC and balanced accuracy separately for all 36 stored prediction vectors and checked the 36 sets of column indices for their cardinality, valid range and uniqueness. Both `external-official` tables agree with their traces. On Arcene, equal-width random selection and the proposed score both average roughly 0.91 AUC. On Dexter the F ranking exceeds the proposed score by 0.019 AUC; both comparisons belong in the paper. The interval for the paired AUC difference conditions on one official validation set per dataset and the three fixed fits. A seed count of three does not increase the number of external test samples or independent external splits. [Original challenge](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2004/hash/5e751896e527c862bf67251a474b3819-Abstract.html)

**Revision requests for the full paper.** Supply matched score and elapsed-time differences for the two predictive backends. Report how often changing the minimum to the size-average or singleton rule actually changes the selected support, and whether the predictive consequence is measurable. Clearly state when a post hoc diagnostic uses already disclosed evaluation results. Check runtime claims against the simple F comparator as well as expensive embedded selectors or Monte Carlo. Present at least one failure case in the results section. The paper and the formal main-split results were still under development at this review.

## Second review of the journal manuscript

I read the 29-page PDF (SHA-256 `9c05ae501843e8bcb831c2ca354724248fe38dc04477aa0ee446e52fe545b331`) from the abstract through its references, with the complete TeX sources alongside it. I inspected all pages in rendered contact sheets and examined Figures 1–3 at higher resolution. This review applies to that snapshot. The later four-arm study and measured certificate timings were still in progress.

**Independent mathematics and formalization.** For a fixed focal sign, a single coalition produces an increment in an interval of length one. This justifies the factor 2 in the Hoeffding exponent at a fixed feature and tier; shared draws across features and triplets are allowed. In the replacement coupling, the conditional event of distinct peers excluding the focal index has probability equal to the stated product. Its complement bounds the difference of conditional and unconditional means because the focal sign is fixed. With no abstentions, only a tie is pivotal at even size and its binomial probability yields the displayed kernel. The scoring, averaging and minimum operations occur in the stated order. The finite-population inequality is uniform in votes, and the kernel interpretation requires the stated no-abstention assumption. Neither theorem supplies a predictive guarantee. For the prefix proof a strict inequality prunes any omitted upper bound, so all ties at the threshold remain eligible. I independently ran Lean 4.35 on all six declarations including the generic prefix result. No custom axioms were reported.

**Original concerns resolved in this revision.** Dense-neighbour complexity and subsequent scoring and sorting are now separate. The manuscript explains why the prescribed width itself gives all methods the same reduction. Matched controls measure the effect of signing, averaging, and using only the largest tier, with raw Jaccard similarities. All four strong screened/unscreened tree outcomes, the Madelon AUC failure, the two regression comparisons, the Arcene random control and Dexter F baseline are reported. Recalculation from split tables corroborated the 0.39–0.45 and 0.64–0.72 paired time ratios, the five passes per source and learner, and the -0.1912 Madelon AUC difference. Largest-tier agreement is 50/55, comprising every classification split (45/45), 4/5 Diabetes splits, and 1/5 Communities splits. On the fixed synthetic vote matrices I recovered the maximum observed replacement score errors of 0.005157 at 64 and 0.00003108 at 16,384 columns, and 0.7531 and 0.9655 top-support recovery for four- and 256-draw sampling at the greatest width. The certificate replay contains 55 distinct source/split rows, each with a successful support match. These are count audits and conditional results, not new independent evaluations.

**Issues to close before the final review.**

1. The abstract calls the eight benchmark sources real. Madelon is an artificial challenge with a known generation mechanism. Distinguish existing benchmarks (including Madelon) from the three simulations generated for the present study, throughout the paper.
2. Distinguish the ordered-prefix mathematical statement from the Python output and its replay. The implementation returns input-order indices of the selected set, including the shortcut at full width. The replay checks this support; it cannot demonstrate recovery of all score ranks or ordering of the head by score.
3. Compare the exact screen with F and uniform screens **before the same forest**. Savings against the full-width forest can follow from providing fewer features to its split search. Use the results of the four-arm study, including its two prospective sources, in the headline interpretation. Preserve both favourable and adverse findings and the different timing runs. If a cheaper screen matches the result, retain the conditional arithmetic/diagnostic contribution and narrow the practical rationale.
4. Position exact counting against classical vote-power computation as well as feature selection. Fixed-weight and cardinality swing counts predate this paper. Bilbao et al. use generating functions in conventional weighted voting games; here the signed ternary triplet payoff, the pooling over witness sign classes, and the specified-cardinality objective are the pieces to distinguish. The two related 2026 methods are already attributed to their respective authors. [Bilbao et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02628555)
5. Put the relevant timing hardware and software versions and an accurate data/code availability statement in the manuscript. Identify the processed SHAPG Communities and Crime source and how to recover the archives for other externally sourced benchmarks. Complete bibliographic metadata for any later external sources. Never imply that unpublished local material is publicly deposited.
6. The size-mean and raw-vote support overlaps answer whether rankings change; when claiming predictive effects of the minimum, explicitly report the observed predictive changes or their absence. For the subject-held-out follow-up give a subject-level dispersion summary at the 10% landmark, available from saved predictions. One average over nine subjects does not show whether the gain is distributed across subjects.
7. Figure 1's middle/right panels show only the reference tick at 1, even though exact ratios matter. Add in-range numeric ticks. Explain the aggregation of six matrices per width in the integration curves. I found no cropped equations or figures in this version.

**Editorial assessment.** This revision supports a well-specified computational methods contribution and a mixed empirical result. Its counting theorem, approximation analysis, empirical ablations and stress tests can be appraised individually. The clinical, regression, synthetic and cross-subject results provide no reason to advertise the smallest-layer aggregation as a generally better predictive selector. A specialist journal could consider the scoped algorithmic result; the international top-journal priority question turns on what surplus scientific or practical benefit survives the equally cheap front-end and certified-engine controls. Polished exposition and correct formalization do not answer that question. A venue-specific submission decision awaits a named track and author finalization.

**Operating-point denominator clarification.** The 5/5 ISOLET and Gisette comparisons above are against direct ET at the same output width. The original laboratory audit additionally demands acceptable quality against the uncompressed full-column learner. With this extra requirement ISOLET passes 0/5 with each learner, whereas Gisette passes 5/5 with each learner. Report both denominators; keep both interpretations separate in the abstract, table and work-point summary.

## Final independent disposition, 16 November 2026

**Reviewed revision.** The release-candidate manuscript is `paper/minpi/main.pdf`, 34 pages, SHA-256 `b07035f514bf129380306ca0008e09ce8fdf0c52e7b902da0a067718ef825320`. I read the final abstract, theory, methods, results, discussion, data and code statement, AI disclosure and references against the TeX and local records. All 34 final pages were rendered and checked for page overflow and lost content. The final compiler log contains no overfull/underfull messages, undefined citations, or unresolved references. I checked the wording of the corrected SECOM outcome in the final PDF. The label records a manufacturing pass/fail decision.

**Closure of prior findings.** The paper now distinguishes Madelon as an artificial benchmark, cites prior weighted-voting computations and threshold retrieval, gives the input-order contract of the public support API, identifies the time scopes and reference implementations, and distinguishes the named-selector quality threshold from the additional full-column threshold. The local artifact statement accurately describes the supplied implementation and evidence without promising public deposition. HAR includes subject-level heterogeneity. The 57-feature comparison is positive on eight of nine people for both learners, with participant 10 losing 0.215 balanced accuracy on ridge and 0.070 on five-neighbour classification. Figures 1 and 2 resolve the earlier scale and aggregation questions. Spambase duplicate-held-out sensitivity, its reversal for ridge, and the repeated CNAE representations are explicit. No manuscript revisions remain open from the independent rounds.

**Computational claims.** I freshly recalculated fixed-matrix times from the paired rows of `certificate/timings.csv`. At 16,384 columns the medians were 44.730 ms for the original implementation, 16.926 ms for the newer exhaustive engine, and 11.874 ms for the same engine with the certificate. The median *paired* certificate/full ratio is 0.71942. At 64 columns it is 1.01595. With training and neighbour construction included, the ratios are 0.94059 and 0.96611 on Gisette and ISOLET versus the newer exhaustive engine. Versus the older implementation they are 0.78044 and 0.79021. None of those latter four timings includes a fitted downstream predictor. Across 55 training splits, the exact support replay matched all 55. The initial shortlist sufficed 48 times, and the median share of layer–column evaluations was 0.40104. The full-score formulation, upper bound, strict pruning criterion and stable tie handling agree with the mathematical proof. I recompiled the six Lean results, which use standard axioms only. The floating-point and real-data checks remain empirical corroboration.

**Predictive claims.** I recalculated the four-arm comparisons from the 400 result rows. On both Gisette and ISOLET, all three 40% screens achieved the named-comparator time and quality criterion against direct ET on five splits with both learners. In the fresh Gisette fits, the pivotal/F time ratios were 0.9373 and 0.9531, while pivotal/random ratios were 1.0590 and 1.0096. The other paired comparisons in the abstract use the main experiment's separate timing run. The additional quality requirement versus the full-width learner produces 5/5 on Gisette and 0/5 on ISOLET. The paper makes this distinction. In CNAE-9 the F pipeline exceeds pivotal by about four accuracy points with either learner. In the SECOM temporal cohort the tree AUC/AP comparison favours pivotal against F, but its balanced accuracy is 0.4992 and only 24 of 392 evaluation records are failures. The ridge AUC comparison favours F. These observations, the matched random arm, the two regression results, and the frequent support identity with the largest tier justify the revised scope.

**Last revision check.** I separately rendered and read pages 14, 25 and 29 after the final revision and recomputed its two additional comparisons. On fixed training witnesses the certified minimum cost 1.11672 times the largest layer alone on Gisette and 1.19429 times on ISOLET, and all ten support pairs agreed. Across all ten datasets and both learners, 0/100 seed pairs met the 20% speed and quality criteria against the same-width F screen followed by ET. The PDF identifies Table 3 unambiguously as the two-threshold table. These statements clarify the practical value of the certificate relative to the simpler ranking and screen.

**Scientific disposition.** I would support external peer review of this release as a specialist machine-learning methods paper on exact scoring and support certification for a specified surrogate game. The mathematical result can be evaluated independently, the runtime improvement is isolated from engine refactoring, and the controls explain a practical speed advantage that would otherwise be misattributed to voting. An across-task predictive advantage of taking a minimum has not been demonstrated. The theorem applies conditionally on the witnesses; selection consistency, recovery or inference about the underlying predictive variables is outside the result. These are scientific limitations, not outstanding editorial repairs. They make priority at a leading general machine-learning journal uncertain. The report makes no acceptance prediction or venue-specific compliance determination. The author still decides authorship, venue and submission; the research material reviewed here is local.
