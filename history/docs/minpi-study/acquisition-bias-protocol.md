# Sensitivity to acquisition bias in the calibration experiment

The initial polarity experiment revealed that sign voting recovered stable channels more often than the complete minimum when the background match was assigned uniformly. This final simulation tests whether that result transfers to an acquisition process that matches same-class references on nuisance conditions more frequently. This protocol was fixed after observing the calibration experiment and before any biased-acquisition results. All four settings will be reported. The grid will remain fixed.

Use the previous instrument population, sample sizes, instruments, tier sizes, budget, learner, prediction distribution and score definitions. Use 48 new independent worlds, with SeedSequence entropy (20261121, world index). Hold each world's loadings, labels, instruments, Gaussian errors and queried rows fixed across four acquisition policies (common random numbers). Only in the cross-instrument triplets, set the probability that the designated hit rather than the miss matches the query's background to 1/2, 2/3, 5/6 or 1. This is a training and assessment comparison-acquisition policy. The downstream test population is identical across policies and its background is independent of class. No coordinate has prior membership in a selector's support.

The primary policy is 5/6; 1/2 checks replication and 2/3 and 1 describe sensitivity. Evaluate whether minimum exceeds each of raw signs and size 16 on (i) independent mean coordinatewise weakest-layer score and (ii) worst-instrument test AUC by more than 0.01 for AUC and by more than zero for surrogate. Use whole-world paired means and two-sided 98.75% percentile intervals (10,000 bootstrap replicates, seed 20261122), Bonferroni for four primary contrasts. This follow-up provides evidence about robustness to the specified acquisition process. It cannot estimate the frequency of these conditions in other applications. Archive predictions, support identities, triplet matrices and scores, including the uniform and extreme cases.
