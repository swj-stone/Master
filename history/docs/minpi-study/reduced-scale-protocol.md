# Measuring common-denominator reduction on observed row pivots

The comparison uses the existing direct selector and one alternative for the identical fixed-ballot, index-stable weakest-layer target. Exact probability numerators are evaluated at every row composition and sign that actually occurs. At layer $k$, let $g_k$ be the greatest common divisor of its binomial denominator $D_k$ and the observed signed numerators. Absent signs contribute zero. The least common multiple $D'$ of $D_k/g_k$ is the smallest positive integer scale for all individual observed row pivots. The bound $wD'$ on partial column sums is sufficient. A further reduction relying on cancellation between rows, tighter bounds or the selection boundary is outside this comparison.

The alternative uses the existing native selector if its raw common bound fits. Otherwise it probes the observed numerators with Python integers, in decreasing peer count and lexicographic row-composition order. As more fractions are seen the required common scale can only grow by divisibility. Stop the probe immediately if a prefix already requires more than `(2**63-1)//w`. On failure call the existing certified dyadic selector, charging both the failed probe and the complete fresh dyadic call, including any recomputed pivots. On success reuse the probe's weights, convert scaled integers to signed 64-bit arrays, and use the largest-layer upper bound, inclusive cutoff and original-column tie rule of the direct selector. Count int64 accumulations and distinguish the dyadic algorithm's arbitrary-precision fallback columns. No precomputed row summaries are handed to either timed method.
