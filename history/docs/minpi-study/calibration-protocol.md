# Follow-up on reference-instrument polarity

This protocol was fixed after observing the two reversal regimes in the matched-triplet experiment, before generating any instrument data. The initial experiment makes every population size-zero vote mean zero by symmetry. Here persistent differences between channels can make a weak size-zero layer informative about the ability to transport a feature.

Generate 48 independent new worlds. Master entropy is (20261119, world index). Fix the primary fraction at 10% (6 of 60), coalition sizes at 0, 4 and 16, and retain the same three selectors, paired analysis and independent sample counts as in the first experiment. Select six stable class channels (loadings independent uniform 0.55–0.85), six polarity-reversing class channels (1.30–1.70), and 48 background channels (1.50–2.50). Permute the physical column order in each world. All measurements have independent standard Gaussian errors. Both class-labelled channel groups carry class information within an instrument; only the first six have a common orientation across instruments.

In each acquired triplet, the class of the designated hit always matches the query and the miss always has the opposite class. A query always uses instrument A. With probability 1/3 the two references also use A, and all three observations share a common random background sign. Otherwise the references use instrument B, which reverses the polarity of the six high-gain channels. One B reference matches the query background and the other takes the opposite sign; a fair coin assigns the match. All 48 background channels respond to the shared background sign. This setup represents aligned nuisance channels in cross-instrument comparisons; no scores are used to construct it.

Draw 192 training and 768 evaluation triplets independently, then generate 384 independent fitting and 1,536 test observations. In the two prediction samples class and background are independent fair signs, and instrument B has probability 2/3 independently of both. Neither model receives the instrument identity. Train the same standardized logistic learner on each support. Record out-of-sample mean coordinatewise weakest-layer score, counts of stable and reversing channels, pooled AUC and log loss, AUC separately by test instrument and their minimum. The learner is shared between instruments.

Predict that the complete minimum will select more stable channels than size 16, that its mean assessment weakest-layer score will increase, and that its mean worst-instrument AUC will improve by over 0.01. The two primary contrasts are minimum minus size 16 on the latter two outcome measures (assessment score and worst-instrument AUC). Use paired whole-world bootstraps (10,000 resamples, seed 20261120) and two-sided 97.5% percentile intervals for these two contrasts; declare joint support only if both lower bounds exceed their respective thresholds (0 and 0.01). Report the same two contrasts against raw sign votes with descriptive 95% intervals even if simple signs perform as well or better. Compare all methods on homogeneous cohorts only as a known boundary from the first experiment. Treat independent worlds, not comparisons or downstream observations, as replicates; report any failure. Support identities, votes, per-instrument outcomes and scores are archived.
