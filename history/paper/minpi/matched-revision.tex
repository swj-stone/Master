\subsection{Consequences of changing a selected feature}
\label{sec:matched}
We compared six rankings at the same budget and with the same test rows: reference-fold geometry and five scores computed from identical triplets (raw distance margin, signed vote $G_0$, largest layer, layer mean and minimum). Five additional Spambase splits kept identical feature vectors together.

The minimum changed the largest-layer support on four Crime splits and one diabetes split (Table~\ref{tab:matched-reg}). Tree MAE increased on all five and tied on the five unchanged supports. Its sole $R^2$ improvement (Crime/1129) coincided with a 5.82 increase in MAE. Ridge $R^2$ rose on two splits. On diabetes the largest layer favoured age over serum measure \texttt{s3} by 0.0352. Minimization penalized age by an extra 0.0625, reversing the order. Median ridge $R^2$ for geometry and the minimum was 0.481 and 0.291 on Crime, and 0.324 and 0.021 on diabetes.

On breast cancer, substituting the largest layer for $G_0$ changed tree AUC by a median $+0.005$ (range $-0.039$ to $+0.014$). Geometry beat the minimum on all five splits. On grouped Spambase, replacing raw margins with signs raised tree AUC on every split (median $+0.064$). Pivotal weighting then changed it by a median $-0.003$ (range $-0.008$ to $+0.008$). The minimum and largest layer agreed throughout both comparisons.

With five witness seeds per training split, the minimum's mean within-split Jaccard overlap was 0.208 on Gisette and 0.194 on ISOLET. Varying the training split at a common seed gave 0.203 and 0.185 (50 pairs for each comparison per source). A common seed indexes different observations when the training rows change.
