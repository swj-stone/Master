\subsection{Transfer to later gas batches and new material compositions}
For UCI Gas Sensor Array Drift~\citep{vergara2012gas}, we trained on batches 1--7 and evaluated batches 8--10 separately. For UCI Superconductivity~\citep{hamidieh2018}, rows sharing all 81 descriptors or normalized elemental composition formed groups under transitive closure. The held-out quarter comprised 5,368 rows in 3,791 groups. The primary seed, ridge learner, 10\% budget and quality margins (0.01 balanced accuracy and 0.02 $R^2$) were fixed before this analysis.

At the primary seed, $G_{16}$ and the minimum selected identical supports on both tasks (Table~\ref{tab:newcohorts}). The additional $G_4$ and layer-mean controls also agreed with each other, giving 0.351 balanced accuracy on gas and 0.579 $R^2$ on materials.

Relative to $G_0$, pivotal weighting changed gas balanced accuracy by $+0.043,+0.025,-0.003$ across the three batches. The average exceeded the prespecified margin, but $F$ performed better on every batch. On materials, weighting reduced $R^2$ by 0.160 (paired 95\% interval $[-0.185,-0.136]$ from resampling held-out groups with fitted predictions fixed). At two further seeds on the same rows, the minimum changed one materials support. Swapping three columns raised $R^2$ by 0.060 while lowering the mean weakest-layer score by 0.0011 on comparisons built entirely from held-out rows.
