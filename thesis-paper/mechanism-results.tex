\paragraph{Constructed and measured patterns.}
A synthetic regression with one useful and one spurious fitted interaction gave a positive control. We exhaustively compared all 32 restorations of five tail variables using population squared error and a 3\% tolerance. Moving the two interactions over the 20 ordered tail positions generated 20 known veto patterns. The nested design found six, the separating design all 20, and 20 equally sized random families found 342 of 400 family--position combinations. The construction establishes the possibility of a hidden failure. Its 20 positions are arrangements of one mechanism.

For the empirical check we used fitted trees from the six development tasks (Abalone, Credit Card Fraud, Gisette, ISOLET, Madelon and Spambase). Among 1,276 distinct split--proposal pairs on the nested search paths, we sampled up to 12 evenly spaced proposals in order of first encounter per split, yielding 274. ISOLET contributed 60 of its 940 available proposals and Madelon all 26 of its proposals. The proposals reflect earlier votes and have unequal sampling fractions. Ranking rows were used during search. A reserved set of validation rows supplied a second set of four panels, called the guard panels.

For the 226 proposals with at least two tail features we enumerated all ordered pairs $(u,v)$ and compared each pair's predicted signs with the observed decisions on every available restoration, separately on each set of panels. Both accepting and vetoing masks occurred for 148 proposals on ranking and 129 on guard. \emph{No ordered pair predicted all decisions for any of the 226 proposals on either sample.} This remained true for the 17 ranking and 19 guard proposals that passed both endpoints yet failed on an interior restoration. The sample contradicts Eq.~\eqref{eq:pattern} for these measured ballots. It leaves open local and higher-order interactions.

Of the 26 ranking-endpoint passes, nine failed both families on guard, one failed just the guard endpoints, five failed just the guard interiors, and eleven failed neither. Among the five additional failures on the same guard rows, the separating and size-matched random ranking tests flagged the same four. Each also flagged two of the other eleven proposals. The nested design flagged one from each group. The five cases came from Fraud (two), Gisette, ISOLET and Spambase. They provide no estimate of the failure rate after retraining.

The 26 proposals had between 12 and 16 novel interior probes apiece. The full set vetoed 14/26 on guard (53.85\%). When each proposal received exactly two distinct interior probes, averaging over all possible pairs gave a veto rate of 35.48\%. The two endpoints vetoed 10/26 (38.46\%). This comparison matches query count while allowing the sizes of the observed masks to differ. Resampling the three bootstrap panels with the original point panel, ranking rows, predictor and endpoint masks fixed rejected 4, 5, 6, 10 and 7 of the 26 originally accepted proposals in five draws. Repeated ballots and repeated probes are both sources of variation.
