# Internal scientific review of context-voted feature deletion

This is an independent review within the project. It records evidence checks and open manuscript questions for the authors.

## First reading of the protocol and implementation

| Question | Finding and action |
|---|---|
| What changes relative to MinPI? | The predecessor assigns indices to individual features in a thresholded refit game. Here the algorithm proposes a deletion, checks a fixed predictor against the *initial anchor* in each of up to three rank-defined contexts, and requires a strict majority of four paired ballots in every distinct context. Credit the earlier negative witness and the established one-element minimality of delta debugging. |
| What does the certificate cover? | Nonempty returned supports satisfy the fixed empirical oracle. At size above one, all individual deletions fail on the returned support. A one-column result reaches the nonempty boundary. The result does not guarantee a minimum-cardinality support, a generalization error, or even feasibility for a refitted model. Adaptive reuse of the same panels precludes a confidence interpretation. |
| Can a timing claim be assigned to the minimum? | Compare the minimum with the exhaustive implementation of the same minimum, with identical search, margins, masks, and seeds. Report selector time, cold end-to-end time, distinct mask calls and support identity. Distinguish comparisons with other selectors and their different workloads. Early rejection within a candidate does not order the total work of min versus pooled votes along diverging paths. |
| What can the ablation establish? | Pooled versus minimum changes how the same contexts and ballots are combined. The one-point variant simultaneously changes panel replication and context coverage. Comparison with the unpruned anchor measures incremental compression. Equal-width leading-feature subsets distinguish the size decision from the choice of columns. |
| What independent evidence is available? | The six main datasets reuse previously examined splits. They establish behavior on a known benchmark. The additional three linear-model tasks use fixed rules and new seeds. The transfer experiment changes both learner and datasets, and its results do not isolate the effect of changing the learner alone. |
| What is an independent observation? | Five seeds repeat data splitting within the same dataset. State each dataset's paired results and variability. Count datasets, not 30 runs, in cross-problem comparisons. A descriptive closeness claim must specify which metric, reference and datasets it covers. |
| Is the formal model aligned with Python? | The Lean source checks strict majority equivalence under early veto, the finite descent of the singleton cleanup, feasibility given an initially feasible support, and a general counterexample to global optimality. The Python block phase preserves feasibility by accepting only after the same frozen ballot. Input ordering uses distinct feature names. Lean does not verify the Python interpreter, refitted predictions, floating-point tolerances, or the data. |

The linear-model replication covers 15 splits, six arms per split. An independent reimplementation reconstructed the partitions, panel weights, coefficients and anchors; evaluated every distinct context with scikit-learn's reference metrics; and checked the frozen selected features against a full-ballot block search and repeated singleton sweeps. All 90 submitted supports and refitted test scores matched to absolute error below (10^{-10}). All 45 min, mean and point outputs satisfied their own empirical predicate and the nonempty single-deletion stopping condition. At the same width, the breast-cancer AUCs were 0.9875 for min and 0.9693 for the coefficient prefix. The corresponding Wine AUCs were 0.9991 and 0.9986; the Diabetes test R² values were 0.4859 and 0.4863. Average effects must retain this heterogeneity.

The end-to-end manuscript and reference-matched study tables were unavailable at this reading. The second review follows compilation and completion of the primary and size-matched comparisons.

## Second reading of the manuscript, proofs and effective runs

**Scientific disposition.** The evidence supports an empirical methods paper on auditable local feature deletion for fitted tabular predictors. I would send this version for author review for a specialist machine-learning or data-mining research meeting. The contribution is a deletion oracle with interpretable vetoes and a measurable saving during its evaluation. For a top general methods track I would request stronger evidence of *why context-wise majority improves the endpoint* before endorsing a broad accuracy or efficiency claim. In the primary benchmark the single-panel rule kept 26.6% of columns versus 29.0% for MinPI, with AUC 0.9322 versus 0.9324 and equal mean accuracy to four decimals. All current evidence can be reported under the narrower claim. This is an internal pre-submission judgment.

| Inspection | Findings |
|---|---|
| Final paper and typeset figure | I inspected the five tables, the paired-effects figure, the two propositions and the rendered anonymous LNCS PDF. The per-task figure uses AP for Fraud, ordinary R² for Abalone and AUC elsewhere; its score differences are only comparable within the task. All 15 cited keys resolve. The revised manuscript explicitly states the point control's compression and performance and restricts proposal scheduling to the observed decision history. |
| Matched controls | The effective data contain 96 primary arms, 90 contemporaneous controls, 270 earlier benchmark arms and nine full-width stress runs. I recalculated 29.006% mean retention, 6.556 s per MinPI arm, 34.542% and 6.057 s for compact Gain. Eighteen of 30 MinPI arms reduced their starting support. Same-width Gain isolates column identity, and the five-block predecessor supplies the comparison with the prior algorithm. The conventional baseline timings come from a separate campaign and depend on the configured budgets. The one-panel control still computes a common set of weighted scores internally, so its time should not be used to claim superiority over an optimized one-panel implementation. |
| Costs and proof | The six paired paths each produce the same final support. They account for 343 proposals and 307 vs 586 distinct masks, with cold totals 40.314 s vs 42.375 s. This is one paired timing per dataset. I recompiled the Lean file with 4.35.0 and inspected the used axioms. The vote/cache identity and finite local stopping claims accord with the deterministic decision schedule and fixed empirical oracle. Application-level numerical predictions are checked separately. |
| Quality trade-off | At matched size, mean ISOLET AUC rose by 0.00077 over the Gain prefix across five positive changes. Relative to its own starting support, MinPI lost 0.0003 AUC there. Fraud lost 0.0193 AP at matched width and 0.0365 AP against the anchor. The refit tolerance diagnostic records five breaches among 30 test splits, conditional on these fitted models. These are incompatible with a claim of guaranteed prediction preservation. |
| Transfer and factorial diagnostic | I independently reevaluated the 30 added linear-model arms with an exhaustive-context search, the reference weighted scikit-learn metrics and a separately implemented terminal sweep. Selected supports, split digests and refitted test scores matched all records within 1e-10. All per-context point votes recovered the full rule's support on 13/15 splits; the empty-context majority recovered it on 12/15. This follow-up was designed after seeing the original transfer data. It quantifies agreement for these fixed splits. |
| Provenance and presentation | All input records named in the evidence manifest matched the listed SHA-256 hashes. The primary analysis replaces two late groups in full; all metrics for an affected dataset--seed come from the selected repeat. The linear extension and full-width runs are clearly distinguished from the six prepared tasks. The anonymous manuscript discloses the assistance used. |

**Remaining scope.** The strongest quality result is specific to the task, endpoint and comparator. The largest original-width run has 5,000 columns but a 33-column Gain anchor; it does not test a 5,000-feature backward search. Feature-set overlap on repeated splits also depends on upstream preprocessing. A stronger general-purpose claim would need a prospective, previously unused high-dimensional task suite, an established tolerance-selection rule and an optimized simpler-point comparison. These are changes to the scope of a future claim. The present 13-page LNCS research manuscript requires the authors to select a venue and check its actual page and authorship rules before submission.

## Addendum on the official ISOLET speaker split

The earlier row-split results motivated this additional comparison. The four rules were frozen before the authors fitted them on the official split. Group 5 is a separate test file from 30 held-out speakers. Its 1,559 recordings constitute one cohort, with repeated utterances per speaker. [UCI ISOLET documentation](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/54/isolet)

I recalculated accuracy and macro one-vs-rest AUC from all four saved probability matrices on the identical test labels. Every value matched both `evidence/speakers.csv` and `audit/isolet-official.csv` to within 1e-12. MinPI retained 96 of the independent Gain selector's 158 columns (39.24% fewer), with a difference of −0.00002246 AUC and two more correct predictions. Relative to the equal-width 96-column Gain prefix, MinPI alone classified 43 recordings correctly and the prefix alone classified 24 correctly, a net gain of 19/1,559 (1.22 percentage points). The full 617-column model correctly classified 14 more recordings than MinPI. The held-out comparison strengthens the ISOLET finding. The specialist empirical-paper recommendation and the limits on general claims stand.
