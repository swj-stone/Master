\paragraph{What a veto can say about a refit.}
Even evaluating every restoration cannot in general recover the performance
of the reduced predictor from the masking votes alone. Let $D$ be an
equiprobable sign, $Y=D$, and let $R$ equal $D$ with probability $p$ and
$-D$ otherwise, independently of the value of $D$. Consider two populations,
$p=0.99$ and $p=0.50$. Both assign positive probability to all four input
vectors. Use the same reference score $f(d,r)=d(2+r)$, replace hidden
coordinates by zero, and use a fixed tie rule for the class prediction.
Whenever $D$ is visible, the score separates both classes perfectly for either
choice of mask on $R$. Whenever $D$ is hidden, the score is constant.
Thus the population mask scores (AUC and accuracy) are $(1,1)$ or
$(0.5,0.5)$ under both populations. All votes formed from these mask scores
agree. The same argument holds for panels that give equal mass to both classes.
After deleting $D$, the best score based on $R$ alone attains AUC and accuracy
$p$. For AUC, a positive case outranks a negative case with probability
$p^2$ and ties with probability $2p(1-p)$. At the $3\%$ tolerance the first
deletion passes the refit criterion and the second fails it. This example
gives the voting rule only the reference model's mask metrics; the original
training rows contain the information needed to distinguish the two cases.
