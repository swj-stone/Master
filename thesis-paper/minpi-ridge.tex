\section{Sampled MinPI with shared ridge fits}
\label{sec:sampled-minpi}
Let \(N\) index the \(p\) columns. Fit a ridge predictor on each coalition \(S\)
and measure its loss \(L(S)\) on a separate voting split. We use mean squared
Euclidean error, with a scalar response for regression and a one-hot response
for classification. The classification scores are unconstrained least-squares
outputs. Write \(b=L(\varnothing)\), replacing zero by one in this scale
factor. The positive utility \(U(S)=(1+L(S)/b)^{-1}\) gives the original
quota game and feature index~\cite{shi2026minpi}
\[
 v(S)=\mathbf{1}\{U(S)\ge\alpha U(N)\},\qquad
 m_i=\min_{S\subseteq N\setminus\{i\}}\bigl[v(S\cup\{i\})-v(S)\bigr].
\]
A negative marginal records a passing model that fails the quota after adding
\(i\). It identifies that particular coalition; the effect of removing \(i\)
from a delivered model is evaluated separately.

\paragraph{One factorization for many additions.}
Let \(X,Z\) denote the fitting and voting matrices, and \(Y,T\) their responses.
Center both splits using fitting means so the intercept is unpenalized. For a base
\(S\) set \(H=X_S^\top X_S+\lambda I\) with \(\lambda>0\),
\(B=H^{-1}X_S^\top Y\), and \(E=T-Z_S B\). For a new column with fitting
and voting vectors \(x,z\), define
\[
 q=X_S^\top x,\quad c=H^{-1}q,\quad
 d=\|x\|^2+\lambda-q^\top c,\quad
 a=(Y-X_SB)^\top x/d,\quad r=z-Z_Sc .
\]
\begin{proposition}
The enlarged model changes the voting predictions by \(ra^\top\).
Its loss change on \(n\) voting rows is
\[
 L(S\cup\{i\})-L(S)=
 \bigl(\|r\|^2\|a\|^2-2r^\top E a\bigr)/n .
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The enlarged Gram matrix has diagonal blocks \(H\) and \(\|x\|^2+\lambda\)
and cross-block \(q\). Its Schur complement satisfies
\(d=\lambda+\|x-X_Sc\|^2+\lambda\|c\|^2>0\). Substitution in the block
normal equations gives coefficients \(B-ca^\top\) and \(a^\top\).
Multiplying by the voting design yields the prediction change. Expanding
\(\|E-ra^\top\|_F^2-\|E\|_F^2\) gives the stated loss.
\end{proof}
Thus a single Cholesky factor of \(H\) serves every individual addition to \(S\).
The identity is exact in real arithmetic. Close to the quota, the implementation
can check an endpoint by direct refitting.

\paragraph{Sampling and stopping.}
Each round shuffles the columns and divides them into four nearly equal blocks.
For each target block in cyclic order, form three bases from the next one,
two, or three blocks. Evaluate every target column individually against each base.
For \(p<4\), use \(p\) singleton blocks and the next \(0,\ldots,p-1\) blocks.
For \(p\ge4\), one complete round schedules \(3p\) additions with at most twelve
base factorizations, excluding numerical boundary checks. The Gram matrix
still requires quadratic storage in \(p\).

The sampled minimum is an upper bound on \(m_i\). Its first \(-1\) fixes
\(m_i=-1\) and ends sampling for that feature. After seeing a zero we may
skip any losing base, since an addition to it cannot yield a negative
marginal. For fixed data let \(q_i\) be the probability that one random
partition contains any negative witness for \(i\). With \(R\) independent
partitions the probability of missing all such witnesses is \((1-q_i)^R\).
This calculation allows arbitrary dependence among the bases within a round.
Some coalition sizes are absent when \(p\ge4\), so a feature can have a
negative exact index even when \(q_i=0\).
