# Independent methodology review of the revision

This is a simulated pre-submission review of the ensemble theorem, the historical transcript study and the two policy comparisons. I checked the preceding manuscript PDF for context and evaluated the new source and receipts independently. The final integrated PDF is a separate reading below.

## Findings affecting the main claims

1. **Give each identification result its observer and its set of admissible worlds.** The finite theorem gives the exact joint metric set for known fitted models, fixed or transcript-adaptive queries, fixed replacements, a specified input domain for each row, and independently selectable row witnesses, optionally constrained by a score histogram. Its population instance for the stump separately allows every compatible class-conditional distribution. The 704-proposal audit keeps the complete empirical multiset of feature vectors and exchanges their row assignments, jointly for the refitted candidate and anchor. That stronger marginal constraint can make its set strictly smaller than the theorem's product-domain set. The empirical results do not count naturally recurring ambiguous test cohorts. The target here is a pass or breach against the corresponding refitted anchor under 3% relative losses on this finite test set. No AP floor was active in these 42 historical folds.
2. **The full-anchor endpoint resolves all 17 remaining historical cases by itself.** With development observations and no row-level test predictions, 704 of 704 proposals admit a reversed tolerance decision. The two empty-restoration outer masks leave 17 such proposals and certify 687 invariant. The full recorded family certifies all 704 invariant. I verified that the anchor with the full split-used tail restored has exactly the unmasked prediction partition on every one of the 42 folds and that its mask belongs to every one of the 704 recorded families. Adding this single endpoint to the two point masks already certifies all 704. These data give zero additional resolved decisions from interior masks after the endpoint. They can still alter other targets, including row-level scores. In the more informed observer that also receives each row's refitted anchor scores, both the point and the full mask family already certify all 704 decisions.
3. **An uncertain score and an uncertain decision have different counts.** With rowwise full-model and refitted-anchor scores supplied, at least one candidate probability vector can be reassigned in **109** of 704 point observers and **93** of 704 family observers. For either observer, 80 proposals have a transcript group with mixed labels. Only three have a group with both mixed labels and candidate scores. All three are on Phoneme fold 1, proposals 0, 1 and 3. Their only metric-changing swap exchanges test positions 64 and 306. AUC changes by at most 0.000138, accuracy stays fixed, and all three still breach. Calling three the number of proposals with unresolved row scores would be a numerical error. In contrast, uniqueness of an aggregate proposal-panel metric signature across the 704 observed supports permits retrospective lookup by that signature alone. It does not identify a fixed proposal's outcome across compatible inputs.
4. **Position the formal result as a finite-sample reduction.** The proof of the grouped integer table is sound, as are the midrank costs, the optional fixed score histogram and the common binary AUC/accuracy corners under monotone threshold classification. Finite tree cells provide a way to find allowed scores, given the refitted predictor. The result is not an efficient way to decide an unknown refit before training it. Computing compatibility on arbitrary input domains requires a way to decide whether cells intersect those domains. The product-domain argument allows duplicated rows. Link the richer fixed-multiset benchmark calculation by explaining that matching actual predictor rows inside each observed signature couples the rows. Continuous here means a real-valued model output at its recorded machine precision. Finite fitted trees only take finitely many values, and different reached leaves can emit exactly equal outputs. The distinctive contribution is carrying the observable/refit-score domains through to sharp joint empirical performance sets and checking the resulting decision on actual fits. Earlier work already verifies tree-ensemble input/output equivalence classes. [Törnblom and Nadjm-Tehrani (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04194) · [Mišić (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10883)
5. **A strict Gain primary policy and a vote screened by Gain answer different decisions.** Width is fixed and the slate contains the Gain prefix. Its rank sum is uniquely optimal by construction. The sum of raw mean gains also has a unique maximum on each of the 35 actionable folds in the saved receipt. The other eleven folds have no deletion. Adding vote *after* strict Gain therefore changes zero nominations. Prioritizing vote *before* strict Gain can override that choice. The matched best-half-of-support-slate comparison provides the nontrivial question. At fixed point tie-breaking adding vote changed one first-panel nomination and zero wider nominations. It increased guarded effective compression by 1.33 percentage points for both receivers on the first panel, entirely from Seeds. With uniform tie-breaking the four increments in the order first XGBoost, first logistic, wide XGBoost and wide logistic were +0.67, +0.67, +2.73 and +1.36 points. The best-half shortlist is a newly defined, retrospective choice; its quarter-width sensitivity makes the point-based difference disappear in all four combinations. These results estimate increments conditional on the chosen shortlist and tie rule.
6. **The forest experiment changes two stages of an XGBoost-defined candidate pipeline.** The forest masks on all fitting features and independently trains the forest guard and final receiver on each proposed support. The feature ranking, anchor, shortlist order, candidate sets and restoration design are inherited from XGBoost. Combining new forest outputs with the historical receivers isolates finite changes in those policies on these specific slates. A new end-to-end forest feature selector or a causal conclusion about learner families would require different evidence. With forest as both voter and receiver, the full margin exceeds the integer vote by 3.44 points of effective compression on the first panel and 5.49 on the wide panel. The first-panel breach rate rises by 6.67 points; the wide difference is zero. Only one first-panel task improves and one worsens; on the wider panel two tasks improve. The respective task-bootstrap intervals for utility are [−4.67, 15.00] and [0.00, 11.05] points. Wider outer concordance improves by about 0.185 on five contributing tasks and eleven mixed folds. Report this conditioning if comparing rankings across receivers.
7. **Full margin and vote preserve the same threshold, not the same ordering.** In the forest data I recovered all 216 vote, full-margin and single-margin scores' winning decisions from the stored panel tensor, including every sign agreement `margin >= 0` iff `vote >= 3`. There are 13 strict vote–full-margin reversals among 370 within-fold pairs on the wider panel. In one wide fold the full margin chooses outside the highest-vote group. Any attribution to breaking ties alone needs a policy that holds the primary count fixed and only uses magnitude within its ties. Point and full forest policies differ on four first-panel and two wide nominations despite equal panel-average guarded utility and breach. Use the equal averages when making a statement about utility and use receipts when making a statement about policies.

## Editorial decision on the inspected material

The theory and independent recounts support the finite conditional claims. The main scientific risk is attributing discrimination to the interior masks or general performance to the forest study's inherited XGBoost slate. The endpoint control and matched Gain contrasts give directly supportable statements in place of those broader interpretations. The magnitude of uncertainty under arbitrary label–feature reassignment should remain separate from the predictive utility of the deployable selector.

I checked its abstract, Sections 1–5, Appendix A and the relevant replication statements against the saved fits/results. I checked the stronger assertion at lines 222–224: the single and full margin give equal U on every one of the 46 forest/forest folds. They differ in breach status on two first-panel folds in offsetting directions, in selected nominees on six folds, and in delivered AUC on three. The claim as written specifically concerns U.

**Verdict for a selective ML evaluation track: borderline, leaning weak accept after a targeted revision.** The manuscript presents a defensible empirical methods contribution through the explicit observer dependence of a finite identification result, paired counterexamples against the actual refit learner, and comparison of one-guard policies at fixed candidate and tie rules. It supports scientific publication as a conditional evaluation of these policies. The known reduced learner in the formal theorem and the finite, empirical input domain are critical to that assessment. A claim of a new general method for forecasting unseen refit risk, or general superiority of the forest margin, would exceed the evidence. The practical gains are heterogeneous, with wider forest gains concentrated on two sources. Neither an additional benchmark panel nor additional forest seeds are necessary for the bounded conclusions in this review.

**Two changes would make the main historical result sufficiently interpretable and reproducible.**

1. **Report the unmasked-only control.** R7 correctly says adding the full model to the point pair resolves the last 17 decisions. In a separately checked comparison I supplied *only* the full model's per-row unmasked score partition and allowed unrestricted exchanges within each signature group, pairing the candidate and refitted anchor jointly. All 704 decisions were invariant. The straightforward baseline already supplies all observed decision identification on these fixed feature multisets. On the 42 folds counted once each, 35,619 of 38,021 outer-row occurrences had an individually unique unmasked score vector, approximately 93.68%. Nine folds had no repeated unmasked score at all. The full ranking of observer results remains correct in the order tested in R7, but without this control it cannot distinguish what requires observing any masked output. Readers should be able to see this in Section 3. It is an empirical finite-domain property, not sufficient identification of arbitrary inputs or out-of-sample prediction.
2. **Describe how all 704 invariant decisions were certified.** A.2 proves the product-domain proposition with a fixed anchor floor or with rowwise anchor scores observed. The principal benchmark calculations use an empirical input multiset with unknown final-anchor pairing. Every allowed reassignment permutes the *same* candidate and anchor score vectors together. Their individual pooled ranks stay fixed, so candidate AUC minus 0.97 times anchor AUC, and the corresponding accuracy gap, are sums of row-by-assigned-label costs, including the macro one-versus-rest AUC terms. Optimizing either gap within each observer signature yields coordinate extrema. Both lower extrema meeting the thresholds certify every assignment passes. Either upper extremum below threshold certifies all fail. A permitted permutation reversing the joint decision certifies ambiguity. In general, separate extrema need not settle a joint decision; here all cases were resolved, including explicit retries of the 26 initially inconclusive searches. A compact paragraph and a pointer to the released witness and verification script would link the theorem to the main numerical result. There was no active AP constraint on these 42 folds. The 1e-12 decision allowance is distinct from the conservative 1e-13 numerical check used for certificates.

**Minor changes.** In abstract lines 2–4 specify that both the full and reduced fitted predictors are fixed and known to the analyst doing the identification calculation. At line 203 the 216 items held fixed are candidate supports; different voters actually choose different nominees. Section 3 or its appendix could name the XGBoost implementation precision at which row probability vectors are compared and mention the 109 versus 93 count if the incremental effect on *individual scores* is discussed. The text already places the forest utility estimates beside the first panel's breach increase and the small numbers of contributing sources. A short abstract qualification covering that breach increase would make the positive numerical headline easier to interpret. A direct citation to earlier tree-ensemble input/output verification would improve the novelty boundary.
