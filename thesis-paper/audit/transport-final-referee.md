# Independent referee assessment

**Evaluations and Datasets vote — weak accept. Methods-track algorithm vote — reject.** I would publish this as an empirical audit of using frozen-tree losses to allocate a single candidate refit. My confidence is high in the finite-panel results and moderate in their significance beyond this protocol.

## Case for acceptance

The strongest experiment gives every candidate the same opportunity to be evaluated. It measures what a random choice among all candidates would achieve, what a random choice among the tied winners would achieve, and what the best choice could have achieved after the guard. Those controls change the interpretation of the apparent improvement. On the ten-task tree panel, replacing Gain with a continuous tie-break raises utility from 1.33% to 7.29%. Randomizing those same ties already gives 6.16%. Someone replicating only the two deterministic rules could attribute nearly all of the gain to reading the continuous loss. The wider-panel lottery among vote maximizers actually exceeds both deterministic tie-breaks. The finite study therefore gives practitioners a concrete reason to include a tie lottery when benchmarking a coarse score.

The second useful result concerns experimental design. On the wider tree panel, both continuous policies return the same final support except on one SECOM fold. With a half-width deletion and a fold weight of 1/18, the largest possible change in mean utility is 2.78 percentage points. The registered five-point target was already out of reach at the end of selection. This diagnoses what the prospective experiment could resolve independently of the outer-test labels. It is a better explanation for the observed zero than a confidence interval obtained by resampling six zeros.

The stump argument identifies the particular missing joint measurement and provides the attainable AUC–accuracy pairs under the stated information restriction. The constructed boosting example addresses a real weakness of the stump as an illustration of this pipeline, since the split-used anchor would ordinarily discard its redundant column. The sample collision analysis likewise distinguishes a finite lookup error floor from prediction on another task. These are sound supporting arguments. Their strength lies in explaining the empirical design. A practitioner who retains paired raw features and labels observes more information than the theorem's observer.

## Findings to address

1. **Correct the reported reversal on the wider tasks.** Lines 315–319 state that the six wider tasks “reversed the ordering of vote and point.” On those same tasks, the two methods tie on every tree-refit fold, with task-weighted utility of 7.34% each. Logistic refitting breaks the tie in favor of vote, 19.16% versus 8.21%. The wider logistic advantage over point arises from two sources. Urban contributes 8.33 of the 10.95 points, and there Gain alone also succeeds. The advantage over Gain comes entirely from Parkinson. A reversal across the two panels is a different comparison because the tasks also change. Tables 2 and 5 contain the correct numbers.

2. **Quantify whether the scores order useful candidates below the winner.** The paper's opening question concerns the information in masked losses. Its external result measures the return from the first nomination after a guard. Two policies can have equal return even when one orders passing and failing alternatives more accurately. The candidate archive allows an external check at negligible fitting cost. On each fold containing both outcomes, compare every outer-passing candidate with every outer-failing candidate, counting a score tie as one half. Average within a fold, then within a source, then across represented sources. On the first tree panel I get concordances of 0.752 for point, 0.717 for the full margin and 0.540 for vote (118 pairs, ten mixed folds, five sources). On the wider tree panel I get 0.630, 0.621 and 0.500 (128 pairs, twelve mixed folds, five sources). If success requires both guard and outer passage, the wider results are 0.604, 0.598 and 0.521 (93 pairs, eight folds, four sources). This would show readers where a measurable ranking difference can coexist with identical policy utility. Report the mixed-fold denominator, give source-level variation, and describe this as retrospective. Pairs within a fold share candidates and test observations.

3. **Keep the scope of the negative result visible.** The tree study evaluates a median replacement, a split-Gain anchor, one pool of up to eight half-width candidates per fold and one tolerance-based guard. Two 16-donor replacements yield full-minus-point effects with opposite signs on the wider tree panel. The six-source panel establishes an exact bound for its sealed median-mask comparison. Generalizing to a replacement distribution would require additional donor draws or a sufficiently accurate marginal average. Generalizing across candidate pools would require independent pools. Neither generalization is necessary for the finite audit. The weak accept reflects the narrow policy family and the small number of sources with informative disagreements.

## Decision boundary

I would move toward a clear accept if the authors provide a reviewer-usable candidate-level artifact and report the external ordering analysis alongside the policy outcomes. An independent replication across pools, with the disagreement bound reported before outer scoring, would strengthen transport claims further. I would move to reject if the work were presented as evidence that richer masking is generally futile or that the minimum vote is an effective selector across learners. The paired comparison with a uniform nominee, the alternative tie-breaks, and the two-source concentration of the logistic effect do not support those claims. The methods-track vote would require a reproducible advantage over credible cost-matched selectors on independent tasks with enough opportunity for their decisions to differ.
