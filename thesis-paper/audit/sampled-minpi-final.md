# Referee assessment of *Finding Negative Min-Marginal Votes for Feature Selection*

## First compiled version

I checked all six pages of the PDF with SHA-256 `CA3C4E990F7283038FBC15F4590DC63C6974B20011B2D8BA80D53800763B95E7` against the quota game in Shi and Zhao, the ridge and chain samplers, the registered protocols, the matched-budget expectation and the source-level evidence. This is an internal, author-requested assessment.

**Assessment.** The finite-observation question has a precise, correct answer. For one fixed coordinate, every coalition is a single endpoint of exactly one edge. Filling unseen endpoints in opposite directions gives one Boolean game that attains the proposed maximum. This result remains meaningful when data and a learner constrain the actual unseen votes, provided sharpness is stated for the unrestricted Boolean completions. The paper also gives an auditable computational result: reusing a base fit was 8.73 times faster at ranking on the six wider sources and slower than cached direct fits on seven of eight narrower sources. Neither result establishes that choosing columns by their worst vote produces useful compression beyond coefficient ranking. I would recommend major revision of a submission claiming a generally better feature selector.

### Substantive requests

1. **Put the practically strongest comparisons in the main result.** The abstract juxtaposes improved exact-witness recovery and the cheaper ridge pipeline relative to boosted-tree gain. They use different sources and answer different questions. On the eight held-out sources, votes changed six of 40 coefficient supports; classification missed the accuracy floor. On the next four sources, bisection matched 16 of 20 coefficient supports, returned fewer negatives than individual-addition sampling and missed the registered speed-and-width objective in both domains. Say what changes for the reader who already has a coefficient selector. State whether the gain comparison survives the common XGBoost guard, giving width, test quality, the measured time component and repetition count together. Publish its adverse results as prominently as its favorable ones. The legacy gain implementation used a GPU; all numerical timing ratios apply to the measured hardware and downstream pipelines.
2. **Test identity-specific deletion directly.** Relative to coefficients, testing a prefix of the MinPI order changes both which columns can be selected and which widths pass. The completed witness-deletion diagnostic is closer to the original question. It finds 16 actionable splits among 60. A witnessed deletion passed the guard on 9 of 16; a matched random proposal passed on 6 and a coefficient-tail proposal on 3. Relative to unmodified coefficient selection, the geometric width improvements were 1.7% in classification and 5.6% in regression, below the specified 10% objective. Report the per-domain opportunity counts and avoid interpreting this reused panel as prospective confirmation. The extra guard can reject a real negative witness because its empirical context differs from the candidate model.
3. **Compare with efficient ridge selection.** Dense bordered solves and exact leave-one-out ridge updates predate this work. The available greedy-RLS control selects faster or slower depending on the comparator. Specifically, chain search took 0.667 and 0.811 times the selection time of the local greedy RLS implementation on the new classification and regression sources and retained 1.159 and 1.231 times as many columns. The narrower supports went to greedy RLS. Include these pairs whenever presenting chain speed relative to slower methods. The comparison is with the local implementation and its actual full ranking path. No inference about the optimized RLScore package follows. [Pahikkala, Airola and Salakoski](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.3570), [Pahikkala and Airola](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v17/16-470.html).
4. **State the novelty at the right scale.** The original article already gives the binary game, self-certifying negative minima, loss of exact negatives under budgeted sampling, and the all-edges definition of a decisive feature. The additional result here is the coordinatewise sharp upper bound for incomplete shared observations. Schur updates, a Boolean dictator and bracket bisection each have elementary derivations. Describe their use in this sampling problem. At introduction lines 19–21, remove the assertion that SAGE/ShapG examine “the same sequence of model comparisons.” The continuous predictive-power game, feature removal, weights and sampling law can differ. A comparison of a weighted average with the worst edge is sufficient. [Shi and Zhao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33885), [Covert, Lundberg and Lee](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/c7bf0b7c1a86d5eb3be2c722cf2cf746-Abstract.html).
5. **Keep the denominator and dependence visible.** The historical bisection advantage (nine versus zero observed, conditional random expectation 0.923) concerns three informative folds from two sources. The external four-source test counted nine for bisection, six for uniform and 43 for individual additions; one returned support differed between bisection and uniform. Landsat reused its official test for five training seeds and had no negative index on any tested split; Power Plant also had no negative on any split. Thus two of the most compressed sources cannot demonstrate a gain from identifying negatives. The four-source comparison has two sources per domain. The actual source-level pairs and explicit primary endpoint failures convey more than any claim of population equivalence.

The article uses eight primary sources, six historical wider sources, six familiar conference sources and four fresh chain-validation sources for different purposes. This sequence explains why adaptively chosen improvements on previous sources have weaker external evidence. Table 1 and Table 2 correctly separate their aggregation conventions and the timing boundaries. Providing a single source for each result and every comparator's measured width guards against implicitly combining the favorable part of one experiment with the favorable part of another.

### What would change the scientific judgment

A positive claim for the selector would need a specified deployment problem where finding additional exact negatives produces a measurable downstream benefit over the cheap matched selector. Fix the search and deletion decision on development data. On new source-level replicates, demonstrate either lower actual decision cost at no larger width or usefully fewer columns within declared predictive tolerances. Report where no eligible proposal exists. Existing experiments already support the narrower result about which votes can be inferred for free and how much they cost to measure. More reruns on the same outer tests would add little to that result.

### Subsequently available strong-guard evidence

I checked the 30-split primary table and the five source-level classification comparisons against the direct gain ranker with a common XGBoost guard and predictor. Classification MinPI took 0.896 times the selection time, retained 1.467 times the columns, lost 0.00357 AUC and lost 0.00511 accuracy. Every classification source had a width ratio above one. Madelon lost 0.0240 AUC and 0.02885 accuracy, and MinPI selected exactly the coefficient support on all five Madelon splits. Abalone took 0.920 times the selection time and retained 1.516 times the columns, with changes of +0.00286 in R² and −0.00650 in MAE. These results fail the registered composite advantage against gain in both domains. Timings come from one execution per split and method, compared with three for the ridge-guard experiment. The five-source resampling interval for the classification width ratio was 1.184 to 1.770. Each source retains its five correlated splits in these resamples; there is one regression source.

## Final compiled version and recommendation

I verified the seven-page PDF with SHA-256 `41305905E61F41FE045ED7817DD9CD1102FFC696A4171BC748DDC8A2B671136A`. I read its text and tables, inspected a rendering of every page and checked the reported comparative results against the saved evidence. The title page displays only the anonymous author line. All three tables are legible in the document and the compiled log reports no unresolved references or overfull boxes.

The abstract and Table 2 now give both effects of changing the receiver. They report 47% wider classification supports with a shared XGBoost receiver. Table 2 specifies the one-versus-three timing counts and the surrounding text identifies the hardware and information used for ranking. Section 5 gives the failure against the full-ridge primary classification floor and the six changed coefficient supports. Section 7 reports all three identity-specific deletion controls, the failed width target, the two sources with no observed negative despite substantial compression, and the failed chain speed–width objective. The introduction corrected the attribution comparison and the manuscript cites the antecedent path search. The random single-drop benchmark follows by adding the two boundary-edge inclusion probabilities and the interior-edge inclusion probabilities and then dividing by the number of candidate edges. This model is clearly separated from the empirical expectation conditional on the measured brackets. [Chen and Waingarten](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05309).

The final greedy-RLS control supersedes my initial three-repeat numbers. Stopping greedy construction at the last required proper prefix preserves predictions at every tested width. I checked the final ratios against the nine-repeat timing comparison in `greedy-rls/effects.csv`: chain/greedy selection time is 0.716 for classification and 1.070 for regression; chain/greedy width remains 1.159 and 1.231. The changed direction of the regression timing reinforces the need to avoid small runtime claims based on a few repeats.

**Scientific judgment.** The paper is defensible as a focused computational study of partial information in MinPI games. For the claims actually made, my substantive correction requests are closed. The evidence for a general feature-selection advance is weak: the new independent comparison has only two sources per domain, the matched coefficient selector usually returns the same predictors, and targeted removal misses the practical width threshold. For a broad top-tier machine-learning venue, I would recommend rejection on significance in its present form. A specialized methods or workshop review is warranted on the narrower contribution of sharp partial-observation bounds and tested costs of recovering witnesses. More editing would not change the former judgment; prospective evidence of an advantage that depends on the identities of witnessed features would.
