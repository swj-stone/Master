# Independent simulated area-chair review of decision R12

**Recommendation: Weak accept. Confidence: high on the decision analyses.** I would support acceptance as a methodological and empirical paper on what masking measurements can identify and how to test their value for selecting a deletion. I see no material scientific blocker for those claims. The evidence for a portable selection rule is weak, and the manuscript accurately describes the held-task results. The limited incremental contribution of shared feasibility on the historical archive is the main reason for my reservation about significance.

## Shared assignments and regret

The fixed-dictionary observer is clear. The two candidates and their anchor face the same assignment, both candidate guards are held at delivery for this comparison, and the analyst at nomination time lacks these outer queries and reduced fits (pp. 3–4). The rank-sum expression on p. 22 allows the anchor floor to vary under the same assignment. Marginal AUC and accuracy maxima may use different assignments, so a joint passing witness matters.

The SPECTF result addresses the earlier reviewer's counterexample directly. I independently loaded the three archived candidate predictions, their refitted anchor, labels and four frozen query codes for fold 2, then enumerated the distinct binary label allocations within the observed groups. Direct AUC and accuracy scoring gave 8 allocations with state 010, 10 with 100, 56 with 110 and 6 with 111. Thus S dominates V for the stated payoff even though both can fail. S and T can each win by 2/7. An equal lottery pays worst-case expected regret 1/7 and neither deterministic choice can do better than 2/7. These claims follow from which states are feasible, irrespective of how many allocations yield each.

The headline count is correct but its interpretation deserves one additional sentence (abstract and p. 4). I recovered 926 constant ties, 294 fixed strict winners, 57 one-sided comparisons including a tie and 6 permitting either strict winner. Of the 1,220 constant differences, **1,218 already follow from separate invariance certificates for each candidate given its own point query and the anchor**. On two SPECTF pairs, adding the other candidate's query also fixes the two individual statuses. This distinction matters if readers want to know what joint reasoning adds to Table 1.

I further held the pair’s combined observer fixed and compared its joint pass set with the Cartesian product of its two marginal pass sets. Common feasibility excluded a joint state on five pairs, all on SPECTF fold 2, and narrowed the utility difference on three. Candidate IDs (4,8) and (7,8) allow 00, 10, 11, excluding the second candidate as strict winner. Pair (8,9) allows 00, 01, 11, excluding the first. On (4,7) and (4,9), excluding 00 leaves both strict orderings and a tie attainable. The common-world result therefore has a precise positive empirical interpretation even where neither candidate's passage is identified. I would report this count near 1,220/1,283 and retain the three-candidate example.

## What the forest and held-source evidence supports

I recomputed each candidate's guard and outer passage from saved probabilities and labels, assembled the highest voting tier on each of the 46 original and 138 local records, and recovered the eligible nominees and guarded U and breach for all four rules. Uniform choice averages the outcomes of the individual eligible nominees before observing their guard results. This resolves the earlier concern about giving the margin a more favorable pool (Tables 5 and 15).

Full minus uniform U was +0.91 and +2.71 percentage points on the original panels, and −0.25 and +1.26 on the local panels. The corresponding changes in breach rate were +4.03, +2.78, +1.25 and −1.04. All four reported U intervals include zero. Applying the paper's hierarchy to visible ties alone gave +1.30, +9.50, +0.31 and +0.70 points. On the wider original panel the conditional result covers ten tied folds and its interval is [−2.77, 23.17]. The guard and fixed widths leave few folds with different attainable U. This explains the difference between the two estimands and preserves uncertainty about a future task. The Parkinson AUC example gives an important reason to report the underlying metrics alongside binary tolerances.

I also reselected the tie rule with each source excluded, using the source means reconstructed from the prediction archives. At a breach penalty of 0.5 the held-source net differences versus whole-slate point selection were −4.17, −6.18, +1.69 and −1.35 points, reproducing Table 16. The library and penalty comparison were developed retrospectively, and each omission trains on largely the same sources. Page 23 restricts the intended reading appropriately. In particular, averaging effects on observed sources does not yield a task-specific trigger for buying more mask evaluations.

## Four-bit control, cost and practical significance

The class-preserving recoding answers a concrete question. Recounting the proposal-level records at four bits gave 338 witnessed reversals and 9 unresolved for the genuine code. The five class-preserving draws yielded 470–475 witnessed and 6–14 unresolved. The number of genuine reversals could be as large as 347 and the smallest witnessed class-preserving count still exceeds it by 123. Across 38,021 row occurrences, 37,336 could receive a different code and 14,940–15,054 actually did. Conditioning donor exchange on predicted class and multiplicity provides evidence about within-class grouping on these dictionaries. These five draws do not estimate a population semantic-information effect, and the manuscript stays within that boundary (p. 5).

Table 3 puts the eight-round alternative on comparable footing. I reaggregated the 414 recorded executions with the printed median-within-fold convention. Across the ten tasks the whole point, full and eight-round policies took 7.87, 8.11 and 9.53 seconds, and across the wider tasks 11.29, 12.11 and 12.78 seconds. The eight-round screen trained 126 and 125 small fits, respectively. Its point-relative U changes were −0.96 and +2.79 points, and the wider-panel interval was [−5.43, 11.06]. Timing and performance are available together, including the singleton folds. The visible 1/36 bound on the original wide-panel comparison is an especially useful design result, because it can rule out the prespecified improvement without opening the outer scores (pp. 6, 26).

I would retain the practical recommendation to benchmark a point mask with a separate guard. The archive demonstrates the value of a matched lottery, the ability of a score to encode row identity, and the circumstances where the observational design has almost no power to distinguish policies. It provides little leverage for recommending an expensive rule on a previously unseen task. My verification reused the recorded fitted probabilities and timings. I did not repeat the training or hardware benchmark.

## Final decision on the revised R13 edition

**Weak accept. No further scientific revision required for the current claims.** I verified the 46-page `thesis-paper/build-decision-r13/main.pdf`, SHA-256 `E128184E2E5E826B7E35A055BE6E83E27A1E8A28511D2446882DC2076B321460`. Its abstract now reports the 1,218 cases of prior individual identification and the three pairs on which common feasibility rules out a strict ordering. Section 3 (p. 4, lines 167–175) distinguishes the two information comparisons explicitly. From the individual certificates and the exact paired pass sets, I independently regenerated all 1,283 rows’ separate-candidate Cartesian sets, combined-query Cartesian sets and shared pass sets. Each matched the new attribution table. Pooling the queries accounts for the two additional singleton differences. At the combined observer, coupling narrows five pass sets and three utility sets, fixes no further utility difference, and all five examples come from a single historical fold.

The earlier review's principal scientific requests are met. The SPECTF example supplies sharp comparative regret, the forest lottery quantifies the residual contribution of the continuous score, the class-preserving control isolates within-class grouping, and Table 3 prices the small-refit alternative alongside its outcomes. The evidence supports publication on these terms. My reservation concerns the size and transferability of the empirical effects. The strictly narrower payoff sets are rare and concentrated, and the forest comparisons offer no consistent held-source payoff for buying more masking computation. Those findings limit the independent case for a stronger acceptance recommendation even after the attribution issue has been resolved.
