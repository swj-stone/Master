# Scientific review of the 14-page release and its revisions

## Reading 1 — before the new experiments

The manuscript examined here is the version with SHA-256 `fbbde8cd0e39ede3dff8c5f5b45d9baf675a88fc262c859dd5de29494c8bf368`. I would reject this version as a paper introducing a superior general-purpose feature selector. I would also request a major revision before evaluating it as a mechanism paper. Its clearest guarantee concerns an assumed veto pattern. The evidence for that pattern in fitted models had not been reported.

### Does the motivating mechanism occur in the archived models?

I fixed an [audit protocol](pair-model-protocol.md) before calculating new compatibility counts. For each archived proposal I enumerated the ordered pairs in its tail. For each pair I compared the actual vector of acceptances and vetoes on *all observed masks* with the vector predicted by the proposition. I repeated this on ranking and guard rows. This uses the original weighted AUC, accuracy and rare-class average precision or MAE/clipped R². Each veto requires at least two failing panels out of four. It is an in-sample falsification test using correlated masks and proposals. A positive match would only be necessary for the full model; a discrepancy on a single observed mask suffices to falsify it for these measured ballots.

| Observed on 226 eligible proposals | Ranking rows | Guard rows |
|---|---:|---:|
| At least one accepting and one vetoing restoration | 148 | 129 |
| Empty or full-tail restoration already vetoes | 200 | 195 |
| Both endpoints accept and at least one interior restoration vetoes | 17 | 19 |
| At least one ordered pair predicts every observed decision | **0** | **0** |

Of the 274 original proposals, 48 have fewer than two tail features. Ten Abalone proposals have exactly two tail columns and all four possible masks were evaluated. They are informative here despite having no novel masks. The 17 and 19 endpoint-pass, interior-veto proposals are particularly relevant. None satisfies even the *sign* implication on its respective rows. This result rules out treating the exact ordered-pair model as an observed explanation of these trials. Local contributions, approximate patterns, higher-order effects, and unobserved candidate populations remain open. The previous 115/216 count of changes at fixed mask size establishes a different property. [Executable audit](pair_model.py) · [Per-task and per-proposal records](pair-model-summary.csv) · [Individual proposals](pair-model-proposals.csv)

The manuscript's signed-regression example does realize the signs. Its values of the continuous veto margin for empty, v-only and joint restoration, however, are all −10⁻¹². The advertised premise requires δ > 2η, placing the v-only margin strictly below both the empty and joint margins. That specific example therefore does not instantiate the stronger quantitative premise. The implication in the mathematical proposition remains valid. The minimum number of nonadaptive probes is Spencer's classical bound, and the connection between incomparable signatures and ordered-pair coverage was already explicit in experimental design. The margin function also combines scores in their reporting units. Rescaling just one component can change its numeric separation even if all ballot signs stay fixed. A new draft should state exactly which model is being proved, show one example satisfying precisely that model, and disclose the real-data falsification. [Spencer](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9800(70)80038-2) · [Hyttinen, Eberhardt and Hoyer](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v14/hyttinen13a.html)

### Claims that this release does not establish

| Claim or reader inference | Evidence and required correction |
|---|---|
| A veto indicates harmful deletion after retraining | The 14 “failures” were failures of a masked fixed predictor on newly sampled masks and other validation rows. Defining harm with the same proxy that casts the vote cannot validate the downstream meaning of that proxy. The existing final-support AUC table is not a proposal-level comparison. Separate row and mask changes, then use independently evaluated refits of candidate and anchor on the *same* proposals. Report false positives, false negatives and task denominators even when no harmful event is observed. [Removal estimands](https://jmlr.org/papers/v22/20-1316.html) |
| The coded family improves detection because of pair coverage | Nested and coded families ask different numbers of questions and produce different widths. Random masks with the same counts and sizes find 11/14 of the previously reported discrepancies compared with 10/14 for code. At 100% ordered-pair coverage, changing codeword assignment also changes refitted AUC. Such results do not identify a real-data effect of pair coverage. Use the code as a controlled design and state the negative finding. |
| Voting itself accounts for the reported compression | The compact-Gain stage already reduces the mean fraction to 47.0%. Voting takes it to 37.9%. Approximately 85% of the total mean reduction from 100% precedes voting. AUC falls from 0.9374 at the anchor to 0.9281, and measured runtime increases. A high-dimensional input also passes through a supervised top-256 screen before the selector sees it. Show the incremental change and its price. |
| The tested Gain baseline corresponds to the historical direct Gain comparator | The [original PI benchmark](../../docs/thesis/experiments/piv0-five-seeds.md) scores each width along a fixed ranking by refitting and picks its validation optimum. The current [compact Gain anchor](../../src/minppi_mofs/piv3_compact_tree.py) uses masked predictions, an 8% relative tolerance, and at most 12 tested widths. Both can be useful controls. Their estimators, objectives and costs differ. The last public-facing report used “直接 Gain” for the latter. Label the two precisely and add a direct refit control when making comparisons with that incumbent. A width donated by the proposed method also cannot establish dominance at an independently imposed budget. |
| A 9.1% drop in mask calls means a stable end-to-end acceleration | Both implementations execute identical proposals and return identical supports with fixed ballots. Of 300 saved evaluations, 193 come from one task, Movement Libras. Eight of 14 dataset totals have zero savings. The four-second suite time times the deletion step and excludes reference and final fits. Baselines can be faster even with the original implementation's input dataset fully retained. Repeat paired, order-balanced end-to-end timing on a controlled or externally specified workload and report task-specific heterogeneity. The 3,304/3,004 observation remains valid on the named trace. |
| Fourteen row folds represent fourteen independently sampled applications or out-of-entity tests | The cohort is a selected collection, with three folds per task. Exact-vector grouping addresses a subset of row overlaps. It cannot identify participants or experimental units. The influential Sonar cohort contains reflections measured at multiple aspect angles from a metal cylinder and rock targets. PenDigits has an original held-out partition from 14 writers, while the PMLB random folds remix the 10,992 observations. These are particularly concrete sources of dependence. Retain the task-specific numbers, characterize the conditional sign-test assumptions, and validate a new frozen procedure on a source with an entity-respecting split. [Original Sonar measurements](https://doi.org/10.24432/C5T01Q) · [Original PenDigits split](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/81/pen+based+recognition+of+handwritten+digits) |
| This selector calculates the published MinPI power index | The conference index minimizes individual discrete contributions to a threshold game over coalitions. The new operation has a support-valued argument, fixed reference, empirical panels and a conjunction of majority tests on selected contexts. It provides no featurewise −1/0/+1 index or guarantee of the original index. A new name, or an explicit “inspired by” description, would avoid implying that equivalence. [Original definition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33885) |
| Replay establishes scientific validity, novelty or acceptability | Replaying the stored predictions is valuable software and data validation. It cannot turn a fixed threshold inherited from development into an independently justified choice, remove the effect of repeated use of the original 14 tasks, or establish acceptance by an external venue. The four panel votes reuse rows, and no calibrated error probability follows from a 3-of-4 rule. The scoped statements in the methods already acknowledge much of this. The title, abstract, discussion and user-facing verdict must observe the same limits. [Selection-induced bias](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html) |

The most constructive contribution left in the release is a testable distinction between a change in a masked-model ballot and a meaningful loss after selecting and retraining a model. A negative empirical answer is worth reporting. Files, fold counts, extra references or repeated checks of identical predictions cannot by themselves resolve this question.

## Reading 2 — the rewritten measurement paper

I read the complete working PDF at `thesis-paper/build-writer-r2/main.pdf` (SHA-256 `9a6370d5da7f9514485dab59c0d0beefc86d1476eb0442c1f5f35af3499f2813`, main text `9292064e2dfe2fdcb04ee3456d8aec440084013ff5299f6a719c6b760a1837fc`). The new paper asks whether votes predict the measured cost of retraining. It no longer presents the support-level vote as the original power index. That is a material revision to the estimand.

* **Scope of 0/226.** The abstract and methods now use the exact decision pattern on observed masked predictions. The methods distinguish 226 eligible proposals, 148 ranking-side and 129 guard-side mixed patterns and 17/19 endpoint-pass candidates with an interior veto. This correctly confines falsification to a finite set of earlier search proposals, fixed row panels and the precise equivalence in equation (2). The new constructed binary classifier truly realizes the stronger continuous margin. Its two features manipulate the masking indicator by construction, and the manuscript says so. No population frequency or absence of general interactions follows.
* **Nonidentifiability example.** I ran Lean 4 on `formal/RefitTarget.lean`. Both finite statements compiled without axioms. Running the exact Wolfram Language source gave AUC/accuracy (½,½) for each D-hidden mask and (1,1) for each D-visible mask in both populations, followed by (99/100,99/100) and (½,½) when predicting with R. Mask *metrics* coincide; the original training distributions can differ and distinguish the worlds. These are attainable population scores for the optimal predictor from the surviving column. A fixed finite-data XGBoost fit can obtain other scores. Specify the population target where drawing the conclusion.
* **Measured proxy utility.** I checked the candidate source code, cutoffs, archived table and 14 task denominators. AUC and accuracy are compared on the same held-out rows for the two fresh fits. Switching from endpoints to the separating design adds 11 true and 20 false alarms among the historical-path proposals. Seven additional true alarms occur on Sonar, two on SPECTF and one each on Vehicle and Yeast. Removing Sonar gives four and nineteen. Contributions range from six to 36 proposals per task. The equal-task TP/FP fractions change from 0.53932/0.34921 to 0.56133/0.38889. The observed additional detections under separately drawn deletions remain zero. Add the influential-task result, the omitted protocol-defined one-panel minimum, and an always-veto or equivalent chance-performance comparator. “False alarm” is relative to one stipulated outer-fold outcome. The table does not disentangle sampling variation, change of model, and use of a restoration at decision time only.
* **Compression and benchmark identity.** I compared the exhaustive-Gain adapter with the historical ascending-width loop and its higher-index tie convention. The new run contains 1,209 width evaluations and 42 final refits (1,251 fits including the initial full fit reused for its width). The old compact-Gain heuristic and exhaustive validation-AUC selection are now explained correctly. The main table in this PDF still omits the exhaustive baseline. Add the row from all 42 folds. Full-precision means give the post-anchor AUC cost as 0.009356, and time increment as 0.084772 seconds. Use approximately 0.00936 and 0.085.
* **Official UCI HAR holdout.** I checked the saved split, worker/scorer boundary, label-read log and participant contrasts. External windows come from nine distinct people excluded from both selection partitions. Supplier-normalized engineered inputs are taken as given. Against same-width RFE, separating's median/mean participant differences were −1.99/−1.27 percentage points (individual range −6.46 to +4.69). Against Gain they were +1.83/+2.16 points (−0.34 to +7.91). Include these prespecified summaries; clarify which training people supply each baseline. Subject separation does not make the overlapping windows independent. The post-hoc random-column controls and hardware-source mapping help put 22 retained columns in context. Their training-derived supports, scoring chronology and conditional meaning should be identifiable.
* **Missing follow-ups in this PDF.** The second-reading build still reports the old four-second inner-search comparison. The paired, repeated end-to-end timing exists and misses the specified 5% target. Include that direct test and the work counts separately. The independent-refit guard was still completing at the time of this reading. Include its results and plain-guard comparisons, including negative outcomes, before reaching a scientific verdict.

**Disposition at this reading.** The proxy diagnostic, mathematical counterexample and genuine subject-held-out case materially strengthen a limited, largely negative paper. They do not show that the original MinPI index has been improved, or that the proposed vote is an advantageous generic selector. The completeness and wording items above remained open in the hashed PDF.

## Reading 3 — the public-facing 17-page manuscript

I read the release `thesis-paper/minpi.pdf`, SHA-256 `fc8d93bb4d1d8dc8f0c78fc805ed187bd0d85fc238df7a6c4f30bd9daba6b560`. This is an internal review by a separate agent, not an external review or venue decision.

| Earlier objection | Outcome in this PDF |
|---|---|
| The predicted interaction was not tested or the zero count was overinterpreted. | The paper gives the observed-mask, eligible-proposal and fixed-panel restrictions. All 226 eligible proposals lack an ordered pair explaining every observed sign on each of two panel sets. The 216 novel-mask tests have a different inclusion rule. The construction formalizes the specific deterministic vote pattern. The possibility of partial or more complicated interactions remains open. |
| The reduced-model target was conflated with a masked model or certified by Lean. | The counterexample assigns the same AUC/accuracy to all four masks in both populations, while their best surviving-column scores differ. The prose specifies the distribution and optimal score; the finite checks concern arithmetic. The empirical proposal outcome now comes from two fresh fits on common rows, tested on common outer rows. Remaining ambiguities in what caused an individual false alarm are acknowledged by specifying what changes at evaluation. |
| Controls and error costs were missing. | The proposal table includes all eight protocol arms. The always-veto counts, cutoff sensitivity, pooled and task-weighted denominators, and the Sonar sensitivity are present. The added interiors produced 11 extra true and 20 extra false alarms on paths, versus zero and nine on drawn deletions. Seven of the 11 true alarms came from one task. Higher false-positive cost would make this operating point still less attractive. Deletion fraction alone outscored maximum panel dissent for predicting observed refit loss across widths. Among pairs at the same width the modest concordances are descriptive, with shared supports and folds. |
| The paper lacked the historical direct-Gain baseline and conflated compression with improvement. | Table 4 contains the exhaustive refit-at-every-width control and describes its validation objective. Widths and timings were computed as stated, with the separate timing batch distinguished. Compact Gain accounts for approximately 85% of the mean reduction. The vote costs a further 0.00936 mean AUC and 0.085 seconds in the original run. Equal-width native RFE and ANOVA receive a width already selected by voting; the manuscript explains the dependency. |
| The guard or maximin variant might make the votes clinically or algorithmically useful. | A held-out guard on 41 folds gave effectively identical aggregate AUC to a width-matched prefix with the same guard; the independent half-anchor prefix had slightly higher average AUC. The new score saved evaluations in an inner enumeration but tied for best on 32 of the 40 nonsingleton supports. Neither result demonstrates an independently validated benefit in selecting identities or a bound on loss. |
| Savings in mask count were reported as end-to-end speed. | The measured complete pipelines preserved support and outer predictions. Three cyclic repetitions on 42 folds give paired median elapsed savings of 0.85%, 2.12% and 1.32% for the three contrasts. All miss the 5% criterion; the paper discloses both the CPU timer resolution and the concentration of savings in Movement Libras. |
| Measures on correlated windows and batches obscured the external unit and comparison. | The HAR analysis gives the nine participant-level units, overlap and supplier preprocessing, paired contrasts including ranges, random anchor/all-input supports, and what the selected columns imply for sensors. The three chronological test batches are separately identified and scored, with a single underlying array. The original RFE comparator does use additional selection-label history. This motivates the retrospective history check below. |

Two statements warranted revision before final release. The abstract's “little protection” conflated observed failure detection (97.4% on paths) with the practical cost of vetoing most acceptable deletions. The discussion's “worked as well” read as an equivalence conclusion without a prespecified margin. I requested the observed sensitivity and false-positive tradeoff, and the actual endpoints of the half-anchor versus voting comparison. The strongest support is for a measurement paper about the tested proxy. I would reject a claim that this study establishes a broadly faster and more accurate selector, a new combinatorial covering bound, independence of the observed tasks, or improvement of the original featurewise MinPI index.

### Final release and decision of the third reading

I checked the actual 18-page `thesis-paper/minpi.pdf`, SHA-256 `515dfa9890c04bf28c0f97637e2488ca65ccf26488a4dece57530c299db1728b`. The final abstract reports 184/189 detected path failures alongside 194/211 false alarms. The discussion gives the one exceedance in 41 folds for each of two guarded rules, with observed mean performance and compression. The holdout chronology is limited explicitly to the primary protocols; the subsequent controls are identified as retrospective.

**External information controls.** I rescored the 12 Gas and two HAR controls from source test labels and each row's archived probabilities. The equal-unit scores and reported secondary endpoints agreed to within `2.23e-16`. Four full-history Gas support/probability arrays and the full-history HAR control exactly reproduced their original results. At a fixed six-column width, RFE selecting through batches 5, 6, and 7 achieved 41.72%, 52.63% and 57.80% mean batch macro recall, against 35.81% for voting. Through batch 6 both procedures can use the same historical labels in different roles. At 22 columns the vote attained 89.40% mean participant accuracy, while RFE using the initial 15 people attained 89.47%, and RFE using all 21 attained 90.68%. Voting also used the remaining six people's ranking labels. Against 15-person RFE, paired vote-minus-RFE differences averaged −0.065 percentage points, had median −0.549, and ranged from −3.401 to +2.362 across nine people. These controls materially change the HAR interpretation. Keeping the same test people and batches does not constitute a new replication. [Full grid and access rules](../../experiments/context_review/matched_history.md)

**Refitted-prefix rule.** I checked the code deciding panels, the fixed candidate widths, the disjoint guards, the final-fold data and the separate full-refit audit. The 147 shared inner fits across 41 folds generated predictions for three decision rules; 63 distinct outer fits cover their selections and anchors. The reproduction audit enumerates 4,236 ballot entries. The point-global, point-class and four-panel-class rules had 16, 8 and 7 observed class-recall exceedances, versus 2, 2 and 3 global exceedances. Compared with the point-class rule the four-panel rule changed four widths, preventing a class-recall exceedance on Satimage and SPECTF, introducing one on New Thyroid, and leaving Vehicle's class-recall outcome unchanged. The 576 versus 199 checks are logical decision counts. Full ballot tables and all grid fits were computed. These data support the statement that adding a class-by-class check accounts for most of the decrease. They cannot establish a reliable incremental gain from four correlated panels or training acceleration. [Grid design](../../experiments/context_review/refit_vote_grid.md) · [Independent fit counts](refit-review-grid.csv)

**Decision.** No remaining material scientific correction is required for this version's stated purpose as a bounded measurement and negative-results paper. I would submit this manuscript for independent review at a venue accepting empirical evaluation of feature selection. I would reject the stronger methods claim that this is an improved featurewise MinPI with demonstrated general speed, accuracy or formal protection. The existing comparisons do not show this. Venue fit, external review, and acceptance remain undetermined.
