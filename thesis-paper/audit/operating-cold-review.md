# Cold review of the feature-deletion study

## First reading

I reviewed the 22-page `packages/operating-review-drafts/reading-1.pdf`, SHA-256 `9D61FC924668248492C3EF1A7B2E20719438AF0747511DD0A804108819CF274B`. I read the PDF before consulting the experimental records. I used no prior review or author response. This is an internal, independent reading by an AI reviewer. It represents one judgment.

**Scientific recommendation — weak accept as an empirical study.** The strongest finding is the difference between predicting the ordering of deletions and choosing a deletion worth refitting. On the observed tasks the vote helps order some same-width candidates. Acting on it within a fixed refit budget yields a measurable gain on one fold at the cost of two more tolerance breaches. A reader deciding how to spend a refit gets something substantive from the combination of these results. I would reject a submission selling this rule as a generally advantageous feature selector or a safety test.

**Presentation recommendation — revise the framing before submission.** The title asks about the “cost” of deletion and the abstract asks about predicting “the loss”. The primary outcome is a binary breach of a specified loss tolerance, and a later analysis prices errors in predicting that breach. State the binary question in the title or opening. Define the Gain-ranked anchor, the tail outside that anchor and the median mask replacement where they first appear on page 2. The anchor's 8% selection allowance is first explained on page 12, after the main experiments. Distinguish it early from the 3% tolerance for a deletion. Keep Figure 1 and the named fold-level examples on page 11.

### Conditions on the scientific claims

1. **Scope of masking.** The construction on page 4 is sound for a rule restricted to the aggregate masked scores given there. It does not rule out prediction using the training rows, a different replacement distribution, or a learned representation. The empirical sensitivity analysis varies restoration family, aggregation, quorum, panel count, tolerance and learner while retaining the original tree-derived candidates. There is a small attribution error in the abstract. Table 3's matched-learner comparison changes the voter and refitted target together. The archive allows a clean comparison. Holding the XGBoost voter fixed and replacing only the target changes added true/false veto counts from 11/20 to 8/23 on the paths and from 0/9 to 1/8 on the draws. With both learners logistic the counts are 2/17 and 2/29. Give the crossed comparison or describe the joint change. I would reject a claim that these experiments establish the failure of masking in general.

2. **What 97.4% and 91.9% measure.** I reconstructed the confusion counts from the original split records. The separating rule vetoed 184/189 observed failures and 194/211 observed passes. The drawn set gave 161/163 and 178/216. Seventy-five fold–support pairs occur in both. Only four tasks supplied the 11 added path detections, including seven from Sonar. The observed break-even ratio of 20/11 against endpoints rises to 19/4 without Sonar. The task-and-fold resampling interval extends to infinity, and whole-task resampling gives an upper endpoint of 19.00. The zero added detections on the draws gives a degenerate conditional bootstrap interval. These quantities answer the finite-sample decision question the text sets out. The repeated folds share training data, the proposals share fits and test outcomes, and the tasks were chosen for convenience. A nominal coverage guarantee for new projects or a portable break-even threshold would be grounds for rejection. A short indication of the 14 task clusters alongside the headline rates would reduce this risk. A second independent cohort is unnecessary for the descriptive result.

3. **Ordering and actual use.** Section 5.3's primary held-out log-loss differences cross zero in both sources. Same-width concordance is 0.582 on 97 path pairs and 0.573 on 137 drawn pairs, with the latter interval including 0.5. As an exploratory check, on these same eligible pairs I assigned greater risk to the larger sum of retained Gain rank positions (rank zero best). Averaging concordance over folds within task and then tasks gives 0.460 and 0.498. These values lend credibility to a limited ranking signal against a cheap identity-aware comparator. Section 5.4 tests raw minimum approval with Gain tie-breaking at fixed widths. It does not apply the learned probabilities across widths. Either state this change of decision rule or show what happens when the trained models nominate candidates directly. An archived cross-width experiment in `results/context_review/learned-guard/` makes the latter possible. It is retrospective, including on Optdigits after the initial test.

4. **Practical recommendation.** At one refit, five of 41 nominees and four final supports changed. On New Thyroid the vote obtained a qualifying two-column fit where the prefix fell back to three. The Ionosphere and SPECTF gains in apparent compression failed the tolerance. The net-benefit inequality on page 11 makes the observed trade-off explicit. Its weights are a choice for the user, and the estimate is driven by these few discordances. At three refits the breach count rises from two to six. The first Optdigits test used disjoint writers, saved predictions before scoring, and produced identical Gain-based supports at each budget. This supports treating a Gain prefix with a guard as a reasonable default for these settings. I would reject a broad deployment recommendation based on ranking improvement alone, an assertion of safety based on the guard, or a claim that a single new task proves general equivalence.

The synthetic pair-separation result is a valid test of a specific mechanism. The complete pair pattern was contradicted in every one of the 226 eligible empirical proposals. Its construction and proof can be shortened or placed later to make room for the decision evidence. I found no arithmetic defect in the primary operating-point comparison that would prevent publication of the narrower study.

## Second reading

I reviewed the 24-page `packages/operating-review-drafts/reading-2.pdf`, SHA-256 `24D7E93C721C46D91CA104A8A17489BD9ABA91E990F81D3F65B66ECFF99F4865`. I inspected the rendered title, main results, discussion and appendix layout and checked the revised results against the stored task records. I consulted no other review.

**Scientific recommendation — accept as an empirical characterization.** The revised study answers which proposals its rules flagged, when the additional warning had decision value, and what happened when an actual candidate had to be selected for refitting. The two target-learner comparisons on page 8 hold the voter fixed. Pages 6–10 distinguish proposal collection, task dependence, alternative rules and miss cost. The 20/11 crossover concerns one paired comparison on this archive. The discussion correctly limits the broader interpretation.

**Practical value.** I independently recalculated the thresholded decisions from the held-out probabilities. At double miss cost, adding dissent to the fitted size predictor saved 22 error-cost units on 379 drawn proposals and cost 20 extra units on 400 path proposals. Repeating the model fits after omitting each task reproduced the drawn-set savings of 1.14–6.41 units per 100 proposals. At equal cost the simple transferred size-cutoff policy outperformed both learned probability models on the draws. These are specific answers for a practitioner who can identify the type of proposal and put a price on the errors.

For the learned one-refit experiment, I rebuilt each nomination from the saved coefficients and candidate list, checked that training excluded its target task, applied the recorded guard decision and recomputed outer accuracy, AUC and tolerance breaches from prediction arrays and original test labels. For the drawn-trained model, votes changed 16 nominations and 15 final supports across 41 folds. Seven folds gained compression within tolerance and three lost it. Mean qualifying compression increased from 8.94% to 11.92%, with three additional breaches and a paired interval crossing zero. The path-trained comparison also reproduced. All four retrospective Optdigits policies selected the same 18 columns. The paper explicitly distinguishes these learned decisions from the first, prospectively fixed width comparison on the digit test. The practical conclusion warranted by both budgets is that the vote can buy compression on some folds, with additional breaches and uncertain average benefit. The results do not establish broadly favorable deployment economics.

**Presentation recommendation — accept with minor edits.** The title now names the breach outcome, and the introduction explains the anchor, tail, and median replacement before the equations. The abstract currently gives the favorable drawn-set error cost (156 to 134) at double miss cost without its path counterpart (190 to 210). Put the opposite directions alongside one another there, as the discussion already does, and call this *proposal error cost* so it cannot be confused with end-to-end runtime or guarded compression. The eight-percent tolerance used to construct the anchor still first appears on page 13. An earlier parenthesis would make that distinction easier. The final appendix page contains five lines continued from page 23. This is ordinary layout polish.

**Remaining scientific limits.** The meta-model and cross-width policy were developed after examining the benchmark, the guarded comparison uses one realization of the candidate pool per width and task split, and the only untouched digit result tested the fixed-width policy. Task-level intervals for the achieved compression are wide, and time for the fixed-width comparison is imprecise even after the standalone timing check. The paper could support a stronger policy recommendation with a locked cross-width decision on fresh tasks at a specified breach price, but that evidence is unnecessary for the present scope. I found no unresolved defect requiring rejection on this reading.

For the illustrative breach price of one twelfth of a unit of qualifying compression and zero computing cost, I recomputed the net benefit from paired fold outcomes. **Final recommendation — accept the scientific scope and presentation of this empirical study.** The added price example shows exactly how little evidence supports a positive economic gain even at a favorable assumed price. The cross-width policy remains exploratory and its performance on new tasks remains open. Page 24 still contains only a few lines of appendix text. This layout preference has no bearing on my recommendation. I found no remaining material objection.
