## Changes for the next text

1. **Qualify the historical payoff in the abstract.** On p. 1, lines 7–9, “fixed the relative return” can be read as identifying the deployed policy's guarded return. The historical pairs have no common recorded guard. Say that the outer queries fixed the *difference in potential compression conditional on both candidates being delivered*. On p. 4, lines 162–168, one short clause can explain that the 1,283 pairs overlap and the outer predictions are unavailable when choosing a candidate. Make “zero worst-case regret” at lines 174–176 explicitly relative to the pair (S,V). An admissible (010) world favors (T) in the three-way decision.
2. **State the denominator of the forest results.** The p. 7 heading and p. 8 Table 5 invite a reading conditional on an actual tie. The four primary contrasts average all folds, including unique winners and singleton-anchor fallbacks. On original wide Gain, +2.71 points across all 16 folds becomes +9.50 [−2.77, 23.17] under the appendix's visible-tie restriction and reweighting. Giving both estimands in the main text distinguishes opportunity to break a tie from performance when a tie occurs. “Order” selects the earliest *eligible* candidate in frozen slate order.
3. **Describe the sequence of evidence.** P. 6, lines 229–233, describes an outcome seal for the original forest comparisons. The new uniform, point and full comparisons used forest outcomes that had already been examined. Mark the same-tier analysis as retrospective at its first appearance. Use “locally prespecified” for the five-point target where the documentation identifies a versioned local protocol. Make clear that the labeled benchmarks were public and the seal records local scoring order.
4. **State the opportunity bound at the right decision point.** P. 1, lines 31–34, describes 2.78 points as the largest improvement. It is a conservative upper bound from delivered support disagreement. The candidates' common outer labels may further restrict attainable outcomes. The original design required the lower paired 95% endpoint to exceed five points with no rise in mean breaches. P. 8, lines 329–331, currently places savings and breach measurement before the pre-outcome calculation. The study can record both policies' guard-delivered supports, widths and weights, bound the largest gain, then score outer savings and breaches. A single-policy deployment would have access only to its own guard.
5. **Repair precise locations and scope.** P. 6, line 256, sends readers to Appendix D for the randomized extension. Equation (7) in D is deterministic. The randomized extension is in B.2, lines 672ff. Tables 3 and 4 float together on p. 7 and neither has a text reference. Refer to each in its results paragraph. P. 8, lines 318–323, should say random codes certified most decisions *at the original precision*. At eight bits they still admitted 454–482 witnessed reversals. Uniqueness of the continuous summaries was checked within fold and retained width, which the Discussion should retain.

The remaining empirical boundary is actionable. On the six wider boosted-tree tasks the eight-round rule had the highest observed savings and added 1.49 seconds over point masking across all 16 folds, with unchanged realized breaches. Its savings interval [−5.43, 11.06] permits both benefit and harm. Across the two masking-background draws even the sign of the full-minus-point difference changed. The reported evidence supports a point-mask comparator and a design check for new studies. It does not establish which rule will be best for the next task. The requested revisions above can be settled from the existing results.
