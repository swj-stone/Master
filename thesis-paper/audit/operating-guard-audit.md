# Independent audit of the guarded selection policies

I rebuilt the splits, feature sets, votes and XGBoost predictions for all 41 feasible PMLB folds and the separate official Optdigits split from the raw tables. I also rebuilt the downstream choices of the subsequently defined cross-width policy using its saved coefficients. The independent replay recovered every shortlisted and selected support, every guard decision, all outer breaches, and every saved probability array exactly. The PMLB primary comparison produced one beneficial compression, two extra outer breaches and a positive measured time increment. Optdigits produced identical final predictions with and without masked scoring.

## Independent reconstruction

Run `./thesis-python.ps1 thesis-paper/audit/operating-guard-audit.py --learned` in the thesis checkout. The script imports a prior independent audit's XGBoost constructor, compact Gain algorithm, AUC and hash readers. It imports no experiment, selector, panel or scoring implementation. It reconstructs the sample quotas, stratified bootstrap weights and constant-weight restoring masks. For each support it computes all four approvals for every restoration using direct weighted positive–negative concordance with half credit for ties. After taking the minima, it separately reconstructs the experimental early-stop mask counts. Guard and final probabilities come from freshly fitted 100-tree models with stage-specific median imputation. Each original probability matrix and every metric is checked against its archive entry.

The fixed-policy replay verifies 774 distinct fold–candidate supports, including 750 from PMLB. Since the half-width appears in both budgets, the archives contain 1,048 minimum scores across the two budgets. The replay checks 261 unique guard models and 101 unique final models, 378 policy–fold results, all task-weighted summary entries, paired estimates and 30,000-draw intervals. The per-policy records are in [operating-guard-audit.csv](operating-guard-audit.csv). The second replay verifies 3,096 learned scores and probabilities across four policy variants, 104 unique guard models, 73 final models and 168 policy–fold results. See [operating-guard-audit-learned.csv](operating-guard-audit-learned.csv).

## Separation and denominators

- PMLB comprises 14 fixed convenience tasks, three original stratified outer folds per task. Wine Quality Red fold 2 had a ranking class with a single example and could not supply both a proposal and a guard example for that class. The independent reconstruction identified exactly this ineligible fold. The remaining 41 cover all 14 tasks. SPECTF fold 0 stays in all denominators even though its one-column anchor offers no deletion. Five guards include only one member of their least frequent class. The smallest guard contains 18 examples.
- Each evaluated outer row ID is disjoint from every fitting, proposal and guard row ID for that fold. No candidate nomination or guard fit consumes an outer outcome. Stratification uses labels to construct partitions. Feature ranking and screening use fitting/proposal rows, candidate refits use fitting plus proposal rows, and the guard uses their disjoint complement within development. The final fit uses the entire development set, including guard rows. A guard can pass a candidate whose final fit subsequently breaches tolerance.
- Across the 41 PMLB folds, 213 of 9,392 guard rows have an exact full predictor vector present in the fitting plus proposal rows. Conversely, 895 of 37,488 outer rows have a predictor vector present in development. These occur on 22 and 25 folds, respectively. The row-level design permits repeated profiles and the source provides no general subject or group separation. Rechecking both primary policies' guard passes after excluding repeats preserved each pass/fail decision. Removing repeated outer profiles while holding the final fits fixed also preserved every primary breach and utility. Other budgets and tie-breaks did sometimes change guard decisions under this diagnostic. Matching exact predictors cannot identify every participant or near-duplicate.
- Optdigits contributes one separate 2,867/478/478 fitting/proposal/guard split of the official 3,823 training rows and 1,797 official test rows. The UCI archive's own description attributes the two files to 30 training writers and 13 different test writers. Neither guard nor outer samples repeat an earlier full predictor vector. The outcome counts examples. Source inspection confirms that the budgeted study first parses the official test-label column for scoring after writing its frozen selections and prediction matrices. Local hashes support reproducibility. They cannot establish the history of human access to the public test set or supply an external preregistration timestamp. The subsequent cross-width analysis used this already-opened holdout.

## What the fixed-budget comparison establishes

On PMLB, the primary policy nominated one candidate on 40 folds and none on the one-column fold. The three-width policy nominated 106 total across 41 folds. Each available width has one genuine candidate refit plus the shared anchor reference per fold. Both arms had identical widths, candidate identities and fit allowances. Each compared pair used the same tie order, and its equal-width limit prevented information from a voted width being donated to the baseline.

The minimum gave every candidate zero approvals on 32 of 40 actionable primary folds. Only eight offered any within-width discrimination. The vote changed five of 41 Gain nominations and four final identities. The changed nominations were Ionosphere 0, New Thyroid 1, Sonar 1, Sonar 2 and SPECTF 1. Sonar 2 reverted to its anchor under both nominations. On Sonar 1 both final ten-column alternatives passed, leaving equal utility. The two additional breaches were Ionosphere 0 and SPECTF 1. A strong relative rank can correspond to an absolute score of only one approval out of four, including on both of those folds. This nomination rule maximizes an ordering. It does not require the original 3-of-4 panel majority.

Under the one-refit Gain comparison, fallback counts were 32/41 for the prefix and 29/41 for voting. Both policies breached on Wine Quality Red 0. Voting additionally breached on the two folds above. The absolute counts are 1/41 and 3/41. Equal weighting of the 14 task means gives breach frequencies 0.035714 and 0.083333. Wine Quality Red has two eligible folds, while the other tasks each have three. The resulting mean utilities are 0.093264 and 0.101200 and their paired difference is 0.00793651, with a percentile interval of [0, 0.02380952]. The full positive contribution comes from New Thyroid 1. Leaving that task out sets the utility difference to zero. Resampling 14 observed tasks with replacement has probability $(13/14)^{14}=0.3543$ of omitting New Thyroid altogether.

For the secondary three-width Gain rule the vote changed 15 slates and 12 final identities. Its utility difference was 0.013946 [−0.014966, 0.047619], with six breaches versus two for the width/Gain baseline. The randomized tie-break at the primary budget changed seven slates and four final identities. Its utility difference was 0.011905 [0, 0.035714] and its breach counts were four versus five. One shared random draw per width supplies a conditional comparison and leaves candidate-pool and tie-order variability unmeasured.

## What the time and utility boundary price

Let one unit of demonstrated safe anchor compression be worth one, a breach cost an additional `h` units, and a computation second cost `c` units. Under the fixed primary comparison, the observed vote-minus-prefix net utility per task-averaged fold is

`0.00793651 − 0.04761905 h − 0.01013938 c`.

Thus the observed vote advantage requires `h < 1/6` for free computation, or `c < 0.78274` for zero breach cost, with equality giving a tie. The window narrows when both costs are positive. This is a tradeoff on these fitted folds with the stated scoring convention. A failed deployment and an anchor fallback both get zero compression credit, even when the failed candidate loses much more predictive performance. The separate breach cost charges every breach equally. The observed primary increase gives no strictly positive lower confidence limit even before costs. At the zero-time break-even breach price, resampling the 14 task contrasts gives a net-utility interval of [−0.01587, 0.01984].

Each budget's masked screening started with an empty cache. Its 491 primary and 1,349 three-width additional masked predictions on PMLB reproduce exactly. The task-equal mean primary vote-screening increment was 0.010299 s; the full charged component difference was 0.010139 s, since different nominees also have different guard/final fits. All archived component sums match. Shared model fits were measured once and charged to the policies that use them. Guard metric evaluation, first-passing selection logic, data loading and serialization fall outside these measured components. Fit timers were not independently randomized and repeated across policies. Independent replay checks allocation and outcomes, not the original stopwatch readings.

The matched experiment also charges the plain Gain arm for generating random alternatives that a dedicated prefix implementation could skip (task-averaged pool construction 0.000077 s primary, 0.000264 s three-width). It charges an anchor guard fit to its anchor-only comparison (mean 0.130101 s), even though an unguarded anchor can be deployed without fitting it twice. These costs cancel or are small for the policy contrast defined here. The table does not provide a minimum stand-alone runtime for simpler alternatives.

## Subsequent cross-width rule

I computed every individual failure probability and expected-compression score from the archived logistic coefficients and independently rebuilt the Gain-rank and column-ID tie-breaks across all widths. Each PMLB coefficient record lists exactly the other 13 tasks. Each Optdigits prediction averages 14 such fitted probabilities. This replay checks application of the stored models and their stated task boundaries. It does not independently refit those historical coefficients. Fresh stage-specific XGBoost fits recovered the guard and final predictions exactly.

| Historical proposal source | Changed widths / nominees / final supports | Safe-saving difference (95% interval) | Breaches, size → size with vote | Accepted deletions, size → size with vote |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Drawn deletions | 3 / 16 / 15 | +0.02979 [−0.01468, 0.07460] | 1 → 4 | 18 → 24 |
| Search paths | 4 / 15 / 13 | +0.02639 [−0.01646, 0.06944] | 1 → 3 | 15 → 19 |

Counts cover 41 folds and the estimates and intervals average 14 tasks equally. Drawn-source voting improved safe savings on seven folds and reduced them on three. The path estimates improved on six and fell on three. Mean outer accuracy declined under both source models. All four learned policies selected the same 18-column Optdigits support. The cross-width protocol was specified after reviewing fixed-budget results, so this round provides a retrospective sensitivity, including for Optdigits. With already-fitted coefficients, the task-mean incremental time was 0.00718 s [−0.03481, 0.03310] from the drawn source and 0.02699 s [0.01870, 0.03587] from the path source. These are marginal application costs. The historical labeling and logistic fits are excluded.

The predicted product of the deletion fraction and unguarded survival probability leaves out the probability that the proposed support passes the subsequent guard. A guarded deployment's saving requires both events. Historical training folds use an earlier proposal construction and the learned screening distribution shifts again when the highest-scoring support is selected. Drawn-source training deletion fractions range from 0.01538 to 0.5. Scoring the most compressed candidates extrapolates beyond this range, although no realized nomination here deleted more than half the anchor. Intervals hold learned predictions fixed across task resampling, and the models trained on overlapping groups of tasks. Their sign and coverage do not establish an out-of-sample advantage for buying votes.
