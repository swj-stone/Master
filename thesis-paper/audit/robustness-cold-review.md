## 1. What the exact set establishes

The construction at lines 84–106 and in Appendix A.2 is coherent. After fixing both models, the label, the allowable inputs and the observed measurements, each feasible score for a row has a witness. Independent choice of witnesses gives the group-count representation under the stated constraints. A fixed score histogram makes AUC a linear midrank statistic and permits an integer transportation calculation. The multiclass extension correctly keeps the class-score vectors together. The proof acknowledges both the unknown-anchor case and the additional constraint necessary when average precision is active. The numerical audit uses candidate and anchor scores under the same reassignment, an important distinction from comparison with fixed floors.

These results quantify uncertainty about a hidden input–label pairing with known fits. One could encounter this regime when independently stored feature records and labelled case-level measurements require linkage. The manuscript offers no such application or claim that the 704 permutations approximate typical changes between training and deployment. An evaluator who already holds the paired inputs, labels and both refits can score the models directly. An analyst yet to fit a candidate lacks one of the theorem's inputs. For an evaluation paper the conditional thought experiment stands on its own, provided it identifies what makes the uncertainty disappear.

## 2. Controls for the 704-proposal result

I used the archived fold-level labels, exact masked-output equivalence classes, full-output classes and both refitted probability matrices in `evidence/transcript_permutations/row-partitions.zip`. Only 17 empty-restoration cases require refinement to establish a decision. They come from five folds on four tasks, with 12 from two SPECTF folds. This dependence matters when interpreting the count.

- **Unmasked predictor alone.** Supplying only the unmasked full-predictor output for each labelled outer position certified an invariant decision on 704/704 proposals using the archived paired-gap calculation without limiting block size. Across 38,021 outer fold-rows, 35,619 (93.7%) had a full output unique within the fold. The median fraction unique across the 42 folds was 96.4% (range 58.3–100%). The masked comparison is unnecessary once this particular output is available on these folds.
- **Label-independent fingerprint.** I shuffled the unmasked output classes across rows within each fold, then added the shuffled class to the actual empty-restoration pair. The shuffle preserves all full-output class multiplicities and destroys the original association with rows. Among the 17 previously ambiguous cases, 1,695 of 1,700 case–repetition combinations had invariant decisions. Five had verified witnesses. Ninety-five of 100 repetitions eliminated all 17 ambiguities. The other 687 remain invariant by refinement. I generated a single shared permutation per fold and repetition using NumPy `default_rng(20261115 + 1000 * fold_index + repetition)`, with zero-based manifest order and repetitions 0–99. This control isolates the extent to which fine row resolution alone can explain the endpoint. It does not simulate the predictive properties of the true score or assign a probability to an admissible reassignment.
- **One exchanged pair.** I separately tested whether exchanging just two feature vectors bearing different observed labels could reverse passage. At development-only information, 191/704 reversed this way (93 of 402 observed passes and 98 of 302 failures, on eleven tasks). Under the empty-restoration pair, 10/17 still reversed this way (seven of ten observed passes and three of seven failures, on three tasks). For each class pair, the change in a metric gap under a swap is the difference of two rows' contributions to the fixed-histogram rank and accuracy objectives. Checking coordinate extremes or searching for a simultaneous passing pair exhausts the single swaps. I directly rescored every reported witness with scikit-learn. The 704 unrestricted reversals and 191 two-row reversals describe different amounts of permissible perturbation. Even two rows need not be close in covariate space.

**Most important revision.** Include the unmasked-only control, a matched-resolution identifier control, and an explicit bound on how many rows may change when presenting the 704 result. Specify the observer's access to labels, records, fits, and linkage. State which conclusions refer to a conditional, adversarial set and which refer to measurements collected at nomination time. These controls yield an informative empirical result without a claim about how often new-task prediction actually fails.

**What would change the vote.** For the evaluation track, the identification controls above, an explicit operational observer, and the short decision/risk interpretation would materially improve the paper. A negative result supported by a strong diagnostic is sufficient in principle. For a methods claim, a prospective test on independently generated candidate pools with genuine opportunities to disagree and a declared safety–compression objective would be needed, or a new tractable certificate producing a decision before paying for the refit. Additional pair counts on the present slates would leave the inferential unit unchanged.
