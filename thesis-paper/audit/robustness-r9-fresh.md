# Independent simulated review of the R9 manuscript

**Recommendation — weak accept as an evaluation study.** I would reject a claim of a generally preferable feature-selection algorithm. The contribution that clears my bar is a worked demonstration of how the information available to an evaluator can change the apparent correspondence between masking and retraining, followed by a separate test of what those measurements buy when only one nominee receives a guard fit.

I read the main study, the ensemble identification argument and supporting methods. The checks below use archived predictions, assignments and policy outcomes. They do not reproduce model training.

**Soundness.** Section 3 and Appendix A.2 specify what is observed in each experiment. Grouping by feasible score bins is sharp when rows may take their own witnesses, subject to any stated joint constraint. With the entire feature multiset fixed, candidate and anchor metrics move under the same assignment. I independently rescored all 721 archived opposing assignments, including the 17 that preserve both point queries. I also reconstructed the two metric gaps from midranks and class predictions and optimized each over whole output-equivalence classes. This certified all 704 unmasked decisions and the other 687 point-pair decisions. The distinction between a feasible joint witness and two separate coordinate maxima matters here.

**Novelty.** Masking and retraining already define different evaluations, and tree split geometry is an established optimization device. The added result is the joint metric characterization for this observation scheme, coupled with a telling empirical control. At four bits, genuine outputs left 338 witnessed reversals and nine unresolved cases, versus at least 648 witnessed reversals for each occupancy-matched code. At full precision, even a randomized code fixed 686–694 decisions. This gives a concrete reason to ask how precisely an evaluator can recover row identity before crediting an outer prediction with information about a deletion. **Decision evidence.** I recovered the forest full-minus-vote returns from fold records. They were +3.44 and +5.49 percentage points on the Gain slates and −2.22 and +1.31 on the local slates. As an additional check, I preserved every strict ordinal preference and used the full margin only among maximum-vote candidates. This recovered the unrestricted full rule’s guarded utility on every original forest fold. On the three local draws it changed just one nominee relative to unrestricted full and recovered both panel contrasts exactly. Thus the entire observed utility difference was attainable through tie-breaking. The 13 strict pair reversals in Section 4.2 did not have to contribute any of it. The ten-source original gain also came with a 6.67-point increase in breaches.

**Strongest remaining limitation.** The controlled identification experiment supplies both fitted models and a closed dictionary, neither of which transfers to predicting the outcome for an unseen support. The practical study measures two specified policies on small, purposive collections. The drop of 5.67 points between designs on ten tasks is driven largely by Dry Bean, whose within-source contrast shifted by 55.56 points. Anchor, deletion fraction, tail and candidate geometry change together, and both paired intervals include zero. There is consequently little evidence for a population-average interaction or a reliable selection advantage. The abstract and discussion keep the scientific claim within the observed designs. I see no remaining methodological blocker at that scope.
