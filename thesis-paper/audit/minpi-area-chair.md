# Independent methods review

**Editorial judgment.** The evidence supports a short empirical or algorithm-engineering paper on finding certificates in a specified ridge quota game. A negative certificate identifies the coalition, feature, split and threshold responsible for a downward crossing. The manuscript has yet to establish a predictive reason to pay for voting when choosing a feature set. For a selective general-methods track, the elementary formal results and the small number of changed supports limit the case for significance. I would consider the narrower paper on its own terms.

## Formal contribution and attribution

The coordinatewise completion formula in Proposition 2 is correct. Each coordinate partitions the cube into disjoint edges, so the unobserved endpoints maximizing any one coordinate can be assigned independently for that coordinate. I checked all 6,561 partial observations against their compatible completions among the 256 games on three features. The bound need not be jointly attainable for all coordinates or by a ridge game on a given design. The Schur-complement prediction update is classical. Binary search for a bichromatic hypercube edge from opposite endpoint values is explicit in Figure 1 of Chen and Waingarten. Here the increasing path fixes the direction of the crossing. The contribution is applying these facts to cache entries, query schedules and computational cost in the existing MinPI game. ## Conditions on interpretation

- Attribute the bisection primitive where it is introduced. Keep the Boolean-completion theorem and timing comparison confined to their stated oracle and implementation.
- Give the common boosted-tree guard result and cheap coefficient control alongside the ridge/Gain result. Only MinPI consults the reserved voting labels while forming its ranking. The historical folds compare fixed pipelines on reused sources. Madelon's strong-guard deficits to matched Gain were 0.024 AUC and 2.88 accuracy points.
- Keep the witness count distinct from out-of-sample utility. A claim that witness identities improve selection would require a new source-level comparison with a cheap coefficient selector and a matched deletion control that succeeds on its prespecified width and predictive criteria. The present panels already answer the narrower certificate and computational questions.
- For reproduction, record the hardware, number of clock repetitions and whether the stated time includes the width guard and final refit. The strong-guard runs have one fresh timing per pipeline and split. Source resampling describes these panels, with five classification sources and a single regression source in the original comparison.

I read the complete seven-page PDF with SHA-256 `41305905e61f41fe045ed7817dd9cd1102ffc696a4171bc748ddc8a2b671136a` and inspected its rendered pages. Section 3 attributes the search primitive. For Section 3's new single-drop reference calculation I verified the probability by enumerating every drop and uniform query set for path lengths 3–12. The repaired ISOLET and Madelon figures match their source records. The numerical and attribution conditions above are satisfied. I would support submission as a narrowly scoped empirical methods paper. The data support a reason to compute witnesses when the certificate itself is valuable. Predictive superiority over a cheap selector and reliable recovery of all harmful features remain open.

## Prospective six-source deletion test

I compared the frozen protocol with commit `54cb7c6` and the archived opportunities and receipt lock with `c0b3667`. All 30 archived selection receipts and their forecast digests matched. Across the fitting, voting, width-selection and outer partitions I found zero shared groups or predictor vectors. Musk held out whole molecules, Hardware held out vendors, and the Estate test contained the last three months in all five runs. Its five decisions repeat one chronological split. The other source definitions and grouping agree with the protocol. The three classification sources and three regression sources are the units for domain comparisons.

I repeated the voting schedule with direct ridge solves for individual coalition models and recovered all 117 witnessed feature–split pairs. Of these, 84 were inside the coefficient prefix, including one that would have removed the entire prefix. I reproduced the 30 starting prefixes, 36 optional decisions and 150 held-out forecasts (largest absolute prediction difference 9.1 × 10⁻¹³). All outcome metrics, including Musk molecule scores and Forest Fires scores in hectares, agreed within 7.2 × 10⁻¹⁵. The paired point estimates and descriptive resampling intervals also reproduced.

The pre-outcome upper bounds on additional compression were 8.44% in classification and 12.67% in regression. All classification opportunities came from Musk. The witness rule accepted two Musk proposals and two Hardware proposals, with four acceptances across 12 eligible runs. The tail control accepted seven and uniform deletion accepted six. Actual width savings relative to the coefficient selector were 3.55% and 5.93%, at selection-time ratios of 1.31 and 1.64. Point predictive changes relative to that selector were within the declared margins. Guarded witness deletion retained as many or more columns than either matched control in both domains. Classification accuracy relative to *full ridge* fell by 1.67 percentage points. A single random proposal per split limits inference about the distribution of possible random deletions.

The fresh panel answers the requested prospective question for this frozen policy. The 10% target failed in both domains, and there is no observed incremental compression advantage from targeting witness identities. The evidence strengthens the narrow empirical case for studying certificate discovery and its limits. It leaves the earlier scientific sign-off in place.
