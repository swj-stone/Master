# Independent methodological review of the operating decision

## Criteria fixed before inspecting new analyses

I read the 18-page PDF with SHA-256 `515dfa9890c04bf28c0f97637e2488ca65ccf26488a4dece57530c299db1728b` on 15 November 2026. The following criteria were fixed from the original PDF, before opening any new analysis outputs, revised draft or other review. I assess the evidence for three different claims.

1. **Scope of the voting result.** To generalize past the published 3-of-4, every-restoration-must-pass rule, compare genuinely different panel thresholds and genuinely different ways of combining restorations. Use the same proposals, panels, masks and independently computed refit labels, and name the particular rules covered. Include a more permissive rule as well as a stricter one, report both proposal sources separately, and identify disconnected or duplicate operating points when relevant. A pooled vote over panels and restorations changes two things at once. A claim about selecting actual supports using these other rules needs new searches or an explicitly conditional statement about the fixed proposals. None of these comparisons supports an assertion about arbitrary masking, weighting, predictors, or datasets.

2. **Error rates and costs.** The positive label is a refit exceeding the outer tolerance. Give TP/(TP+FN) and FP/(FP+TN), with denominators and clearly named weighting, for each source. Uncertainty must preserve all proposals on the same fitted split and the clustering of folds within task, with paired resampling for rule contrasts. Specify the unit, interval and treatment of zero denominators. Three cross-validation folds overlap in training rows and the 14 convenience tasks are a small top-level sample. The 75 shared proposals prevent interpreting the sources as independent replication. Overlapping marginal intervals are not a test of the difference. If a veto prevents a missed loss at cost of an extra false alarm, the loss is proportional to FP + r·FN, where r is the missed-loss/false-alarm cost ratio. State pairwise and overall break-even regions, ties and empty regions, including always-veto and never-veto when relevant and a deletion-fraction-only comparison. Keep the proposal distribution fixed or explain how the intended prevalence changes the boundary. Do not imply a ratio is justified in practice without supplied costs. Do not claim a robust break-even when paired uncertainty allows zero extra detections. Interpret proposal decision loss separately from final selector utility and runtime.

3. **When a user should pay for votes.** Show at least one concrete, reproducible decision that a fair, feasible deletion-fraction/prefix plus independent refit guard could have made with the same information, fit budget or recorded incremental costs. Compare identical eligible cases, specify prospective choices or tag retrospective choices, provide both benefits and harms, and report reversion to the anchor where applicable. A favorable task, selected after examining outcomes, can illustrate a decision but cannot validate a policy. A vote beating a same-width prefix supplied with the vote's own width, or an advantage over endpoints alone, does not establish a benefit over the available deployable simpler policy. A practical superiority conclusion needs an explicit decision criterion and sign-consistent evidence at its stated scope. A fully documented null or adverse result with withdrawal of that conclusion answers the assessment honestly.

## Original PDF findings

- Section 3 defines an uncalibrated ballot. Section 3.1's refit non-identifiability construction is valid for decisions restricted to its mask metrics. It does not establish usual performance of all masked predictors. Section 5, Table 2 already includes point and pooled rules and Section 6, Table 4 includes nested pooled search. There is no systematic matched sensitivity to the within-context panel threshold and between-context veto aggregation. The title poses a broader question than the fixed rule and learner can settle. Explicit delimitation or additional matched operating rules is needed.
- Section 5, pp. 6–8 gives proposal counts and a correct pairwise 20/11 break-even against endpoints. Seven of eleven additional true vetoes come from one task. Endpoint-versus-vote costs are insufficient to recommend the vote: on the 400 path proposals, beating always-veto also requires r < 17/5, and the pooled rule changes the tradeoff. On drawn proposals the endpoints weakly dominate the separating minimum. Task averages of true and false veto fractions on p. 7 are joint event frequencies, not conditional sensitivity and false-positive rates. No cluster interval is given for either rate.
- Section 5.1, Table 3 provides a deployable half-prefix comparator. At equal 1/41 outer exceedances it has 0.9351 versus 0.9333 AUC, 82.42% versus 82.23% accuracy and 42.9% versus 43.3% retained, favoring the simpler method on each displayed average. Table 3 does not identify discordant decisions. Width-fixed comparisons on pp. 11–13 show that identity can matter and do not establish incremental utility above a guarded independent width. Gas is adverse and the apparent HAR advantage over a Gain prefix nearly disappears against RFE on just the original 15 fitting participants. The original data support a negative practical conclusion at these settings. Any positive replacement needs an actual comparable decision and costs.

## Separate checks of the archived predictions and ballots

I recalculated AUC, accuracy and any applicable average precision from the probability matrices and recovered all 704 candidate labels and original endpoint, pooled and separating-minimum decisions on every one of the 779 source rows. I cross-checked the 41 guard partitions, recalculated their choices and outer outcomes from the probability matrices and checked that their test row IDs agree. The following analyses are retrospective.

- For matched ballots, requiring four approvals within a restoration and vetoing only when more than a quarter of restorations fail gives path FP/FN of 190/2 and drawn FP/FN of 172/2. The original gives 194/5 and 178/2. This counterexample to default optimality applies at every positive ratio in both samples. It was discovered in these data and is insufficient to recommend the alternative on new tasks.
- Allowing thresholds at every observed deletion fraction, there is a width-only rule on paths (fraction at least 1/6, 173 FP, four FN) and on drawn deletions (at least 1/12, 162 FP, two FN) that achieves an error count no higher than the original vote in either coordinate and strictly lower FP. The thresholds were selected retrospectively within each source. In the wider comparison of width cutoffs, constants, pooled, endpoints, and sixteen panel/context quorum combinations, the observed lower envelope consists entirely of width cutoffs and never-veto. This envelope uses the evaluation labels for threshold selection.
- The two guarded selectors returned identical supports on 20/41 folds. The vote kept fewer columns in 12 and more in nine. Both exceeded the outer tolerance on one fold, but these were different folds. On Wine Quality Red fold 0 the vote retained eight columns and passed (AUC 0.82524, accuracy 63.79%); half-prefix retained four and failed (0.83461, 61.73%). On Yeast fold 0 the vote retained four and failed (0.80386, 56.19%); half-prefix retained five and passed (0.83060, 56.19%). These examples were located after outcomes were known. The vote recorded 3,462 extra mask checks across all 41 folds (median 31, range 0–482). Each strategy requires at most two inner guard fits when run alone. An unpriced additional mask check cannot establish a monetary or elapsed-time saving.

## Independent judgment on the revised PDF

I read the 24-page `packages/operating-review-drafts/reading-3.pdf` directly and verified its SHA-256 as `4a0ca3fd689e6d010554505ae0e8cb32f3b015b74bf0a157994847eb2c9699a8`. I applied the criteria fixed above. I did not consult another review in reaching these judgments.

1. **Operating-rule scope is addressed.** Pages 7–9 use matched proposals, panels and restorations to distinguish a minimum from pooling, relax and tighten panel requirements, vary the number of reweighted panels and vary vote-side tolerances at a fixed outer target. The logistic comparison holds the tree-derived proposals and restoration families fixed and explicitly says so. I reconstructed all 360 decisions for each learner on every one of the 704 supports from the per-mask metric arrays. Every decision matched the archived results. Independently rescoring both learners' outer probabilities recovered the reported 111 and 82 changed labels. The revised discussion describes effects on this collection and the two learners. Neither general failure of masking nor general optimality of the original minimum is needed for the stated result. The independent quarter-of-restorations example above remains a useful illustration of the open design space, not an additional prerequisite to the scoped paper.
2. **Uncertainty and cost accounting are addressed.** Table 2 gives the denominators, conditional rates and intervals. Pages 7–10 give paired rate differences, hierarchical and intact-task sensitivity, the infinite upper endpoint of the uncertain path crossover, independently sized and constant alternatives, prevalence dependence and separate sources. The 30,000 released draws have both class denominators for every reported primary rate; the implementation excludes undefined rates if they occur. I used a different seed and separately resampled 14 tasks and three complete folds within each selected task 30,000 times. The path four-panel minimum intervals were 92.4–100.0% for sensitivity and 85.4–96.2% for false-positive rate. My paired path intervals for the increases were 0–13.6 and 4.5–15.2 percentage points. They agree with the manuscript within Monte Carlo variation. On the drawn source the paired sensitivity increase remained zero in every draw. I recomputed the highlighted out-of-task posterior costs directly from the 779 stored probabilities and archived outcomes: 190 versus 210 on paths and 156 versus 134 on draws at a miss-to-false-alarm cost of two. Page 11 distinguishes the posterior size-model comparison from the separate task-selected simple cutoff. Page 9's restricted-library optima do not imply a globally optimal vote, a known real-world cost ratio, or coverage for new fitted models.
3. **An actual decision is demonstrated; general practical superiority remains unsupported.** I reconstructed both one- and three-candidate guarded selections from the disjoint guard prediction arrays and evaluated the selected outer probabilities on all 41 eligible folds and Optdigits. At equal one-candidate budget, New Thyroid fold 1 is a bona fide empirical win over an independently chosen half-width prefix. The two-column vote support passed (0.99816 AUC, 97.22% accuracy); the rejected prefix reverted to the three-column anchor (0.99428, 95.83%). This one fold explains the entire 0.79-point increase in compression within tolerance. The additional breaches on Ionosphere fold 0 and SPECTF fold 1, and the identical 24-column Optdigits one-refit result, also reproduced. Across all folds the one-candidate breach counts were one versus three; at the three-candidate budget they were two versus six. The resulting task-weighted net-benefit inequality on p. 12 accounts for computation, compression and the separate price of a breach. Its illustrative positive estimate changes sign on omitting New Thyroid, as the manuscript discloses. The cross-width rule is separately labeled exploratory and again gains compression alongside additional breaches. I would reject a transferable deployment-superiority claim on this evidence. The submitted reading makes the narrower claim about a real favorable decision and its observed trade-offs.

**Disposition.** The revised manuscript answers all three methodological demands at the scope it claims. I found no remaining fatal issue for that scope. The 14 reused tasks, fixed held-out predictions, overlap among the training folds and lack of supplied operating costs preclude stronger population or deployment conclusions. Optdigits demonstrates prospective agreement in one external task. It does not independently reproduce a benefit of voting.
