## Identification and practical relevance

Equation (3) correctly moves the candidate and benchmark together in the finite-data audit. Fixing both empirical score histograms makes each metric gap an additive assignment objective. The attainable set for a fixed labelled dictionary and the expected failure rate in a deployment population are materially different quantities, a distinction now explicit at lines 129–136 and 170–178.
- **One exchange.** Table 1 reproduces my earlier independent checks. With unrestricted assignments, 704 proposals have both decisions when development information alone is available. Only 191 have an opposing decision after one exchange of two rows carrying different observed labels. Two genuine point queries reduce those counts to 17 and ten. The evidence directory additionally contains 200,702 reversals among 602,729,629 allowed proposal-specific exchanges for development-only and 60 among 7,774 exchanges after the point pair. Those fractions weight large folds heavily. They answer a hypothetical uniform-pair question within the stated dictionary, with no estimate of deployment likelihood.
- **Precision and fingerprint.** The random control is a function of the raw feature vector. It reassigns genuine codes among feature-vector types with the same multiplicity, preserves every code occupancy, gives identical records the same output, uses the same permutation across resolutions, and avoids labels and refit scores. This is stronger than assigning an unconstrained random key to individual rows. At four bits, genuine-code ambiguity is bounded between 338 and 347 of the 704 supports. Every one of the five random-code draws has at least 648 witnessed reversals. Using the proposal-level dispositions, I found 12 of 14 tasks on which even the **minimum** randomized witness count exceeds the genuine witness count **plus every unresolved genuine case**. At two and eight bits the corresponding counts are 9/14 and 11/14. Full precision leaves few movable labels in either arm. The four-bit contrast is a substantial result about the recorded feature–label arrangement. With the true point pair already provided, a coarse random code can distinguish more remaining assignments than the corresponding real code; complementary partitions can be more informative than two similar ones.
- **Remaining limitation.** The observer knows the feature dictionary, labelled positions, fitted models and code mapping, while the linkage remains hidden. The audit does not show how often the same key matches a fresh feature vector or whether an analyst deciding which unfitted candidate to train could recover the true tolerance decision. The abstract and discussion largely respect this boundary. The archive lookup result and the choice-policy experiment use different observables. A practical interpretation of the finite dictionary is linkage validation between two releases of measured records.

## Pooling and recommended editorial changes

1. **Prefer a paired comparison between pipelines over two stand-alone averages in the abstract.** For each source I calculated the difference between its local-pipeline full-minus-vote utility and its original-pipeline full-minus-vote utility, averaging the three local draws within fold, then folds within task. The mean interaction is −5.67 percentage points on ten sources (paired task-bootstrap 95% interval [−18.57, +3.59]) and −4.18 on six wider sources ([−13.82, +5.39]). Four sources in each panel account for all nonzero interactions. On the first panel, Dry Bean contributes −55.56 points and Steel +17.94. On the wider panel, Semeion contributes −22.22 and Urban +16.22; Urban contributes one official split. Thus the paper demonstrates specific reversals with genuine refits, while a material mean population interaction is unresolved. Qualify “materially affects” at lines 15–16 accordingly.
2. **State the aggregation at its point of use.** Lines 201–203 say that every table averages folds within task and weights tasks equally. Tables 1 and 2 count proposals directly. Identify these as fixed archive counts and include the four-bit across-source sensitivity. Equal-task resampling in Tables 3–5 is appropriate for the disclosed estimand. Five fingerprint draws, three candidate draws, multiple classifiers and repeated folds are conditional comparisons, not additional independent data sources.
3. **Make the observer and cost visible.** In Section 4.3, “restorations ... unchanged” is precise only for the construction rule. Different anchors generate different tails and actual restored feature sets. When comparing resources, distinguish a count of distinct masked predictions from end-to-end wall time. The current text reports mask counts accurately. One sentence pairing identical original forest point/full utility with the two differing breach outcomes would clarify the difference between an unsafe returned deletion and an anchor fallback, both assigned zero utility by Eq. (4).

These are bounded revisions. I would retain the evaluation-track vote with the headline narrowed to the observed pipelines. A methods-track change in vote would require a feasible pre-refit decision procedure and a prospective advantage on defensible safety and utility outcomes. More resolutions of the same mask experiment alone would not address that gap.
