\paragraph{Exact stopping under a fixed tie order.}
Let $v_j$ and $m_j$ denote the minimum vote and margin after the first $j$ restorations of a candidate. Every completion satisfies $v_{\rm final}\le v_j$ and $m_{\rm final}\le m_j$. The pair $(v_j,m_j)$ is an upper bound in the lexicographic order for the refined vote. If it cannot strictly beat the current winner and that winner precedes this candidate in the final tie order, processing further restorations cannot select this candidate. For a visit order different from final tie priority, include that priority as the last, fixed comparison key before stopping on equality. The scalar versions follow by applying the same argument to the relevant single minimum. The Lean proof in \texttt{formal/Challenger.lean} uses increasing ranks of the finitely many margin values, with tied values assigned the same rank. The online comparison itself uses the measured values directly.

For the matched timing run we fitted one 100-tree forest per fold with one worker and timed seven independent nominations with fresh caches. They comprise point, exhaustive vote, margin and refined vote, and the three corresponding stopped implementations. Each slate was timed three times per method, permuting method order by a fixed pseudorandom schedule within repetition. The timer includes constructing the ballot, hiding columns, predicting on the proposal rows, computing panel metrics and comparing candidates. Data preparation, feature ranking, the shared forest fit, guard and delivered-model refits are excluded. Reported times sum the within-slate medians across folds on the same machine. Earlier whole-pipeline times refer to the original implementation and include its training and setup costs.

\subsection{Changing the refitted classifier}
\label{sec:receiver}
The split memberships, label encoding, tree-nominated supports, rare-class flags and all decision tolerances were inherited from the two external tree experiments. We fitted a logistic model separately to every anchor and candidate, first on fitting plus proposal rows for the guard and then on all development rows for the outer comparison. Each fit used its training-column medians for imputation and its own training means and standard deviations for scaling, followed by unweighted L2 regression with $C=1$, a free intercept, multinomial loss for multiclass tasks, L-BFGS tolerance $10^{-6}$ and a 2,000-iteration cap. All 524 fits converged. The 46 sets of logistic predictions were sealed before their outer scores were computed. An independent implementation replayed every fit, obtaining maximum absolute differences of .00109 in probabilities and .000025 in metrics. It reproduced every candidate's guard and outer decision, and all 230 policy--fold results. The study changes the downstream learner for tree-selected candidates. Logistic regression never ranks the original features.

For random nominations the return-bound calculation also admits a direct extension of Eq.~\eqref{eq:opportunity-bound}. Let $p_{LfS},p_{RfS}$ be the probabilities that the respective policies return support $S$ after the guard. Its coefficient in the expected difference is $c_{fS}=w_f s_{fS}(p_{LfS}-p_{RfS})$. Summing $\min(0,c_{fS})$ and $\max(0,c_{fS})$ over supports and folds bounds that difference before outer scoring, since the same returned fit has the same passage bit for both policies. Lean verifies the deterministic and randomized bounds for rational coefficients, using nonnegative weights and savings in the deterministic case.

\subsection{Averaging predictions over training backgrounds}
\label{sec:masking-background}
Each masked input retains its observed coordinates and takes all remaining coordinates jointly from the same donor. After fitting-sample imputation, we drew 16 donor rows without replacement, shared these rows across all candidates, restorations, proposal rows and the matched anchor, and averaged their class probabilities before calculating any panel score. We repeated the entire calculation with an independently seeded second draw, retaining both. We adopt the marginal removal convention discussed by Covert et al.~\cite{covert2020sage,covert2021removing}. Donor rows are drawn independently of the visible coordinates. We evaluate the resulting predictions directly.

For the first panel, the XGBoost-refit all-context/point $U$ percentages were $6.18/4.85$ on draw A and $6.18/6.18$ on draw B. The corresponding logistic results were $4.67/3.33$ and $4.67/4.67$. For the wider panel, the XGBoost pairs were $10.07/4.63$ and $4.57/10.07$, and the logistic pairs were $10.78/5.50$ and $19.16/19.16$. Candidate pools and refit outcomes were fixed between draws. With two donor samples of this size the data describe sensitivity to those particular replacements.

\paragraph{Predicting a breach on an omitted PMLB task.}
For deletion fraction $x$, the width model used $x$ and positive parts of $x-c$ for $c=1/4,1/2,3/4$. The identity-aware model added the fraction of the anchor's total split gain removed and the fraction of linear rank points removed, assigning $k,\ldots,1$ points in descending order of mean split gain. The endpoint model added three continuous features. For each panel and restoration it used the largest $(R_{bi}-V_{bi}-\tau_i|R_{bi}|)/\tau_i$ over metrics. It retained the original-panel empty-restoration value and the mean and maximum over all four panels at the distinct empty/full endpoints. An alternative included the mean and maximum over all separating restorations. These historical risk predictors measure excess in nominal metric-tolerance units. The decision rules in Sect.~\ref{sec:transport} normalize by the metric-specific absolute allowance. All predictors use the same fold- and task-weighted standardization, unpenalized intercept and ridge penalty of .01 on the slopes. Probability-score intervals use 50,000 whole-task paired bootstrap draws, conditional on cross-validated fits. An exact-width, in-range comparison uses the source collection only to define the range of removed total-gain mass at that width in the same fold. Reported intervals are pointwise and comparisons share training data and test records.

\paragraph{Supplementary studies.}
The following appendices describe the earlier context and panel tests, decision costs, different learners, feature widths and deployment settings. They retain the experiments' original sampling units and metrics. Methods used as precursors to the present tests include group votes, worst-case selection over contexts and a deletion search with block subdivision~\cite{shi2026minpi,zeller2002ddmin}. Other coalition methods characterize redundancy or sample graph-dependent feature contributions~\cite{zheng2026minshap,zhao2025shapg}. Their observational unit differs from a tolerance decision on a reduced fit. The archive contains protocols, fold and support manifests, predictions or reconstruction scripts, checksums and a record of AI assistance with implementation, writing and internal review.
