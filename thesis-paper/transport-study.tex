\section{Does voting improve the decision that receives a refit?}
\label{sec:transport}
In the Gain experiment the fixed slate contains the prefix of the best $w=\lceil |A|/2\rceil$ features and up to seven further distinct uniformly drawn $w$-column subsets. On a singleton anchor every policy returns the anchor. Each policy proposes one support. We fit it and the anchor on the fitting and proposal rows and compare them on separate guard rows. A rejected nominee returns the anchor. We retrain the delivered support $S_f$ on all development rows and measure
\begin{equation}
 U=(1-|S_f|/|A|)\,\mathbf 1\{\text{delivered refit meets every outer tolerance}\}.
 \label{eq:utility}
\end{equation}
A fallback has zero savings. A delivered deletion that fails outside the guard also has zero $U$ and counts as a breach. We separately fitted every candidate at both stages, permitting exact expectations for randomized nominations and a hindsight upper bound for these slates.

\paragraph{Sources and timing of comparisons.}
Ten numeric or ordinal UCI classification tasks with 4--41 predictors contributed 30 stratified outer folds. Within each development split we used 75\% for fitting, 12.5\% for proposal scoring and 12.5\% for the guard. Six separate sources with 72--856 predictors contributed 16 folds, including chronological splits for Ozone and SECOM, participant aggregates for Parkinson and the official Urban split. There were 106 and 110 deletion candidates on 20 and 15 actionable folds. We retained all 46 folds in policy estimates. Original boosted-tree nominations and predictions were recorded before scoring each panel. The later comparisons within Gain shortlists reused these outcomes. The forest sensitivity likewise uses the same sources and slates. We fixed its settings before fitting and sealed its new predictions and nominations before scoring the forest outcomes. Policy tables average folds within task and then weight tasks equally. Tables~\ref{tab:observer-controls} and~\ref{tab:precision} instead count proposals in the fixed historical archive. Intervals resample whole tasks in pairs, 30,000 times, conditional on the recorded folds and fits. The two purposive collections have ten and six task units. Appendix~\ref{sec:transport-details} gives source identifiers, split details, overlaps and per-task results.

\paragraph{What to read from each masked fit.}
For metric $i$, let $h_{bi}=\tau_i|R_{bi}|$. The normalized slack on the classification metrics is $(V_{bi}-R_{bi}+h_{bi}+\epsilon)/\max(h_{bi},\epsilon)$. On each panel, take the minimum slack across metrics. The \emph{point margin} uses only the original panel at the empty restoration. The \emph{full margin} takes the third-largest of four panel margins at each restoration, then the smallest of those values across restorations. A nonnegative full margin is exactly equivalent to a three-of-four approval at every restoration. The ordinal rule maximizes $v(S)$ from Section~\ref{sec:measurements}. Restorations can be evaluated sequentially for this rule. Once its running minimum cannot improve the incumbent, remaining masks cannot make a candidate win. Ties follow the specified secondary rule. Unless noted otherwise the deterministic final tie order is increasing sum of within-anchor feature ranks, then sorted feature indices.

\subsection{How much does the vote add after Gain?}
Literal Gain selection has a unique winner on every actionable fold. For ordered within-anchor ranks $r_1<\cdots<r_w$, each $r_i\ge i$, so only the feature prefix minimizes $\sum_i r_i$. A second check using the sum of the actual unrounded mean training split gains also found a unique best support on all 35 actionable slates. Uniform and point tie-breaking therefore make precisely the same strict Gain decisions, yielding 0.00\% and 7.34\% $U$ with XGBoost refits on the two panels, and 2.59\% and 16.55\% with logistic refits.

To allow a secondary choice, we declared a broader Gain group before calculating the new contrasts. Retain the best $\lceil m/2\rceil$ of the $m$ candidate \emph{sets} by Gain rank sum. On this identical shortlist, choose uniformly or choose the largest point margin. Repeat each choice after restricting it to the candidates with maximum minimum vote. This design isolates the contribution of the vote for each given tie rule. Every candidate in every group has the same feature width. Uniform values are exact expectations over the realized guarded outcomes.

Only 16 of 30 first-panel folds and 15 of 16 wider folds had more than one candidate after the half-slate restriction. With point selection, the vote changed one first-panel nominee, on Seeds. Both receiving learners gained 1.33 percentage points of $U$ (paired 95\% interval $[0,4.00]$). The breach rate was unchanged for XGBoost and fell by 3.33 points for logistic refits. On the wider panel voting left every nominee unchanged. Across its 16 folds, point selection required 71 distinct masks and adding the vote required 168. Uniform selection gives the vote more opportunity to alter the decision. It added 0.67 points to $U$ on the first panel for each receiver ($[0,2.00]$). On the wider panel XGBoost gained 2.73 points entirely on Ozone ($[0,8.19]$), and logistic gained 1.36 ($[-2.08,6.15]$) with an offsetting loss on SECOM. Restricting the Gain group to the top quarter left vote--point gains at zero throughout. Thus a contribution exists for particular shortlists and sources, but the extra computation rarely altered a deterministic point choice.

\subsection{Does the cost of thresholding depend on the learner?}
We kept the boosted-tree Gain anchor and the 216 candidate supports on the original slates fixed and varied the learner used for masking and the learner used for the guard and final refits independently. A random forest here uses 100 bootstrap Gini trees, square-root feature subsampling and one fixed seed per fold~\cite{breiman2001random}. The design includes both ten-task and six-task results for logistic and forest receivers. The primary new comparison uses a forest at both stages and contrasts the full continuous margin with the ordinal minimum on the identical restoration family.

For the forest voter, the continuous margin distinguished 265 of 274 integer-tied within-fold candidate pairs on the first panel and 274 of 278 on the wider panel. On the wider panel it also reversed 13 strictly ordered pairs. Agreement at the approval threshold holds for every candidate. The two scores can still order candidates differently on the same side of that threshold.

With a forest at both stages the full margin gained 3.44 points over the vote on the ten tasks ($[-4.67,15.00]$). Dry Bean gained 50.00 points within task, Steel lost 15.56 and eight tasks tied. Breaches rose from 3.33\% to 10.00\%. On the wider panel the gain was 5.49 points ($[0,11.05]$), from Ozone and Semeion, with a 16.67\% breach rate for each rule. On folds with both outer-passing and failing candidates, forest/full concordance against forest/vote was .678 versus .543 on the first panel (113 pairs, ten mixed folds, five tasks) and .728 versus .543 on the wider panel (128 pairs, eleven mixed folds, five tasks). The paired differences were .134 ($[-.061,.319]$) and .185 ($[.086,.284]$). Each passing--failing pair contributes one for correct order and half for a tie, averaged within mixed folds and tasks. All 46 folds contribute to $U$.

For this same forest/forest comparison, point selection attained the full margin's $U$ on \emph{every} fold at the primary tolerance. A fallback and a breached deletion both yield zero $U$. On Seeds and QSAR the two rules had different breach outcomes in opposite directions, despite identical overall breach rates. Point used 126 versus 760 distinct nomination masks on the ten tasks and 125 versus 1,334 on the six wider tasks. Nomination took 0.47 versus 2.75 seconds across the first panel and 0.47 versus 5.27 seconds across the second on one thread, excluding the shared model fit, anchor screening and guard. Relative to an unrestricted uniform nomination, the full margin gained 1.54 ($[-2.67,6.54]$) and 5.56 points ($[-3.05,13.83]$). Keeping the forest voter but switching the wider receiver to logistic gave the full margin and ordinal vote identical $U$. With a boosted-tree voter and the same logistic receiver the ordinal vote led by 8.33 points. Thresholding demonstrably coarsens the observed panel losses. Whether the coarsening matters to refit selection depends on the learner and the slate.

\subsection{Changing the candidate construction with the forest held fixed}
The first forest experiment changes the models, while retaining slates generated from boosted-tree Gain. We next used ANOVA $F$ scores estimated on the fitting partition to select the top half of the input features as a new anchor. The anchor had 2--21 features on the first panel and 36--428 on the wider panel. We removed $\max(1,\lfloor |A|/4\rfloor)$ columns. Each slate contained the $F$ prefix at that retained width and up to seven uniformly sampled neighbors, each exchanging a single retained column for another anchor column. The voter, guard and outer learner all used the same forest specification as before. We used the same folds, median replacement values, tolerances and rule for constructing restorations. The membership of each mask could change. Here the tail comprises the columns below the ANOVA anchor in $F$-score order. In the original design it comprises the split-used columns below the Gain anchor in boosted-tree order. The $F$-score anchor and local neighborhood together change the selection problem. The denominator in $U$ is now this new anchor's width.

We locked the first exchange draw before scoring its new fits. It contributed 138 candidate records on the ten tasks and 128 on the six wider tasks. We later fixed two further draw seeds and sealed their nominations before scoring, giving 565 distinct fold--support records in total. Averaging over draws within each fold gives results conditional on the same partitions and outcomes. The mean pairwise Jaccard overlap of candidate sets across draws was .775 on the first panel and .071 on the wider panel. The candidate slates were identical in 63 of 90 and zero of 48 within-fold draw pairs, respectively.

\paragraph{Could the original experiment have detected its target effect?}
After the guard, two deterministic policies delivering the same support obtain the same outer return even before the labels are examined. For distinct delivered supports with removal fractions $d_L,d_R$ and fold weight $w_f$, their contribution to $U_L-U_R$ lies in $[-w_fd_R,w_fd_L]$. Summing over discordant folds gives an observable range (Appendix~\ref{sec:extended-operating}). On the wider boosted-tree panel, the full and point policies disagreed after the guard only on SECOM fold 0, where each saved half of its anchor and $w_f=1/18$. Their mean difference could therefore never exceed 2.78 points in either direction on these splits. The registered five-point improvement was already impossible before outer scoring. Both supports subsequently breached despite an AUC difference of .163 on that fold. Measuring all candidates gave a 21.05\% hindsight ceiling for mean $U$ on the wider panel despite the two policies realizing 7.34\%.
