\section{Does voting improve the decision that receives a refit?}
\label{sec:transport}
In the Gain experiment the fixed slate contains the prefix of the best $w=\lceil |A|/2\rceil$ features and up to seven further distinct uniformly drawn $w$-column subsets. On a singleton anchor every policy returns the anchor. Each policy proposes one support. We fit it and the anchor on the fitting and proposal rows and compare them on separate guard rows. A rejected nominee returns the anchor. We retrain the delivered support $S_f$ on all development rows and measure
\begin{equation}
 U=(1-|S_f|/|A|)\,\mathbf 1\{\text{delivered refit meets every outer tolerance}\}.
 \label{eq:utility}
\end{equation}
A fallback has zero savings. A delivered deletion that fails outside the guard also has zero $U$ and counts as a breach. We separately fitted every candidate at both stages, permitting exact expectations for randomized nominations and a hindsight upper bound for these slates.

\paragraph{Sources and timing of comparisons.}
Ten numeric or ordinal UCI classification tasks with 4--41 predictors contributed 30 stratified outer folds. Within each development split we used 75\% for fitting, 12.5\% for proposal scoring and 12.5\% for the guard. Six separate sources with 72--856 predictors contributed 16 folds, including chronological splits for Ozone and SECOM, participant aggregates for Parkinson and the official Urban split. There were 106 and 110 deletion candidates on 20 and 15 actionable folds. We retained all 46 folds in policy estimates. The outcome labels were public. We recorded the original boosted-tree nominations and predictions before local scoring of each panel. The later comparisons within Gain shortlists reused these outcomes. The forest sensitivity likewise uses the same sources and slates. We fixed the initial forest settings before fitting and sealed the original forest predictions and nominations before scoring them. The within-tier lotteries and omission-of-source comparisons were specified later and use those previously seen outcomes. Policy tables average folds within task and then weight tasks equally. Tables~\ref{tab:observer-controls} and~\ref{tab:precision} instead count proposals in the fixed historical archive. Intervals resample whole tasks in pairs, 30,000 times, conditional on the recorded folds and fits. The two purposive collections have ten and six task units. Appendix~\ref{sec:transport-details} gives source identifiers, split details, overlaps and per-task results.

\paragraph{What to read from each masked fit.}
For metric $i$, let $h_{bi}=\tau_i|R_{bi}|$. The normalized slack on the classification metrics is $(V_{bi}-R_{bi}+h_{bi}+\epsilon)/\max(h_{bi},\epsilon)$. On each panel, take the minimum slack across metrics. The \emph{point margin} uses only the original panel at the empty restoration. The \emph{full margin} takes the third-largest of four panel margins at each restoration, then the smallest of those values across restorations. A nonnegative full margin is exactly equivalent to a three-of-four approval at every restoration. The ordinal rule maximizes $v(S)$ from Section~\ref{sec:measurements}. Both the vote and the full margin take a minimum over restorations. Each running minimum is an upper bound on the final score. For either scalar score the resulting cutoff is the classical depth-two max--min pruning rule~\cite{knuth1975alphabeta}. A candidate can stop as soon as its bound, including the specified tie order, cannot beat an already evaluated candidate. When the vote is primary and the full margin secondary, their two running bounds in lexicographic order give the same stopping test. Unless stated otherwise the final tie order is increasing sum of within-anchor feature ranks, then sorted feature indices.

\paragraph{How large a difference can the design resolve?}
On a fold with weight $w_f$, two policies returning the same support have identical $U$. If their delivered supports differ and remove fractions $d_L$ and $d_R$, their contribution to $U_L-U_R$ lies in $[-w_fd_R,w_fd_L]$. On the six wider XGBoost tasks, full and point margins returned different supports only on SECOM fold 0. Each removed half the anchor and the fold weight was $1/18$. Thus an upper bound on the average improvement was $1/36=2.78$ percentage points, below the locally prespecified five-point target before outer scoring. Both returns later breached. This is a design check available after observing both guards. Deploying a single nominee observes only its own guard. Appendix~\ref{sec:receiver} gives the corresponding bound for randomized policies.

\subsection{How much does the vote add after Gain?}
Literal Gain selection has a unique winner on every actionable fold. For ordered within-anchor ranks $r_1<\cdots<r_w$, each $r_i\ge i$, so only the feature prefix minimizes $\sum_i r_i$. A second check using the sum of the actual unrounded mean training split gains also found a unique best support on all 35 actionable slates. Uniform and point tie-breaking therefore make precisely the same strict Gain decisions, yielding 0.00\% and 7.34\% $U$ with XGBoost refits on the two panels, and 2.59\% and 16.55\% with logistic refits.

Table~\ref{tab:gain-matched} evaluates secondary choices. To allow such a choice, we declared a broader Gain group before calculating the new contrasts. Retain the best $\lceil m/2\rceil$ of the $m$ candidate \emph{sets} by Gain rank sum. On this identical shortlist, choose uniformly or choose the largest point margin. Repeat each choice after restricting it to the candidates with maximum minimum vote. This design isolates the contribution of the vote for each given tie rule. Every candidate in every group has the same feature width. Uniform values are exact expectations over the realized guarded outcomes.

Only 16 of 30 first-panel folds and 15 of 16 wider folds had more than one candidate after the half-slate restriction. With point selection, the vote changed one first-panel nominee, on Seeds. Both receiving learners gained 1.33 percentage points of $U$ (paired 95\% interval $[0,4.00]$). The breach rate was unchanged for XGBoost and fell by 3.33 points for logistic refits. On the wider panel voting left every nominee unchanged. Across its 16 folds, point selection required 71 distinct masks and adding the vote required 168. Uniform selection gives the vote more opportunity to alter the decision. It added 0.67 points to $U$ on the first panel for each receiver ($[0,2.00]$). On the wider panel XGBoost gained 2.73 points entirely on Ozone ($[0,8.19]$), and logistic gained 1.36 ($[-2.08,6.15]$) with an offsetting loss on SECOM. Restricting the Gain group to the top quarter left vote--point gains at zero throughout. Thus a contribution exists for particular shortlists and sources, but the extra computation rarely altered a deterministic point choice.


\subsection{What does a continuous score add among equally voted candidates?}
We replaced both the masking and receiving learners with a 100-tree random forest~\cite{breiman2001random}. In the original design the forest evaluated the same Gain anchor, subsets and folds as the booster. The second design ranked features on the fitting partition by their univariate ANOVA $F$ statistic, took the top half as the anchor, and proposed removing roughly one quarter of it. Each local slate contained the $F$ prefix at the chosen width and at most seven one-column exchanges. Three draws shared folds, anchors and model seeds. We average those draws within fold. The two constructions change several parts of the decision at once, including the denominator of $U$.
