\paragraph{Which candidate can win under the same assignment?}
Let $J_S(\pi)$ indicate that refitting on $S$ meets both outer floors when the same permutation $\pi$ assigns feature rows to labels for the anchor and every candidate. For fixed guard decisions $g_S,g_T\in\{0,1\}$ and removal fractions $d_S,d_T$, the sharp set of utility differences is
\[
 \bigl\{d_Sg_S J_S(\pi)-d_Tg_T J_T(\pi):\ \pi\text{ preserves every observed query}\bigr\}.
\]
Each member uses one feasible assignment for both decisions. Subtracting the two marginal sets permits different assignments. For the historical supports we measure potential compression conditional on delivery ($g_S=g_T=1$). On all 1,283 same-fold, equal-width pairs, development observations alone admitted all four pass/fail combinations. Given the outer empty-restoration predictions for both supports and their common anchor, 926 pairs always tied, 294 always had the same strict winner, 57 allowed a tie or one strict ordering, and six allowed either strict ordering. For 1,218 of the 1,220 fixed differences, each candidate's own query and anchor already fixed its decision. To isolate the contribution of a shared assignment, hold the joint observations fixed and allow two independent assignments instead. This enlarges the set of pass/fail combinations on five pairs and the set of utility differences on three, all on SPECTF fold 2. Each of the three recovers a strict ordering ruled out by the shared assignment. The candidate-specific queries vary across pairs.

For a comparison with identical observations throughout, take three five-column supports from one seven-column SPECTF anchor (fold 2). They share columns $11,15,39$ and add, respectively, $\{25,32\}$ for $S$, $\{25,43\}$ for $T$, and $\{32,43\}$ for $V$ (zero-based indices). After observing all three point predictions and the anchor point prediction, the 80 feasible label allocations gave exactly four joint pass vectors in $(S,T,V)$ order, namely $110,100,010,111$. Each candidate can pass or fail. The utility difference $U_S-U_V$ can be $0$ or $2/7$, while $U_S-U_T$ can be $-2/7$, $0$ or $2/7$. When the choice is restricted to $S$ and $V$, always choosing $S$ incurs zero worst-case regret for this payoff. Between $S$ and $T$ either deterministic choice can lose $2/7$. An equal lottery reduces worst-case expected regret to $1/7$. These are consequences of the specified observer and payoff. No distribution on possible row assignments is required.
