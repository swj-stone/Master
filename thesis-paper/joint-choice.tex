\paragraph{Which candidate can win under the same assignment?}
Let $J_S(\pi)$ indicate that refitting on $S$ meets both outer floors when the same permutation $\pi$ assigns feature rows to labels for the anchor and every candidate. For fixed guard decisions $g_S,g_T\in\{0,1\}$ and removal fractions $d_S,d_T$, the sharp set of utility differences is
\[
 \bigl\{d_Sg_S J_S(\pi)-d_Tg_T J_T(\pi):\ \pi\text{ preserves every observed query}\bigr\}.
\]
Each member uses one feasible assignment for both decisions. Subtracting the two marginal sets permits different assignments. For the historical supports we measure potential compression conditional on delivery ($g_S=g_T=1$). On all 1,283 same-fold, equal-width pairs, development observations alone admitted all four pass/fail combinations. Given the outer empty-restoration predictions for both supports and their common anchor, 926 pairs always tied, 294 always had the same strict winner, 57 allowed a tie or one strict ordering, and six allowed either strict ordering. The candidate-specific query sets vary across these pairs.

For a comparison with identical observations throughout, take three five-column supports from one seven-column SPECTF anchor (fold 2). They share columns $11,15,39$ and add, respectively, $\{25,32\}$ for $S$, $\{25,43\}$ for $T$, and $\{32,43\}$ for $V$ (zero-based indices). After observing all three point predictions and the anchor point prediction, the 80 feasible label allocations gave exactly four joint pass vectors in $(S,T,V)$ order, namely $110,100,010,111$. Each candidate can pass or fail. The utility difference $U_S-U_V$ can be $0$ or $2/7$, while $U_S-U_T$ can be $-2/7$, $0$ or $2/7$. Choosing $S$ instead of $V$ thus incurs zero worst-case regret for this payoff. Between $S$ and $T$ either deterministic choice can lose $2/7$. An equal lottery reduces worst-case expected regret to $1/7$. These are consequences of the specified observer and payoff. No distribution on possible row assignments is required.
