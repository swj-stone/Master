oral toxicity (508), Tehran Residential Building (437), and UJIIndoorLoc

partitions; Tehran used completion-quarter blocks and UJI a later cohort
of Wi-Fi observations. UJI and Tehran each reuse one outer test across

MinPI selected fewer reachable columns than MDI in 11 of 20 fits (width
ratio $.893$). Relative to MDI the mean differences were $-.00334$ in AUC,
$+.00007$ in accuracy, $+.00079$ in $R^2$ and $+.00014$ in scaled MAE.
Both regressions lost more than $.02$ mean $R^2$ against the full forest.
Choosing feasible supports by reachable width reproduced the predictions
obtained by choosing nominal widths on all 55 jobs in the two panels.
Table~\ref{tab:wide} illustrates why counting the original columns as
measurements saved would exaggerate the benefit: the full advertising
forest itself requested only 264.6 of 1,558 columns.

In a post-release comparison with the same acceptance rule, replacing the
second path with mean-size or intact-size ranking gave virtually the same
widths: the MinPI width ratios were $1.009$ [$ .967,1.070$] and $1.007$
[$ .967,1.057$], respectively. MinPI and mean-size ranking each had four
held-out quota violations. The selected columns themselves varied across
seeds. Pairwise Jaccard similarity, averaged over ten seed pairs per source,
was $.344$ for MinPI and $.360$ for MDI across the wide panel, compared
with $.838$ and $.835$ on the seven narrower sources.

Recomputing votes with uniform masks on the full forest's referenced
columns instead of all declared inputs changed no acceptance or outer
quota decision for either recorded support in the 55 jobs. Repeating the
entire selection under the referenced-column law on the wide panel changed
one portfolio, by four columns on oral toxicity. The resulting width ratio
against MDI was $.892$.

We compared interval rejection with scalar enumeration, native early
rejection and one-thread BLAS, with exact batching of equivalent dormant
candidates available to the controls. Timings used an Intel Core Ultra
9 275HX, one native thread, warmed compiled kernels and interleaved repeats.
Every method returned the same
complete deletion order on all 20 fits. Against the fastest measured
alternative per fit the production interval algorithm's geometric
ranking-time ratio was $.466$ [$ .316,.694$]. Repeating the full fits,
selection and prediction gave a time ratio of $.936$ [$ .853,1.003$],
short of the registered 10\% full-workflow target. The ratios ranged
from $.809$ for oral toxicity to $1.004$ for UJI. Against the cheaper
MDI-only selector the full workflow took $1.293$ times as long
[$1.035,1.617$].

\paragraph{When replacing the forest is allowed.}
We also tested importance-ranked backward elimination with retraining,
following the subset-search idea of \citet{diaz2006gene}. We fixed
the original MDI ranking, removed 20\% or 50\% of remaining columns
at each step, refit on the same rows, and applied the same independent
quota and intact-metric checks. Both learners face masks drawn from the
original input universe. Their expected losses follow by applying the
path expansion separately to each model, including its empty prediction.
On the seven sources the 20\% grid used 10.1 fits on average against one
for MinPI. The frozen workflow took $.142$ times its time to select,
but used $1.413$ times its columns. Frozen MinPI had higher mean AUC
($.827$ versus $.817$) and lower mean $R^2$ ($.281$ versus $.310$).
One additional refit at the frozen width reduced worst-size violations
from ten to six for MinPI and from nine to three for MDI. Across the four
wider seed-42 fits both refit grids had better average regression scores
and higher classification accuracy than frozen MinPI.

\paragraph{Recorded missing measurements.}
The original Beijing study used complete cases at one station. We next
retained incomplete cases in the full 12-station network, using common
time boundaries and holding out the final 292 days. To evaluate further
random failures conditional on a row's observed measurements, zero that
row's increments for every path requiring an absent input, then apply
the same size-vote formula. All five selection variants chose the same
five columns. Among the 239 incomplete station-days with observed next-day
PM2.5, mean absolute error of the 12-input forest was 51.12 under stopping
and 49.09 with training-median imputation. The paired difference was
$2.03$ [$0.81,3.28$], resampling seven-day blocks synchronously across
stations. With the common five selected inputs the difference was
$1.81$ [$0.61,2.97$]. On these recorded outages, imputation produced
better forecasts. The stations belong to one monitoring network.
