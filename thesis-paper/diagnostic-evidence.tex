\section{Which refit outcomes does a masked transcript identify?}
\label{sec:evidence}
A transcript can contain predictions for individual cases or only the metrics computed from them. It also matters \emph{which} cases were masked. We first characterize the information in per-row predictions for a fixed evaluation sample, then compare the resulting ambiguities with collisions between proposals in the historical data.

\paragraph{A sharp set for finite tree ensembles.}
Suppose the fitting observations, target feature set, trained full and reduced predictors, evaluation labels, and allowed values of each hidden evaluation input are known. For row $i$, observe the vector of full-model outputs under the specified coordinate masks. Partition its admissible inputs by the split thresholds visited by all masked trees and all reduced trees. Keep every cell yielding that row's observed output vector. Each surviving cell supplies a possible score of the reduced predictor. Different leaf tuples that give the same output remain observationally equivalent. This construction applies to fixed finite ensembles of arbitrary-depth axis-aligned trees, including boosted trees followed by a logistic link~\cite{misic2020ensemble}. Enumeration can be exponential in the number of splits.

For binary outcomes let the distinct reduced scores be $r_1<\cdots<r_J$. Rows with equal labels and the same feasible score bins form a group. An integer allocation assigns the observed number of rows of each group to its feasible bins. If the pooled reduced-score histogram is also observed, fix its bin totals. Write $a_j,b_j$ for the positive and negative counts assigned to bin $j$ and $d_j$ for its predicted class. The joint identified set comprises exactly the pairs
\begin{equation}
 \left(\frac{\sum_j a_j(\sum_{\ell<j} b_\ell+b_j/2)}{n_+n_-},
       \frac{\sum_j [a_jd_j+b_j(1-d_j)]}{n_++n_-}\right)
 \label{eq:main-tree-set}
\end{equation}
over feasible allocations. Necessity follows by counting rows on their realized cells. For sufficiency, place each assigned row in a compatible cell with its assigned score. This requires rowwise admissibility up to the stated histogram constraint. Other known constraints on joint inputs must also be imposed. For fixed reference floors, a tolerance decision is identified precisely when every attainable pair passes or every pair fails. Without a histogram, moving positives to their highest feasible bins and negatives to their lowest simultaneously maximizes binary AUC and accuracy. A fixed histogram instead makes both coordinates affine in the cell counts. Appendix~\ref{sec:ensemble-identification} gives the proof, a minimum-cost-flow computation of extrema for linear objectives, and the extension to multiclass outcomes.

For a stump that uses only a subsequently deleted feature, a two-level reduced score suffices to realize the whole interval from chance or worse to perfect prediction. At balanced label and surviving-feature margins, the same frozen transcript admits $(\mathrm{AUC},\mathrm{accuracy})=(s,s)$ throughout $[0,1]$ in the population. If a false veto costs $a$ and a missed breach costs $m$, an ambiguous observation has sharp minimax expected cost $am/(a+m)$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:identification}). Ambiguity about this decision can also occur for a nontrivial ensemble. For a fitted nine-tree, depth-three booster on twelve legal inputs, exhaustive enumeration after observing all eight masks and the reduced-score histogram gave exactly three pairs, $(13/18,1/2)$, $(8/9,5/6)$ and $(1,5/6)$, straddling the prespecified floors. A separate 100-round construction passes the actual Gain nomination and guard (Appendix~\ref{sec:boosted-example}). These witnesses establish possibility. They do not estimate its frequency on an evaluation population.

\paragraph{What the 704 proposals reveal.}
Fourteen PMLB classification tasks supplied three stratified folds each~\cite{romano2022pmlb}. There were 400 proposals sampled from deletion search paths and 379 drawn at predetermined removal sizes. Their union contained 704 distinct task--fold--support records, including 75 shared records. Within each fold and retained width, all 704 records had distinct vectors of unrounded masked AUC and accuracy on the four proposal panels. After thresholding, 31 ballot groups included opposite refit decisions, covering 136 records on eight tasks. A rule receiving only the entire ballot matrix has to make at least 41 errors on these records, even if it chooses the majority outcome in every group using hindsight. The corresponding minimum was 51 when the target changed from a 100-round XGBoost refit~\cite{chen2016xgboost} to a logistic refit. Uniqueness of a continuous vector across different supports permits lookup in a finite archive. A single previously unseen support presents a different identification problem.

We therefore held each individual proposal, all development data, and both actual boosted-tree fits fixed. We kept the outer labels and multiset of complete outer feature vectors, permitting each vector to be reassigned to another outer position only if all observed predictions at that position remained unchanged. This preserves the joint distribution of the recorded features and permits their pairing with the labels to vary. Given the actual development and proposal observations alone, all 704 proposals admitted an explicit reassignment that reversed the outer tolerance decision against the correspondingly reassigned anchor. After revealing the outer rowwise scores of the frozen model on the empty-restoration anchor and candidate, 17 still admitted opposing decisions. Adding the single unmasked initial predictor to this pair ruled out reversals for every proposal. The full-anchor/full-tail endpoint gave the same partition as the unmasked predictor on all 42 folds. Observing every recorded restoration likewise left all decisions invariant. The interiors contributed no further identification of passage in this comparison. Giving the observer also the final anchor's rowwise scores and the unmasked frozen scores left 93 proposals with an ambiguous individual reduced score but only three whose AUC could vary. None could cross the joint tolerance. A finite observed multiset can be substantially more restrictive than the input space in Eq.~\eqref{eq:main-tree-set}.

On the historical search paths, adding interior restorations caught eleven more refit failures and vetoed twenty more passing deletions than the two endpoint restorations. On randomly drawn deletions, the extra restorations caught no additional failures and vetoed nine additional passes. The cost crossover, task/fold bootstrap intervals and out-of-task breach prediction results appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:extended-operating}.
