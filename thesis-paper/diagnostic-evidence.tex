\section{Which refit outcomes does a masked transcript identify?}
\label{sec:evidence}
A transcript can contain predictions for individual cases or only the metrics computed from them. It also matters \emph{which} cases were masked. We first characterize the information in per-row predictions for a fixed evaluation sample, then compare the resulting ambiguities with collisions between proposals in the historical data.

\paragraph{A sharp set for finite tree ensembles.}
Suppose the fitting observations, target feature set, trained full and reduced predictors, evaluation labels, and allowed values of each hidden evaluation input are known. For row $i$, observe the vector of full-model outputs under the specified coordinate masks. Partition its admissible inputs by the split thresholds visited by all masked trees and all reduced trees. Keep every cell yielding that row's observed output vector. Each surviving cell supplies a possible score of the reduced predictor. Different leaf tuples that give the same output remain observationally equivalent. This construction applies to fixed finite ensembles of arbitrary-depth axis-aligned trees, including boosted trees followed by a logistic link~\cite{misic2020ensemble}. Enumeration can be exponential in the number of splits.

For binary outcomes let the distinct reduced scores be $r_1<\cdots<r_J$. Rows with equal labels and the same feasible score bins form a group. An integer allocation assigns the observed number of rows of each group to its feasible bins. If the pooled reduced-score histogram is also observed, fix its bin totals. Write $a_j,b_j$ for the positive and negative counts assigned to bin $j$ and $d_j$ for its predicted class. The joint identified set comprises exactly the pairs
\begin{equation}
 \left(\frac{\sum_j a_j(\sum_{\ell<j} b_\ell+b_j/2)}{n_+n_-},
       \frac{\sum_j [a_jd_j+b_j(1-d_j)]}{n_++n_-}\right)
 \label{eq:main-tree-set}
\end{equation}
over feasible allocations. Necessity follows by counting rows on their realized cells. For sufficiency, place each assigned row in a compatible cell with its assigned score. This requires rowwise admissibility up to the stated histogram constraint. Other known constraints on joint inputs must also be imposed. For fixed reference floors, a tolerance decision is identified precisely when every attainable pair passes or every pair fails. Without a histogram, moving positives to their highest feasible bins and negatives to their lowest simultaneously maximizes binary AUC and accuracy. A fixed histogram instead makes both coordinates affine in the cell counts. Appendix~\ref{sec:ensemble-identification} gives the proof, a minimum-cost-flow computation of extrema for linear objectives, and the extension to multiclass outcomes.

For a stump that uses only a subsequently deleted feature, a two-level reduced score suffices to realize the whole interval from chance or worse to perfect prediction. At balanced label and surviving-feature margins, the same frozen transcript admits $(\mathrm{AUC},\mathrm{accuracy})=(s,s)$ throughout $[0,1]$ in the population. If a false veto costs $a$ and a missed breach costs $m$, an ambiguous observation has sharp minimax expected cost $am/(a+m)$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:identification}). Ambiguity about this decision can also occur for a nontrivial ensemble. For a fitted nine-tree, depth-three booster on twelve legal inputs, exhaustive enumeration after observing all eight masks and the reduced-score histogram gave exactly three pairs, $(13/18,1/2)$, $(8/9,5/6)$ and $(1,5/6)$, straddling the prespecified floors. A separate 100-round construction passes the actual Gain nomination and guard (Appendix~\ref{sec:boosted-example}). These witnesses establish possibility. They do not estimate its frequency on an evaluation population.

\paragraph{What the 704 proposals reveal.}
Fourteen PMLB classification tasks supplied three stratified folds each~\cite{romano2022pmlb}. There were 400 proposals sampled from deletion search paths and 379 drawn at predetermined removal sizes. Their union contained 704 distinct task--fold--support records, including 75 shared records. Within each fold and retained width, all 704 records had distinct vectors of unrounded masked AUC and accuracy on the four proposal panels. After thresholding, 31 ballot groups included opposite refit decisions, covering 136 records on eight tasks. A rule receiving only the entire ballot matrix has to make at least 41 errors on these records, even if it chooses the majority outcome in every group using hindsight. The corresponding minimum was 51 when the target changed from a 100-round XGBoost refit~\cite{chen2016xgboost} to a logistic refit. Uniqueness of a continuous vector across different supports permits lookup in a finite archive. A single previously unseen support presents a different identification problem.

\paragraph{Which outer observations identify the decision?}
For each of the 704 proposals separately, we fix all development data and the fitted predictors. The observer knows the ordered outer labels and the multiset of complete outer feature vectors, but their row-to-label linkage is hidden. This is an identification calculation after fitting the candidate and its anchor. The proposed selector has neither these two fits nor the outer queries when nominating a support. An assignment $\pi$ places feature vector $\pi(i)$ at position $i$. The observer can rule out an assignment if it changes any supplied frozen-model probability vector at any position, at the implementation's original precision. This finite experiment allows the association between features and labels to change, while preserving every within-row feature relationship. It asks whether the observed decision could reverse. Sampling frequency and the risk on a new draw of test cases require different assumptions.

The 17 point-query reversals comprised ten observed passes and seven failures on five folds from four tasks, twelve on SPECTF. Unmasked full-model predictions alone identify the decision for every proposal. Of 38,021 row occurrences over 42 outer folds, 35,619 (93.68\%) had a probability vector unique within their fold. Such an output identifies a particular row of the finite feature dictionary, forcing its assignment. A scalar or short probability vector can thus carry considerable row-identity information when matched at full precision against a known dictionary. The model can achieve this without measuring any candidate mask on the outer rows. The full-anchor/full-tail endpoint gives the same partition on all 42 folds. Interior restorations resolved no additional decisions in this experiment. If we also reveal all masked outer predictions and the refitted anchor's rowwise predictions, individual reduced scores can still vary for 93 proposals and reduced AUC for three. All variations remain on the same side of the joint tolerance.

\paragraph{How a counterexample or invariance certificate is computed.}
Both the candidate refit $S$ and its anchor refit $A$ follow the same reassignment. As the assignment changes, the candidate score and the floor supplied by the anchor can both change. For class $c$ with $n_c$ positives among $n$ rows, let $R^S_{jc}$ and $R^A_{jc}$ be the ascending midranks of feature row $j$'s predicted probability of $c$ under the two fits. The ranks are fixed under any reassignment. With $\rho=.97$, the one-versus-rest AUC gap is
\begin{equation}
 G_c(\pi)=
 \frac{\sum_{i:y_i=c}\!\big(R^S_{\pi(i)c}-\rho R^A_{\pi(i)c}\big)
       -(1-\rho)n_c(n_c+1)/2}{n_c(n-n_c)}.
 \label{eq:assignmentgap}
\end{equation}
This follows by subtracting the two rank-sum AUC formulas~\cite{bamber1975area}. Use the positive class for binary AUC and average $G_c$ over classes for multiclass AUC. The accuracy gap is the average over $i$ of $\mathbf 1\{\hat y^S_{\pi(i)}=y_i\}-\rho\mathbf 1\{\hat y^A_{\pi(i)}=y_i\}$. Each gap is therefore an additive assignment objective within equivalence classes of the supplied outputs. A pass requires both gaps to be at least $-\epsilon$. Average precision was inactive on these folds. Two above-floor minima certify invariance of a pass, and a single below-floor maximum certifies invariance of a failure. To establish ambiguity we exhibit an assignment and rescore \emph{both} metrics against their reassigned anchor. Separate maxima may occur at different assignments and need not jointly witness a pass. Restricted searches supplied admissible witnesses. Bounds for invariance use the entire allowed class, including a conservative numerical margin of $10^{-13}$ around the decision tolerance. We revisited 26 initially inconclusive records with unrestricted within-class assignment.

\paragraph{Does the score carry information beyond row identity?}
We compared the genuine output with an identifier of the same precision and frequency. At $b$ bits each class probability $p$ becomes $\min(2^b-1,\lfloor 2^bp\rfloor)$. For each of five draws, we randomly reassigned the genuine codes among distinct input vectors of the same observed multiplicity. Equal input vectors share a code, and every code has the same occupancy as in the genuine partition. Each observer knows the complete mapping from input vectors in its dictionary to codes and the code at each labelled position. Across resolutions we use the same reassignment within a draw. Matching the marginal occupancy preserves the number and sizes of code groups. It leaves their intersection with the candidate-specific point-query groups unconstrained. The reassignment uses neither labels nor refitted scores. The original full-precision result was known when this control was designed.

This measures distinguishability among the known inputs. Forecasting the decision for an unseen support or new cases requires an out-of-sample comparison.

On the historical search paths, adding interior restorations caught eleven more refit failures and vetoed twenty more passing deletions than the two endpoint restorations. On randomly drawn deletions, the extra restorations caught no additional failures and vetoed nine additional passes.
