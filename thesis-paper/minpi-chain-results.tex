\section{Reusing observed coalition votes}
\label{sec:chain-evidence}
On the eight primary sources, checking all observed neighbouring coalitions
raised the number of independently verified negative indices from 14 to 17
out of 47 on the enumerable games. The additional witnesses came from Raisin
and Energy. Only two of the 40 selected supports changed: on two Energy
splits the receiver retained four features in place of six and eight.
Across the four regression sources the resulting width ratio to coefficient
ranking was .947, with a change of $+.0059$ in $R^2$ and $-.0103$ in scaled
MAE. Selection took $1.37$ times as long. These post-hoc gains were
concentrated in one source. On the 16 wider folds all predictions stayed
identical to those of the original sampler.

\paragraph{Bisection at a fixed query budget.}
On the eight primary sources only four of 40 splits required interior queries.
In all four the budget exhausted the available interior prefixes, so choosing
them uniformly produced exactly the same result. On the 16 wider folds,
800 initial endpoint votes identified nontrivial brackets in two Parkinson
folds and one SECOM fold.
Bisection found nine negative feature--fold pairs in 49 additional distinct
queries. Uniform sampling of the same number of prefixes from the same
brackets found zero in the recorded draws. Its exact conditional expectation
was .923 over those draws (summing each feature's probability of detection).
Across all 16 folds this changed the delivered subset twice relative to uniform
sampling. Mean AUC increased by .0016 and accuracy fell by .0002. Discovering
more witnesses here had little effect on the final predictions.

\paragraph{New sources.}
We fixed the chain search before testing it on four further UCI sources
(Table~\ref{tab:chain-four}). Landsat retains the official test set across
all five seeds. For the two QSAR tasks we grouped duplicate descriptor vectors;
the test assesses new records without a scaffold holdout. Power Plant uses
one of the five reshufflings distributed by the repository. Across the two
classification and two regression sources the chain rule retained 52.4\%
and 59.8\% of the columns geometrically. The average metric changes met the
predefined margins. Landsat individually lost 1.02 accuracy points.

Relative to univariate $F$, chain search retained 21.8\% fewer features in
classification and 12.9\% fewer in regression within the same predictive
margins. Selection took 9.2\% and 14.4\% longer. Against individual
addition sampling, chain search took 7.7\% and 22.7\% less selection time,
respectively, but the regression supports were 2.9\% wider. Neither domain
met the registered joint criterion of 10\% less time at no larger width.
The individual-addition sampler found 43 negative feature--split pairs,
chain bisection nine, and its matched random control six. Bisection changed
one final support relative to that control. Coefficient ranking gave the
same supports as bisection on 16 of 20 splits and took less time in both
domains. On Landsat and Power Plant, bisection found zero negative features
on all five splits despite substantial compression.

An additional comparison used greedy forward regularized least squares
with leave-one-row-out squared loss~\cite{pahikkala2010rls}. We implemented
the rank-one smoother updates and checked the resulting orders against explicit
leave-one-out refits on scalar and multioutput problems. Stopping at the
last tested prefix before full width preserved all predictions. Across nine
interleaved timings per split, the chain rule took 71.6\% of this wrapper's
selection time in classification and 107.0\% in regression. Its supports
were 15.9\% and 23.1\% wider.

\paragraph{Deleting witnessed features directly.}
In a retrospective test on all twelve sources, we first selected the coefficient
prefix, then proposed removing only its witnessed negatives. A single additional
refit checked the proposal. Nine classification and seven regression splits
out of 60 contained eligible features.
The guard accepted nine batches when targeted by witness, three when targeting
the same number of lowest coefficients and six when targeting random columns.
Across all splits the extra step saved 1.7\% of coefficient-selected columns
in classification and 5.6\% in regression. Both reductions fell short of the
registered 10\% width target. Selection time increased by 14\% and 83\%, respectively.
