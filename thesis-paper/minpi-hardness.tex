\section{Complexity of choosing the support}
\label{app:hardness}

The exact size votes let us test a given support efficiently. Finding the
narrowest support introduces a separate combinatorial problem.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:support-hardness}
Given a regression forest, labeled calibration rows and an integer $k$,
deciding whether some support $S$ of size at most $k$ satisfies
$\max_s V_s(S)\le .02\|z\|^2$ is NP-hard. This holds for an equally
weighted ensemble of depth-two trees, each using only one feature.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We reduce from \textsc{Clique} in a simple graph~\citep{karp1972reducibility}.
Take $3\le k\le |V|$, put $b=\binom{k}{2}$, and adjoin $2b$ isolated
edges on new vertices. This preserves all cliques of size $k$. Let $p$
and $M$ be the numbers of vertices and edges of the enlarged graph.

Assign one input to each vertex. Tree $j$ splits on $x_j$ at $-1/2$ and,
on the right branch, at $1/2$. Its three leaf predictions in order are
$-p,0,p$. These are empirical response means on the common training sample
with inputs $\pm e_i$ and respective responses $\pm p$ for $1\le i\le p$,
where $e_i$ is the $i$th unit vector. Give each internal node its training
mean. Every tree then has root value zero. Averaging the trees gives
$f_T(x)=\sum_{j\in T}x_j$ on $\{-1,0,1\}^p$.

For each edge $\{i,j\}$ with $i<j$, include the calibration row
$(e_i-e_j,0)$ and the two rows $(e_i,1)$ and $(e_j,1)$.
Add $100(M-b)-2M$ copies of the all-zero input with response one.
This count is nonnegative since $M\ge 2b$, and the enlarged sample has
polynomial size. Here $z=y$ and $\|z\|^2=100(M-b)$.

The path vector $a_{\{j\}}$ is column $j$ of the calibration matrix.
Its squared norm is $2\deg(j)=2\langle z,a_{\{j\}}\rangle$, so its
singleton loss coefficient vanishes. Two distinct columns have inner
product $-1$ if their vertices share an edge and zero otherwise.
Hence the only nonzero loss coefficients are the edge weights
$w_{\{i,j\}}=2$. If $e(S)$ counts edges induced by $S$, the vote is
\[
  V_s(S)=2\,\frac{s-1}{p-1}\bigl(M-e(S)\bigr).
\]
Its maximum occurs at $s=p$. The quota thus requires $e(S)\ge b$.
A set of at most $k$ vertices meets this condition exactly when it is a
$k$-clique.
\end{proof}

For a numerical instance, take edges $12,13,23,34$ and $k=3$.
Here $M=4$ already permits the construction without adjoining edges.
The four edge rows, eight unit rows and 92 zero rows give $\|z\|^2=100$.
For $S=\{1,2,3\}$ the four votes are $(0,2/3,4/3,2)$, so the support
passes at equality. For $S=\{1,3,4\}$ they are $(0,4/3,8/3,4)$.
