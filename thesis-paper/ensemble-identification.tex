\subsection{What a complete score transcript identifies for a tree ensemble}
\label{sec:ensemble-identification}
Fix a binary frozen predictor $F$, a retained set $S$, and the refitted positive-class score $H(x_S)$. The training observations, random seeds and both fitted models are known. The hidden quantities are the evaluation inputs paired with their observed labels. For each row $i$ specify an admissible input set $\mathcal X_i$, which can encode known coordinates. In the absence of a stated marginal constraint, the rows can take any values in $\prod_i\mathcal X_i$, including repetitions. Observing the raw paired inputs would determine all refit scores immediately.

Let $T_i(x)$ collect the real-valued outputs of $F$ on the requested masks of $x$, filling hidden coordinates by fixed values. Write $t_i$ for the observed vector. Both $F$ and $H$ may be finite ensembles of finite axis-aligned trees of arbitrary depth, with scores determined by their leaf outputs. This includes regression-tree boosting followed by a logistic link. For a fixed mask, pulling each split back to the input coordinates leaves a coordinate threshold or a constant decision. Intersect the resulting split regions across all masks and trees with the split regions of $H$ and with $\mathcal X_i$, retaining the original boundary conventions. Each nonempty cell has one transcript and one refit score. This common refinement uses the finite leaf geometry of tree ensembles~\cite{misic2020ensemble}; its size can grow exponentially. Exact equality of output vectors is the observation criterion. Different tuples of leaves can give the same vector.

Let $r_1<\cdots<r_J$ be the distinct refit scores. Write $B_i$ for the indices $j$ of scores attained on at least one cell with transcript $t_i$. Rows with the same label and the same allowed set form a group $g$, of size $m_g$, label $y_g$ and allowed indices $B_g$. Both label counts $n_+,n_-$ are positive. Let $d_j$ be the predicted class at score $r_j$ under the fixed threshold and tie convention.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:ensemble-identification}
Take the nonnegative integer arrays $k_{gj}$ with row totals $m_g$ and $k_{gj}=0$ for $j\notin B_g$. If the histogram $h_j$ of reduced scores is also observed, impose column totals $h_j$. Write $a_j=\sum_{g:y_g=1}k_{gj}$ and $b_j=\sum_{g:y_g=0}k_{gj}$. The sharp empirical set of refit AUC and accuracy consists of the following pairs, one for each feasible array,
\begin{equation}
\left(
 \frac{\sum_j a_j(\sum_{\ell<j}b_\ell+b_j/2)}{n_+n_-},\
 \frac{\sum_j \bigl(a_jd_j+b_j(1-d_j)\bigr)}{n_++n_-}
\right).
\label{eq:ensemble-set}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Any compatible set of evaluation inputs induces its cell counts and hence a feasible array. Conversely, assign $k_{gj}$ of group $g$'s rows to score $r_j$. Each assigned row has a witness input of its own with the observed transcript and assigned score by definition of $B_i$. Taking these witnesses together preserves every observation, including the column totals when specified. Counting favourable positive-negative comparisons, with half credit for ties~\cite{bamber1975area}, gives the first coordinate. Counting correct decisions gives the second. For a finite adaptive mask sequence chosen solely from earlier observed outputs and other fixed information, equality along the realised query sequence reproduces every query and its stopping decision by induction.
\end{proof}

Without a pooled constraint, assign each positive its largest allowed score and each negative its smallest. Every pairwise AUC credit and each thresholded classification indicator are then simultaneously maximal. Exchanging smallest and largest gives the simultaneous minima. Thus even a nonsingleton set can identify passage against fixed AUC and accuracy floors: the lower corner passing forces every world to pass, and the upper corner failing forces every world to fail. Otherwise the two corners witness opposite decisions, giving the minimax cost derived above. A supplied score for a separately refitted anchor can be included in each row's transcript to fix the reference floors. An unknown anchor would require joint candidate and anchor scores. These statements concern binary AUC and accuracy; a separately active average-precision floor requires evaluating that additional metric.

When the pooled histogram is known, set $R_j=\sum_{\ell<j}h_\ell+(h_j+1)/2$. The AUC numerator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ensemble-set} becomes $\sum_j a_jR_j-n_+(n_++1)/2$ by ranking tied observations at their midrank. Both coordinates are then affine in the counts. A linear objective in AUC and accuracy can be optimized by sending $m_g$ units from each group to its allowed bins, with demands $h_j$ and the corresponding linear costs. Integral supplies and demands admit an integral minimum-cost flow. The full joint set is the discrete image in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ensemble-set}; optimizing individual coordinates alone can discard its dependence. For two attainable scores and known high-score mass this table reduces to the finite-sample overlap construction of Proposition~\ref{prop:refit-identification} when every row permits both scores.

\paragraph{Multiple classes and average precision.}
The cell-count argument also applies when $H$ outputs a vector of class scores. Index bins by distinct vectors, group rows by observed class and allowed bins, and let $k_{jc}$ denote the assigned count of class $c$ in bin $j$. To compute one-versus-rest AUC for $c$, sort bins by their $c$th coordinate, pool equal coordinates, and use $k_{jc}$ as positives and $\sum_{d\ne c}k_{jd}$ as negatives in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ensemble-set}. Average over classes with both outcomes present, using the prespecified evaluation convention. Accuracy counts the rows whose bin predicts their class. For binary average precision, group equal scores and order these groups from high to low. If group $\ell$ adds $a_\ell$ positives, and $A_\ell,N_\ell$ are the cumulative positive and total counts through that group, its contribution is $(a_\ell/n_+)A_\ell/N_\ell$. Evaluating all active metrics on the same feasible arrays gives their sharp joint set and tests simultaneous passage at the supplied floors. The binary two-metric shortcut for the joint corners need not carry over to multiple class-score coordinates.

\paragraph{Two fitted examples.}
We trained a depth-three CART and a nine-tree XGBoost classifier (maximum fitted depth three) on the same 360 observations over twelve possible inputs $(D,G,R)\in\{0,1\}^2\times\{0,1,2\}$. Each learner was refitted on $(G,R)$. All eight coordinate masks, including the unmasked input, were observed with the training medians as replacements. Each fitted model yielded eight distinct continuous-output transcripts. Six identified the reduced score; each of the other two allowed three different scores. On six labelled rows, exhaustive raw-input enumeration and Eq.~\eqref{eq:ensemble-set} agreed on 81 compatible assignments and eleven distinct AUC--accuracy pairs for each model. Their joint lower and upper corners were $(13/18,1/2)$ and $(1,1)$. Supplying the observed reduced-score histogram left six assignments and exactly three pairs, $(13/18,1/2),(8/9,5/6),(1,5/6)$. They give opposite tolerance decisions against the full model's AUC and accuracy of $2/3$ each. The construction shows precisely which observed transcripts identify a reduced score for these fits. Frequencies among the twelve constructed inputs have no sampling interpretation for the benchmark tasks.

The accompanying Lean file proves the rowwise witness equivalence with an optional constraint on the score vector and monotonicity of the empirical pair-credit sum. The fitted examples separately check the integer-count calculation, midrank identity and extrema by enumeration.
