# Independent replay of test scores

Extract the archive, then run `python -I replay.py` in the extracted directory with the three packages in `requirements.txt`. The script checks the release digests, reconstructs aligned test metrics from the sealed prediction arrays, and writes `recomputed.csv` and `recomputed-source-means.csv`. It uses the public dataset names and coded participant folds. It contains no participant names, author list or workstation directories.

The seven-source panel reports AUC and accuracy for classification and R² and MAE for regression. The wide panel also verifies that the structurally restricted predictions agree with the parent forecasts, then counts the reachable columns in the frozen supports. The Cleveland study checks held predictions for the 12- and 13-variable cases, the number of selected measurements, and the recorded 1992 panel fees. The two wearable studies check every reported arm’s held-subject performance and enumerate all eight or 32 device masks to recover its worst conditional excess Brier loss. For the follow-up refitting comparison the reader checks the separate 18-fold lock against its parent, then reevaluates the forecasts after bounded refitting on nominated supports.

Evaluation labels and row indices were extracted from the registered data providers. The supplement compares them with the sealed evaluation order and checks its results against the reported rows. It does not refit models, independently reconstruct fee schedules, reproduce hardware timings, or reconstruct raw study inputs. The research replication release provides those implementations, source provenance and the historical comparisons. The manuscript and its independent source archive accompany this supplement separately.
