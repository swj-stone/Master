\section{Predictive and computational comparisons}

We developed the receiver on the six previously preprocessed tasks (Madelon,
Spambase, Gisette, ISOLET, Credit Fraud and Abalone). A second assessment
used eight previously unopened UCI datasets and a separately specified
five-dataset extension chosen for its wider input sets. Table~\ref{tab:source}
lists every source in these two panels. XGBoost ran 100 boosting rounds
at depth four and learning rate .1, with one tree per class in each
multiclass round~\citep{chen2016xgboost}. Within each split we estimated
the ensemble on the pooled fitting and voting rows, selected inputs on
separate calibration rows, then evaluated predictions on held-out rows.
The test fraction was about one fifth. Recordings, animals, materials,
locations and patients were kept in groups wherever the source provided
identifiers. Appliances and Online News used chronological splits fixed
across seeds. Five seeds per source yielded 65 paired comparisons.
All prospective supports and test forecasts were hashed before scoring.
The public data recipes give the exact outcomes, exclusions and group keys.

Each selector got the same fitted ensemble, retained-column traversal and
calibration budget. The primary control repeatedly swept the inputs in
ascending mean split gain, taking every deletion that still passed fidelity.
The Gain prefix control checked every retained width. Two additional controls,
specified after opening the primary results, used the same repeated sweep
with inputs ranked by their single deletion effect in the full ensemble or by
mean absolute native TreeSHAP contribution over calibration rows and outputs.
All outer labels were hidden while these rankings were computed.
Another prespecified arm added a supervised calibration check along each
deletion path. It required AUC and accuracy within .01 of the full model on
classification tasks, or $R^2$ and MAE divided by the training target
standard deviation within .02 on regression.

We average paired log ratios over seeds within each source, then average
sources equally and exponentiate. Score differences use the same weighting
without the log transform. The 95\% percentile intervals resample sources,
carrying all their seeds together. They describe variation on this small,
purposively chosen panel. Classification uses macro one-versus-rest AUC
and accuracy. Regression MAE remains in source units in the table;
between-source differences in MAE are scaled by the development target
standard deviation.

\paragraph{Inputs and prediction.}
The greedy-to-Gain width ratio was .894 (95\% interval .847--.939);
the eight-source estimate was .906 and the five-source estimate was .875.
The 10\% width reduction specified before scoring was attained by the overall
point estimate. The interval also includes reductions smaller than 10\%.
Across seven classification sources, the AUC difference was $-.0025$
($-.0081,.0019$) and the accuracy difference was $-.0015$ ($-.0055,.0022$).
Robot LP1 saved four additional inputs on average and lost .0176 AUC
and .0111 accuracy. Giving each frog recording equal weight changed the
Anuran accuracy difference from $-.0059$ to $-.0011$.

On six regression sources, the mean scaled MAE difference was $-.0153$
($-.0508,.0045$). The mean $R^2$ gain of .120 came mainly from Appliances.
Both compressed forecasters had $R^2$ below $-5$ there, substantially worse
than the test-mean reference for $R^2$. Against the uncompressed teacher,
CT location and Superconductor lost .057 and .049 $R^2$, respectively.
The supervised guard gave a width ratio of .864 against the corresponding
guarded Gain sweep, with mean differences of $-.0020$ AUC and $+.0014$
accuracy. These are measurements on held-out cases. A fidelity check on
calibration margins offers no distribution-free bound on those scores.

The greedy width ratio was .768 against the Gain prefix control, .963
(.944--.982) against the TreeSHAP sweep and .988 (.980--.995) against the
full-model ablation sweep. The latter gives a competitive inexpensive rule
for this same prediction objective. Re-fitting a learner after selecting
columns changes the comparison. In the original development experiments,
for example, re-fitted supervised Gain scored about .040 higher in AUC
on Madelon than the guarded greedy refit.

\paragraph{What the minimum saved.}
We replayed every deletion on the 65 held-out splits and the 30 development
splits. The size-conditioned and direct solvers agreed at every step.
On the 65 held-out splits, 96,417 candidate visits involved 3,255 initial
exact norms. Caching alone still required 6,859 fresh norms after that
initialization. The full-size vote reduced these to 1,064, and the three
sizes together to 1,041. The extra two sizes therefore saved 23 fresh norms.
The two-feature example in Section~\ref{sec:tree-minpi} shows where the
smaller sizes can matter; the measured incremental benefit was small.

Timing included constructing the relevant caches and projections. A compiled
control kept current deletion vectors contiguous and accumulated their
squared error 32 coordinates at a time, stopping as soon as the incumbent
was exceeded. This control recovered every deletion and was faster than the
reference projection code. Discarding candidates before norm evaluation
saves work only when that saving exceeds the cost of the bounds. The
replication tables report standalone search and matched sums of fit,
extraction, search and restricted prediction time, including an optimized
Gain sweep and native full-forest prediction.

\section{Scope}
The method selects a single set of input measurements for a fitted numeric
tree ensemble and a specified calibration distribution. The greedy path can
miss a smaller feasible set, since two deletions may be feasible together
even when each fails separately. Source shifts also affect the usefulness of
matching the teacher. The observed advantage is a smaller global input set
than Gain at modest mean predictive cost; it narrows considerably when
the ranking measures single-feature deletion directly. The individual
calibration votes explain which costly calculations can be omitted. Their
number alone does not establish an advantage in elapsed time.
