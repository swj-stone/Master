\paragraph{Ties and strict ordering.}
For the forest voter, the continuous margin distinguished 265 of 274 integer-tied within-fold candidate pairs on the first panel and 274 of 278 on the wider panel. On the wider panel it also reversed 13 strictly ordered pairs. The zero-margin threshold agrees with three-of-four approval on every candidate. The two rankings can therefore disagree through either ties or reversals on the same side of the threshold. Only a disagreement affecting the delivered support can change the return of a single nomination.

To separate the two effects, we compared a third rule on the archived slates. The \emph{refined vote} maximizes the full continuous margin among candidates tied for the highest minimum vote. All three rules retain the same deterministic feature order for any remaining ties. Going from the original vote to its refinement changes only how maximum-vote ties are settled. Going from the refinement to the unrestricted full margin also allows a lower-vote candidate to win.

With a forest at both stages the full margin gained 3.44 points over the vote on the ten tasks ($[-4.67,15.00]$). Dry Bean gained 50.00 points within task, Steel lost 15.56 and eight tasks tied. Breaches rose from 3.33\% to 10.00\%. On the wider panel the gain was 5.49 points ($[0,11.05]$), from Ozone and Semeion, with a 16.67\% breach rate for each rule. On folds with both outer-passing and failing candidates, forest/full concordance against forest/vote was .678 versus .543 on the first panel (113 pairs, ten mixed folds, five tasks) and .728 versus .543 on the wider panel (128 pairs, eleven mixed folds, five tasks). The paired differences were .134 ($[-.061,.319]$) and .185 ($[.086,.284]$). Each passing--failing pair contributes one for correct order and half for a tie, averaged within mixed folds and tasks. All 46 folds contribute to $U$.

For this same forest/forest comparison, point selection attained the full margin's $U$ on \emph{every} fold at the primary tolerance. A fallback and a breached deletion both yield zero $U$. On Seeds and QSAR the two rules had different breach outcomes in opposite directions, despite identical overall breach rates. Relative to an unrestricted uniform nomination, the full margin gained 1.54 ($[-2.67,6.54]$) and 5.56 points ($[-3.05,13.83]$). Keeping the forest voter but switching the wider receiver to logistic gave the full margin and ordinal vote identical $U$. With a boosted-tree voter and the same logistic receiver the ordinal vote led by 8.33 points. Thresholding demonstrably coarsens the observed panel losses. Whether the coarsening matters to refit selection depends on the learner and the slate.

\subsection{Changing the candidate construction with the forest held fixed}
The first forest experiment changes the models, while retaining slates generated from boosted-tree Gain. We next used ANOVA $F$ scores estimated on the fitting partition to select the top half of the input features as a new anchor. The anchor had 2--21 features on the first panel and 36--428 on the wider panel. We removed $\max(1,\lfloor |A|/4\rfloor)$ columns. Each slate contained the $F$ prefix at that retained width and up to seven uniformly sampled neighbors, each exchanging a single retained column for another anchor column. The voter, guard and outer learner all used the same forest specification as before. We used the same folds, median replacement values, tolerances and rule for constructing restorations. The membership of each mask could change. Here the tail comprises the columns below the ANOVA anchor in $F$-score order. In the original design it comprises the split-used columns below the Gain anchor in boosted-tree order. The $F$-score anchor and local neighborhood together change the selection problem. The denominator in $U$ is now this new anchor's width.

We locked the first exchange draw before scoring its new fits. It contributed 138 candidate records on the ten tasks and 128 on the six wider tasks. We later fixed two further draw seeds and sealed their nominations before scoring, giving 565 distinct fold--support records in total. Averaging over draws within each fold gives results conditional on the same partitions and outcomes. The mean pairwise Jaccard overlap of candidate sets across draws was .775 on the first panel and .071 on the wider panel. The candidate slates were identical in 63 of 90 and zero of 48 within-fold draw pairs, respectively.

\paragraph{What did reversing a strict vote order buy?}
Table~\ref{tab:tier} decomposes the original vote-to-full comparison on all 46 original folds and 138 local fold--draw records. Changing the tie-break among the highest votes accounted for the entire observed difference in effective compression and outer breaches on \emph{each} record. Permitting a lower-vote candidate to win changed two further nominations. On the original Parkinson fold 0 the two returned supports both met the outer tolerances and removed 49.02\% of their anchor. Outer AUC was .9161 for the refined vote and .8753 for the unrestricted margin. On Steel fold 0, local draw 3, the guard rejected both nominees and returned the anchor. Continuous margins distinguish many pairs whose vote totals coincide, but the strict pair reversals reported above produced no further change in either decision outcome in this experiment. Other supports or a different loss, such as the delivered model's AUC, can distinguish the policies.

\paragraph{What does exact stopping save at the same decision?}
The first implementation stopped early on the ordinal minimum and evaluated continuous margins exhaustively. We repeated the search allowing exact early stopping for each rule. All methods visit candidates in the same archived order, with the empty restoration first. Within a nomination they share duplicate anchor and candidate masks, and each method starts with an empty cache. On all 184 slates, including the three local draws on the same folds, each stopped rule recovered its own exhaustive nominee. The refined rule takes two separate minima, then compares their pair. The two minima can occur at different restorations (Appendix~\ref{sec:transport-details}).

\paragraph{How a counterexample or invariance certificate is computed.}
Both the candidate refit $S$ and its anchor refit $A$ follow the same reassignment. As the assignment changes, the candidate score and the floor supplied by the anchor can both change. For class $c$ with $n_c$ positives among $n$ rows, let $R^S_{jc}$ and $R^A_{jc}$ be the ascending midranks of feature row $j$'s predicted probability of $c$ under the two fits. The ranks are fixed under any reassignment. With $\rho=.97$, the one-versus-rest AUC gap is
\begin{equation}
 G_c(\pi)=
 \frac{\sum_{i:y_i=c}\!\big(R^S_{\pi(i)c}-\rho R^A_{\pi(i)c}\big)
       -(1-\rho)n_c(n_c+1)/2}{n_c(n-n_c)}.
 \label{eq:assignmentgap}
\end{equation}
This follows by subtracting the two rank-sum AUC formulas~\cite{bamber1975area}. Use the positive class for binary AUC and average $G_c$ over classes for multiclass AUC. The accuracy gap is the average over $i$ of $\mathbf 1\{\hat y^S_{\pi(i)}=y_i\}-\rho\mathbf 1\{\hat y^A_{\pi(i)}=y_i\}$. Each gap is therefore an additive assignment objective within equivalence classes of the supplied outputs. A pass requires both gaps to be at least $-\epsilon$. Average precision was inactive on these folds. Two above-floor minima certify invariance of a pass, and a single below-floor maximum certifies invariance of a failure. To establish ambiguity we exhibit an assignment and rescore \emph{both} metrics against their reassigned anchor. Separate maxima may occur at different assignments and need not jointly witness a pass. Restricted searches supplied admissible witnesses. Bounds for invariance use the entire allowed class, including a conservative numerical margin of $10^{-13}$ around the decision tolerance. We revisited 26 initially inconclusive records with unrestricted within-class assignment.
