# Independent computational comparison on 18 previously opened datasets

All runs used source seed 42 and the same calibration rows, teacher, .02 excess-loss quota and greedy rule. The computational experiment used three independent banks of shared random permutations per dataset. Reversing each permutation gives its antithetic partner; the stated budget counts both. The smaller budgets are prefixes of the 128-permutation bank. Rows are paired within source and bank, and the percentile intervals below resample the 18 sources. None of these sources was reserved for algorithm selection on this objective.

For a centered target vector t and path increments a_g, the coefficient of a union U is the sum of the linear terms 2⟨t,a_g⟩ with g=U minus the sum of ⟨a_g,a_h⟩ over ordered pairs with g∪h=U. A term is lost on deleting any of its required features. Analytic risk uses the probability that a uniform size-s background contains U. Empirical risk uses the fraction of permutations where the highest rank in U is at most s. Both searches use the same union coefficients and incremental selector, retiring each union once. A separate direct numerator uses the calibration-row predictions for each nested mask; it agrees with the empirical union scores.

Selector time includes the Gram and the union dictionary, estimator-specific setup and the full deletion path. Fit-inclusive time adds the common fitted forest and traced calibration path matrix. It excludes source loading and compilation. The exact incremental union path also agreed with the original production path on every source. The mean numbers of extra columns for the 8/32/128-permutation estimators were .59/.41/.15. Mean held-out worst normalized excess was .0029/.0028/.0014 *lower* for the sampled estimators. These data establish an exact computational endpoint. They do not establish that this exact greedy policy predicts better than its sampled version.

A second native Monte Carlo solver avoids the Gram and union construction by maintaining residuals for each calibration row, permutation and coalition size. Its 12 searches on Coimbra, Retinopathy, Grid and CT location gave exactly the same empirical paths as the union-based estimator. For eight permutations it took 0.0007 s on Coimbra and 0.0185 s on Retinopathy, faster than the exact union selectors (0.0031 and 0.0441 s). It took 0.0068 s on Grid and 1.525 s on CT location, where the exact selectors took 0.0049 and 0.0086 s. At 128 permutations the Gram-free times for these four tasks were 0.0067, 0.3331, 0.1173 and 25.552 s. The cost depends strongly on calibration dimensions, union count and implementation. Measured speedups over sampled weights do not establish superiority over every Monte Carlo algorithm or over the pooled decision rule.
