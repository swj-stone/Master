# Independent review of the acquisition cases

## Cleveland charges

I checked the source and forecast archive hashes, the subject-row partitions, and the matched test rows in both feature universes. I read the binary outcome from the original `cleve.mod` records, grouped exact repeats of the 12 primary input fields, and independently encoded Turney's Table 17 in integer cents. On all 200 frozen forecast records, the listed physical support gave exactly the reported bundled fee. A separate rank-statistic calculation reproduced the reported AUC and accuracy to machine precision. These fee checks cover the cost of the reachable panel. I did not independently retraverse every patient path or reconstruct the Cleveland outer outage risks from scratch. The authors supply a separate deterministic replay of those forests. [Turney, Appendix A.2 and Table 17](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/project/jair/pub/volume2/turney95a.pdf) · [UCI Heart Disease](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/45/heart+disease) · [Archived forecasts](forecast-receipts.zip) · [Fold results](scores.csv)

On the five primary paired splits, MinPI + MDI reduced reachable inputs from 10.2 to 9.6 and mean bundled charges from CAD 210.47 to 208.616. The 0.88% reduction comprises two ties and reductions of CAD 1.00, 7.27 and 1.00. At matched acceptance, MDI + pooled cost CAD 207.162, with two outer quota violations versus one for MinPI. Exhaustively minimizing acceptance-sample fee yielded CAD 199.108 with two violations. Switching the terminal choice from minimum width to minimum fee changed zero of the 90 physical supports compared across both panels. The angiographic vessel-count sensitivity reuses the same 296 records. The historical charges combine Ontario's 1992 schedule with assigned nominal prices; Cleveland patients' bills were never observed. ## Wearable devices

The primary unit is a physical worn device. Individual channels and their two summaries stay together. I checked the two original archive hashes and reconstructed subject IDs and evaluation labels from the raw logs and segment filenames. For every fold the participant sets were disjoint, their union contained every participant, and the archived held row indices and forecast digest matched the receipt. Each subject was tested once. Every MHEALTH test subject had all 12 activities; only 1,251 of 4,859 complete five-second blocks met the steady, non-null criterion. All 9,120 DSADS segments were included, and each test subject had all 19 activities. Subjects recur across fitting and acceptance sets of different folds. The units have equal nominal cost and the stress masks are synthetic. [MHEALTH](https://doi.org/10.24432/C5TW22) · [Daily and Sports Activities](https://doi.org/10.24432/C5C59F) · [Registered folds, devices and comparators](../../../../experiments/minpi_rework/wearable_protocol.md)

I recalculated 216 held arm records from the sealed probability arrays, explicitly enumerating all equally weighted availability masks at each positive device count and using the held full forest's empty-input Brier loss for normalization. The maximum discrepancy was below $4\times10^{-16}$ across macro AUC, accuracy and worst normalized excess. The separate acceptance normalization uses the acceptance rows. All four frozen searches, including exhaustive enumeration, retained every device on every fold. Forcing one retirement failed the joint held guard on all 18 folds. This is negative evidence for frozen-forest device retirement under the specified tolerances. [Subject-level records](../wearable-groups/held.csv) · [Acceptance subsets](../wearable-groups/acceptance-subsets.csv)

Refitting every device subset on the same fitting subjects retired at least one device in 8/10 MHEALTH folds and 8/8 DSADS folds. Retirement and successful joint held checks coincided in **4/10** and **3/8** folds, respectively. Two further MHEALTH folds passed at full width. The held mean accuracy changes for unconstrained refitting were −0.0318 and −0.0087. The forced-width and clean-only refit arms address separate questions. ## Assessment

These cases improve specialist methods-paper credibility by testing the actual decision unit and an external fee schedule, including same-forest exhaustive checks and the option to retrain. They do not establish an advantage for MinPI on either physical-device dataset, prospective savings in healthcare, or a measured protective effect under real device failures. My broad, highly selective conference assessment remains weak reject on demonstrated impact. I would support a specialist evaluation of the exactly computable risk criterion with its practical failures visible. No additional modeling is necessary to report these cases accurately.
