# What did deleting a column save under an external fee schedule?

Cleveland is a retrospective diagnostic classification benchmark with a published acquisition-charge schedule. The UCI archive includes Turney's actual machine-readable price and group files. They use Canada's 1992 Ontario Health Insurance Program schedule, supplemented with nominal amounts for inexpensive intake fields and for additional interpretations of a procedure. Each selected test is billed at the marginal price plus one shared setup fee per included group. For example, measuring both fasting glucose and cholesterol costs CAD 10.37. Measuring both peak heart rate and the thallium reading costs 103.90. These are historical benchmark charges. They give no estimate of current bills, care costs, diagnostic benefit or test delays. [UCI dataset, attribution and license](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/45/heart+disease) · [Turney, Appendix A.2, Table 17](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/project/jair/pub/volume2/turney95a.pdf)

We used the 296 complete rows of the original 303-row `cleve.mod` benchmark. The primary analysis omits angiographic vessel count, which comes from the workup closely related to the diagnostic label. The 13-input sensitivity uses the same patients with the column restored. Five new stratified partitions assigned 141 observations to training, 47 to ranking, 48 to acceptance and 60 to outer scoring. Exact 12-feature duplicates were kept together. The budget rule, historical charges, endpoints and cost comparators were declared in `c4f680d`. All 200 forecasts on ten fitted forests were sealed in `823ec51` before score release. No patient-specific test recommendation is evaluated. ## Five-seed means

CAD is the cost of obtaining all distinct features referenced by one deployed model, including the shared-procedure discounts. Traversed CAD charges only distinct tests that the forest actually visits for the specific outer patient, with results available at every acquired split. The two approaches nearly coincide because a 100-tree forest usually queries most selected inputs on each case. The clean scores are computed on unmasked input. The last column counts how many of the five outer samples exceeded the .02 all-size proper-loss quota. The identical quota and clean acceptance criteria were applied to every selected support.

In the primary analysis MinPI added to MDI saved CAD 1.85 per model feature bundle (0.88%) relative to MDI. Three paired runs saved money and two tied. It saved CAD 0.40 relative to MDI plus the Brier benefit–cost path, but cost CAD 1.45 more than MDI plus pooled, with four ties and one more expensive run. Its saving versus the full forest was 6.48%. The exhaustive cost optimum saved a further CAD 9.51 relative to MDI + MinPI, and exceeded quota on two outer partitions. Replacing the width objective with the actual fee objective on the same finite paths did not change a single selected support over either feature universe. Adding vessel count raised all model charges and changed the relative ordering of the Brier comparator. Both panels use the same 296 patients and the five outer partitions overlap, so no cross-source interval or clinical generalization is justified.

The forward comparator follows cFS Algorithm 1, greedily adding the covariate with the largest positive gain per additional cost. The AIC version fits its logistic models on the fit and vote rows, using each source-coded feature as one coefficient. The Brier version ranks under the same frozen predictive model as MinPI using voting rows. Each charges the published marginal group fee. The acceptance guard and final predictions are always calculated with the shared frozen forest. This adapts cFS's cost objective to grouped fees and forest evaluation. It does not reproduce the source paper's logistic prediction experiment. Neither approach tunes a cost exponent. The exhaustive control evaluated all 4,096 and 8,192 subsets for the two feature universes, including the clean guard, to find the least costly structurally available panel on the acceptance rows. We independently checked its risk values and structural supports on 256 randomly chosen masks against direct computations. [Jagdhuber et al., cFS Algorithm 1](https://eldorado.tu-dortmund.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/3e6902fe-a461-48d0-be4e-8174a513f8e9/content)

**Referee finding.** The historical fee experiment shows small savings over MDI and no general economic advantage for the minimum. Counting only fields overstates its relative gain against MDI on the primary panel (9.6 versus 10.2 fields, yet less than one percent in fees). The observed clean differences and held quota counts separate the cost and quality trade-offs. The originally examined wide-source compression results remain relevant for counting acquired input channels under equal costs.

A separate post-release timing replay recomputed each of the ten fits three times with one numerical thread after warmup. Each fit included archive reading, training, all ranking and acceptance procedures, exhaustive cost selection, pricing and all 20 outer feature-only predictions. Median total times were .258 s for 12 inputs (range .183–.289) and .277 s for 13 inputs (.245–.306). The clock stopped before serialization and comparison with the archived records. All 600 arm predictions and all support and cost records reproduced exactly. These are joint experiment times; shared intermediates preclude attributing them to any single selector.
