## Recommendation

**Weak accept for a specialist data-mining methods track. Weak reject for a competitive broad ML main track.** The paper gives an exact answer to a concrete question about global input compression of a fixed shallow forest under partial availability. It supplies an efficient update rule, a verified implementation and an instructive limit on the interpretation of robustness to missing measurements. The retrospective pooled path matches the minimum path on retained width. I see a specialist contribution in exactly computing and searching this risk criterion. A general predictive or efficiency advantage in deployment remains unsupported. This is a judgment about scientific merit; the author must check any actual venue's submission requirements. ## Mathematical checks

* The signed excess identity follows by expanding squared error before averaging over masks. The minus sign on the ordered Gram terms is correct. The factor $p/s$ is essential to the claimed exchangeable-law equivalence. The result concerns a common mask law independent of example and label and a relative budget proportional to its expected available fraction. The manuscript states both restrictions. Structural closure preserves every reachable prefix on every input and further mask, including branches unobserved in calibration.
* The Clique reduction produces distinct-column cross terms of weight two, zero singleton weights and a null squared norm $100(M-b)$. At full availability its budget requires at least $\binom{k}{2}$ surviving edges. The supplied numerical example uses the same normalization. The result pertains to the allowed forest class and quota. It does not certify global optimality of the reported two-path selector.
* The first-excluded-split argument bounds each row's probability-weighted change in the forest predictions. The stated Brier and bounded-regression constants suffice. The union-bound/Hoeffding margin assumes fixed candidate paths independent of iid acceptance rows and future rows from the same population. Its two numeric examples reproduce. The reported procedure imposes the empirical constraint. There is no calibrated guarantee for the time-shifted data.
* For a fixed support, the conditional distribution of acquired split features under padding is hypergeometric. At a common independent availability rate $t$, size biasing the binomial size-$p$ law yields the Bernstein weights at degree $p-1$. Degree elevation takes convex combinations. For the individual order-$k$ term, coupling $k$ with-replacement draws to distinct draws bounds the all-marked probability error by the probability of a collision, at most $\binom{k}{2}/(p-1)$, capped at one. The triangle inequality remains valid for signed weights. This proves both sides of the bound and the claimed fixed-forest limit. The rational all-mask check independently verified the finite-universe mixture identity for signed set losses. The three-feature witness reverses the actual .02 decision when a single unused input is appended. [Diagnostic and proof](../predictive-universe/README.md)
* The extrema kernel encloses differences of its stored binary64 basis columns, handles signed coefficients, and falls back to direct scalar evaluation if the sign margin is unresolved. The guarantee is relative to the implementation's finite arithmetic and tie tolerance. I reran the executable parity test. It passed 1,950 curves and tolerance decisions, including 526 strict interior maxima and 493 nonunique maxima. Worst-case flat or oscillatory curves can still cost a grid scan.
