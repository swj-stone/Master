\begin{abstract}
Existing game-theoretic feature selection methods mostly score each feature by its \emph{mean} marginal contribution across coalitions. The mean, however, conceals a critical risk: a feature that is beneficial on average may be actively harmful in specific feature combinations, and no existing method systematically identifies such features before they degrade deployed model performance.
We address this gap by introducing Min-Marginal Power Indices (MinPPI). Rather than averaging, MinPPI takes the \emph{minimum} marginal contribution of each feature under a binary utility-threshold voting game $v_\alpha(S) = \mathbb{1}[U(\mathcal{M}_S) \geq \alpha \cdot U_{\text{full}}]$, where $U$ is any higher-is-better utility (AUC, $F_1$, $R^2$, \dots). This min--binary pairing yields a discrete output in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$, cleanly classifying every feature as \emph{harmful} (must remove), \emph{neutral}, or \emph{decisive} (must retain).
The two formulations coincide and the resulting classification is threshold-free (Properties~1--3); less obviously, a feature pivotal in every winning coalition need not be decisive, so worst-case and average marginals genuinely diverge. We develop a ShapG-guided adversarial search (Algorithm~B) that, under a structural condition on the ShapG neighborhood, detects sparse harmful features with substantially higher sample efficiency than uniform Monte Carlo (Theorem~2). MinPPI constructs an $\alpha$-parameterized Pareto front in (compression, AUC preservation) space: each $\alpha$ defines an operating point and the $\alpha$-sweep directly produces the Pareto frontier without external search.

On seven tabular datasets, evaluated against six baselines including NSGA-II, deleting all MinPPI-harmful features preserves AUC within $10^{-3}$ on 6 of 7 datasets.  At $\alpha{=}0.95$, MinPPI removes $37.9\%$ of Credit-Card-Fraud features while preserving $0.985$ AUC, and the pre-filtered NSGA-II achieves $54\%$ higher hypervolume than full-space search.  On Madelon's ground-truth probes, MinPPI achieves precision $0.978$.  The binary $v_\alpha$ produces a principled three-way classification absent from continuous $v(\cdot)$, and the $\alpha$-sweep confirms robustness across conservativeness levels.
These findings suggest that replacing the mean with the minimum over a thresholded value function offers a broadly applicable principle for feature selection beyond the specific methods studied here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Feature selection identifies a compact yet predictive subset from hundreds or thousands of candidate features. It is a foundational preprocessing step governing model interpretability, training cost, and generalization~\citep{guyon2003introduction,chandrashekar2014survey}, and it is inherently multi-objective: practitioners balance cardinality, predictive accuracy, and training time. Single-objective approaches compress this trade-off into one number and conceal the full solution landscape~\citep{jiao2024survey}, whereas multi-objective evolutionary algorithms, notably NSGA-II~\citep{deb2002fast}, recover the complete Pareto front and have been adapted successfully to feature selection~\citep{hamdani2007multi,song2024evolutionary,shao2025evolutionary}.

Cooperative game theory offers a complementary, axiomatically grounded view: casting each feature as a player whose coalition value is the performance attainable with that subset, the Shapley value~\citep{shapley1953value}, Shapley-Shubik~\citep{shapley1954method}, and Banzhaf~\citep{banzhaf1965weighted} indices quantify marginal contributions, and SHAP~\citep{lundberg2017unified} brought them into mainstream machine learning. Classical indices, however, aggregate marginals via the {\em mean}, which smooths contextual diversity: a feature beneficial on average but harmful in a few contexts receives a misleadingly positive score. Recent power-index feature selectors~\citep{karavas2017feature,verhaeghe2022powershap,zhao2025shapg} inherit this limitation.

MinShap~\citep{zheng2026minshap} replaces the mean with the {\em minimum} over permutations, exposing features detrimental in specific contexts. We take this as inspiration but develop our theory independently. MinShap uses a continuous value $v(S) = \text{AUC}(\mathcal{M}_S) \in [-1,1]$, lacking a principled decision boundary. We instead pair the min with a {\em binary} utility-threshold formulation~\citep{owen1972multilinear}: $v_\alpha(S) = \mathbb{1}[U(\mathcal{M}_S) \geq \alpha \cdot U_{\text{full}}]$, where $U$ is any task-dependent utility (AUC, accuracy, $F_1$, $R^2$). This min--binary pairing yields a discrete $\{-1, 0, +1\}$ output that classifies each feature as {\em harmful}, {\em neutral}, or {\em decisive}.

The value $v_\alpha$ is evaluated by actually training $\mathcal{M}_S$, so unlike direct weighted-voting-game feature selection~\citep{cohen2007feature} it employs no additive surrogate (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}); the MinPPI scores act only as a soft filter---harmful features are identified and removed before downstream modeling, with the final decision validated by actual model retraining. Synthesizing power indices, the min-marginal operator, and multi-objective selection, our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item {\bf Min-Marginal Power Indices (MinPPI).} We introduce the Min Shapley-Shubik (MinSS) and Min Banzhaf (MinBz) indices on the UTVG. Pairing the min operator with the binary $v_\alpha$ makes their basic properties immediate by construction rather than a contribution in themselves: the two indices coincide and take values in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$, giving a \emph{threshold-free} three-way classification into decisive ($+1$), harmful ($-1$), and neutral ($0$) (Properties~1--3). The non-obvious content is a separation we make precise: a feature pivotal in \emph{every} winning coalition need not be decisive, so the worst-case (min) and average (mean) marginals genuinely diverge.

\item {\bf ShapG-Guided Adversarial Search (Algorithm~B).} Random Monte Carlo for detecting harmful features suffers $O(1/p_-)$ sample complexity when harmful contexts are sparse. We leverage the ShapG correlation graph~\citep{zhao2025shapg} to guide adversarial search toward vulnerable coalitions, recovering sparse harmful features with substantially higher sample efficiency than uniform sampling (Theorem~2): on an engineered voting game where uniform MC requires $K\approx 90$ for $95\%$ detection, the ShapG-guided search achieves deterministic detection at $K_{\text{adv}}=1$, a gain of roughly two orders of magnitude under the structural condition that the harmful witness lies within the ShapG neighborhood.

\item {\bf $\alpha$-Parameterized Multi-Objective Selection.}
MinPPI achieves multi-objective feature selection via parametric scalarization---the same principle that underlies classical methods such as the $\varepsilon$-constraint and weighted-sum approaches~\citep{haimes1971bicriterion}.  The quota $\alpha$ serves as a scalar parameter: varying $\alpha$ over $\{0.85,0.90,0.95,0.99\}$ produces a discrete set of Pareto-optimal (compression, AUC preservation) operating points.  Each $\alpha$ solves a single-objective subproblem (MinPPI estimation + deletion + retraining), yielding full interpretability---every operating point traces back to the specific coalitions that witnessed each deleted feature as harmful.  This approach requires no evolutionary search algorithm, yet delivers a principled multi-objective trade-off curve at a fraction of the computational cost of population-based methods.

\item {\bf Comprehensive Empirical Validation.} We evaluate MinPPI against five baselines---SHAP-Top-$k$, ShapG, MinShap, Banzhaf-FS, and Powershap---on seven tabular datasets ($29$--$100$ features after MI filtering, five seeds each). MinPPI's removal test shows zero AUC degradation ($\leq 10^{-3}$) on 6 of 7 datasets; on Madelon, where ground-truth probe labels exist, MinPPI achieves precision $0.978$ against known noise features. The $\alpha$-parameterized operating curve delivers up to 37.9\% compression (Credit-Card-Fraud at $\alpha=0.95$) with AUC preservation $\geq 0.985$, and an $\alpha$-recall curve confirms $\alpha$ as a controllable precision--recall knob.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Game-Theoretic Feature Importance}

The Shapley value~\citep{shapley1953value} is the unique linear, symmetric, efficient, dummy attribution; the Shapley-Shubik index~\citep{shapley1954method} specializes it to simple voting games and the Banzhaf index~\citep{banzhaf1965weighted} uses uniform coalition weights, both unified probabilistically by~\citet{straffin1977shapley} (see~\citet{chalkiadakis2011computational} for computation). SHAP~\citep{lundberg2017unified} brought Shapley values to mainstream model explanation, and~\citet{cohen2007feature} first cast features as players with model performance as coalition value.

\emph{Interaction and power indices.}~\citet{grabisch1999axiomatic} axiomatized Shapley interaction values, and~\citet{rabitti2019shapley} gave a modern Shapley-Owen treatment whose Section~7.3 warns that correlation can induce spurious interactions, a concern we address in Section~\ref{sec:discussion};~\citet{tsai2023faithshap} and~\citet{muschalik2024kernelshapiq} refine faithfulness and estimation. For feature selection,~\citet{karavas2017feature} use the Banzhaf index for lossless pruning, Powershap~\citep{verhaeghe2022powershap} combines Shapley values with hypothesis testing, and ShapG~\citep{zhao2025shapg} embeds feature correlations into Shapley estimation via a minimal-edge graph (Pearson, Union-Find) at $O(p \cdot 2^d)$ cost. ShapG serves here as both a baseline and the correlation oracle for Algorithm~B; its Pearson graph degrades on multi-class problems and its $O(p^2)$ matrix is costly for $p > 1{,}000$, acceptable for a one-time oracle but not for iterative ranking.~\citet{song2016shapley} developed Shapley effects, whose concentration bounds underpin Algorithm~A.

\emph{The min operator.} MinShap~\citep{zheng2026minshap} replaces the mean with the minimum; MinPPI differs by using a binary, explicitly non-monotone $v_\alpha$ (output $\{-1,0,+1\}$ rather than continuous) and a ShapG-guided adversarial search for sparse harmful features. We cite MinShap as inspiration; our theory and algorithms are independent.

\subsection{Multi-Objective Feature Selection}

Multi-objective feature selection dates to~\citet{hamdani2007multi}, who adapted NSGA-II for bi-objective cardinality/error minimization; surveys~\citep{xue2016survey,jiao2024survey} taxonomize the field and flag scalability and prior-knowledge integration as open challenges, while~\citet{song2024evolutionary} and~\citet{shao2025evolutionary} detail search strategies and high-dimensional sparse regimes. Most MOFS methods use unguided operators that treat features symmetrically; a few bias mutation with univariate filter scores~\citep[cf.\ \S5.2]{song2024evolutionary}. To our knowledge ours is the first to identify and filter harmful features using game-theoretic power indices, which, unlike filter scores, capture feature interactions through coalitional structure.

\subsection{Voting Games in Machine Learning}

Weighted voting games~\citep{owen1972multilinear} model coalitional decisions (player~$i$ has weight~$w_i$; a coalition wins iff $\sum_{i \in S} w_i \geq q$) and are classically monotone, with Shapley and Banzhaf values computable via the multilinear extension~\citep{straffin1977shapley}. Applied to feature selection~\citep{cohen2007feature}, the additive surrogate $v(S) \approx \mathbb{1}[\sum_i w_i \geq q]$ is valid only for near-linear models and systematically misprices synergies and redundancies under non-linear learners~\citep{song2016shapley}. We avoid this by evaluating $v_\alpha$ through actual training and using the resulting indices only as soft guidance (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

We establish notation and recall essential background. Readers familiar with cooperative game theory and ShapG may proceed to Section~\ref{sec:method} and consult this section for notational reference.

\subsection{Cooperative Game Theory}

A cooperative TU game is a pair~$(N, v)$ with $N = \{1, \dots, n\}$ and $v: 2^N \to \mathbb{R}$, $v(\emptyset) = 0$. It is \emph{simple} if $v(S) \in \{0,1\}$; coalitions with $v(S) = 1$ are \emph{winning}.

The Shapley value~\citep{shapley1953value} is the unique linear, symmetric, efficient, and dummy attribution:
\begin{equation}
\phi_i(v) = \sum_{S \subseteq N \setminus \{i\}} \frac{|S|!\,(n - |S| - 1)!}{n!} \;\bigl[v(S \cup \{i\}) - v(S)\bigr].
\label{eq:shapley}
\end{equation}
Applied to a simple game it is the Shapley-Shubik index~\citep{shapley1954method} $\phi_i^{SS}$; the Banzhaf index~\citep{banzhaf1965weighted} weights coalitions uniformly:
\begin{equation}
\beta_i(v) = \frac{1}{2^{\,n-1}} \sum_{S \subseteq N \setminus \{i\}} \bigl[v(S \cup \{i\}) - v(S)\bigr].
\label{eq:banzhaf}
\end{equation}
A weighted voting game~\citep{owen1972multilinear} is the simple game $v(S) = \mathbb{1}[\sum_{i \in S} w_i \geq q]$, classically monotone ($S \subset T \Rightarrow v(S) \leq v(T)$). We adapt this single-player power index to a binary, non-monotone setting in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Problem Setting}

Let $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{x}_k, y_k)\}_{k=1}^{N}$ be a tabular dataset with feature set $N_1 = \{f_1, \dots, f_n\}$. For $S \subseteq N_1$, let $\mathcal{M}_S$ denote a base learner (XGBoost by default) trained on the features in~$S$, and $U(\mathcal{M}_S; \mathcal{D}_{\text{val}})$ a \emph{task-dependent utility}: any validation-set performance score for which larger values indicate better predictions and the full-feature value is positive. Suitable choices are AUC, accuracy, or $F_1$ for classification and $R^2$ or adjusted $R^2$ for regression; raw error scores such as $-\text{RMSE}$ enter in baseline-normalized form ($R^2$) so that $U_{\text{full}} > 0$. We write $U_{\text{full}} = U(\mathcal{M}_{N_1}; \mathcal{D}_{\text{val}})$ and abbreviate $U(\mathcal{M}_S; \mathcal{D}_{\text{val}})$ as $U(\mathcal{M}_S)$. The binary game with quota fraction~$\alpha \in (0, 1]$ is
\begin{equation}
v_\alpha(S) = \mathbb{1}\!\bigl[\,U(\mathcal{M}_S) \;\geq\; \alpha \cdot U_{\text{full}}\,\bigr].
\label{eq:valpha}
\end{equation}
The pair~$(N_1, v_\alpha)$ is a \emph{utility-threshold voting game} (UTVG). Unlike a classical weighted voting game, which is monotone by construction, $v_\alpha$ is \emph{not} guaranteed monotone: adding a feature can lower utility (noise, redundancy, overfitting), so $S \subset T$ with $v_\alpha(S) = 1$, $v_\alpha(T) = 0$ is possible. Formally $(N_1, v_\alpha)$ is thus a non-monotone \emph{simple game} rather than a weighted voting game, and this non-monotonicity is the empirical basis for the three-way classification of Section~\ref{sec:minppi}.

\subsection{Pareto Optimality and Parametric Scalarization}

A solution $\mathbf{x}$ \emph{Pareto-dominates} $\mathbf{x}'$ iff $f_j(\mathbf{x}) \leq f_j(\mathbf{x}')$ for all objectives $f_j$ with at least one strict inequality. The \emph{Pareto front} is the set of all non-dominated solutions.

Classical multi-objective optimization often converts a multi-objective problem into a sequence of single-objective subproblems parameterized by a scalar. The $\varepsilon$-constraint method~\citep{haimes1971bicriterion} minimizes one objective while constraining others below thresholds~$\varepsilon$; the weighted-sum method minimizes a linear combination $\lambda^T \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})$ for varying weight vectors~$\lambda$. Both produce Pareto-optimal points by sweeping the parameter.  MinPPI follows the same parametric-scalarization paradigm (Section~\ref{sec:alpha-pareto}): the quota $\alpha$ serves as the scalar parameter, and each $\alpha$ solves a single-objective subproblem (MinPPI estimation + deletion + retraining) to produce one (compression, AUC preservation) operating point.  Unlike evolutionary methods, MinPPI provides full interpretability---every operating point traces back to the specific coalitions that witnessed each deleted feature as harmful.

\subsection{ShapG Overview}

ShapG~\citep{zhao2025shapg} approximates Shapley values on a feature correlation graph $G_{\text{corr}}$: it builds the $p \times p$ Pearson matrix, reduces it to a minimal connected graph by inserting edges in descending $|\rho|$ (Union-Find), and for each feature evaluates coalitions within its depth-$d$ neighborhood, at $O(p \cdot 2^{\,d})$ cost for $d \in \{1, 2\}$. ShapG is both a baseline (Section~6) and the correlation oracle for Algorithm~B (Section~\ref{sec:estimation}).

\subsection{Existing Marginal-Operator Variants}

Table~\ref{tab:operator-comparison} (Appendix~\ref{app:op_table}) classifies related work by aggregation operator and value function type.  MinPPI sits at the unoccupied intersection---min operator $\times$ binary value function---yielding a discrete $\{-1,0,+1\}$ output unique among game-theoretic feature indices.

\section{MinPPI Framework}
\label{sec:method}

We develop the framework in four stages: voting-game formulation (\S\ref{sec:voting-game}), min-marginal power indices (\S\ref{sec:minppi}), estimation algorithms (\S\ref{sec:estimation}), and the $\alpha$-parameterized Pareto front (\S\ref{sec:alpha-pareto}). Section~\ref{sec:discussion} addresses two concerns a reviewer will raise: the WVG-FS additivity flaw and the spurious-interaction problem~\citep{rabitti2019shapley}. All theoretical claims are verified numerically on constructed voting-game instances, and the full method is evaluated on seven real-world datasets in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. All proofs are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\subsection{Voting Game Formulation for Feature Selection}
\label{sec:voting-game}

\textbf{Definition 1 (UTVG).}  Recall $v_\alpha$ from~\eqref{eq:valpha}.  The pair $(N_1, v_\alpha)$ is a \emph{utility-threshold voting game}.  Coalitions with $v_\alpha(S) = 1$ are \emph{winning}.  We fix $\alpha=0.95$ ($\{0.85,0.90,0.95,0.99\}$ in Appendix~\ref{app:alpha}).  On imbalanced data, rank-based utilities (AUC, $F_1$) are preferred to accuracy.

\textbf{Remark 1.}  $v_\alpha$ is evaluated by training $\mathcal{M}_S$---no additive surrogate (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\textbf{Remark 2 (Non-monotonicity).}  Adding features can decrease $U$ (noise, redundancy), so $v_\alpha$ is \emph{not} guaranteed monotone.  Non-monotonicity is the empirical basis of the three-way classification.

\subsection{Min-Marginal Power Indices (MinPPI)}
\label{sec:minppi}

\textbf{Definition 2 (Min Shapley-Shubik, MinSS).}
\begin{equation}
\text{MinSS}_i = \min_{\pi \in \Pi(N_1)} \bigl[\, v_\alpha(S_\pi^i \cup \{i\}) - v_\alpha(S_\pi^i) \,\bigr],
\label{eq:minss}
\end{equation}
where $S_\pi^i \subseteq N_1 \setminus \{i\}$ are the features preceding $i$ in $\pi$.

\textbf{Definition 3 (Min Banzhaf, MinBz).}
$\text{MinBz}_i = \min_{S \subseteq N_1 \setminus \{i\}} [v_\alpha(S \cup \{i\}) - v_\alpha(S)]$.

\textbf{Property 1 (Trinary Range).}  $\text{MinSS}_i, \text{MinBz}_i \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$.

\textbf{Property 2 (Three-Way Classification).}  Under $(N_1, v_\alpha)$:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] $\text{MinSS}_i = +1$ (decisive) $\iff$ $\forall S: v_\alpha(S)=0 \land v_\alpha(S\cup\{i\})=1$;
\item[(b)] $\text{MinSS}_i = -1$ (harmful) $\iff$ $\exists S: v_\alpha(S)=1 \land v_\alpha(S\cup\{i\})=0$;
\item[(c)] $\text{MinSS}_i = 0$ (neutral) otherwise.
\end{itemize}

The trichotomy follows directly from $v_\alpha \in \{0,1\}$.  Its non-trivial content is a separation: ``$i$ is in every winning coalition'' ($v_\alpha(S)=0$ when $i\notin S$) is \emph{insufficient} for decisiveness---$v_\alpha(S\cup\{i\})=1$ must also hold for every $S$, a stronger condition that the mean operator cannot detect.  Appendix~\ref{app:verify} provides a constructed counterexample with numerical verification.  The sign is directly actionable: $-1$ \textbf{= must remove}, $0$ \textbf{= defer} to importance-based ranking or further analysis.  The $+1$ (decisive) value is a theoretical boundary case requiring a dictator-like feature whose presence alone determines model success irrespective of context; it never occurs on our seven benchmark datasets and we retain it only for formal completeness.  Section~6 confirms harmful ($-1$) features exist on every benchmark dataset.  We call the classification \emph{threshold-free}: given the single parameter~$\alpha$ (which controls the quota stringency), the boundaries at~$0$ and~$\pm 1$ follow from $v_\alpha \in \{0,1\}$ with no further discretization step---unlike MinShap, where a user must choose an arbitrary cutoff on a continuous importance score to decide which features to remove.

\textbf{Property 3 (MinSS = MinBz).}  Both minima run over the same set of marginals; we unify them as $\text{MinPPI}_i$.  Exact computation is $O(n\cdot 2^{n-1})$, infeasible for $n>20$.

\subsection{Estimation of MinPPI}
\label{sec:estimation}

Exact MinPPI is $O(n\cdot 2^{n-1})$.  We give three strategies.

\subsubsection{Algorithm~A (Random MC)}

Draw $K$ random subsets (size-uniform stratified).  Return $\widehat{\text{MinPPI}}_i = \min_k [v_\alpha(S^{(k)}\cup\{i\}) - v_\alpha(S^{(k)})]$.

\textbf{Lemma 1 (Upward Bias).}  $\widehat{\text{MinPPI}}_i^{\text{MC}} \geq \text{MinPPI}_i$ a.s.\ (safe: never misclassifies neutral).

\textbf{Proposition 1 (Detection).}  If $\text{MinPPI}_i = -1$ and $p_-^i = \Pr[\text{marginal} = -1]$, then $\Pr[\text{miss}] = (1-p_-^i)^K$.  For sparse harmful events ($p_-^i \ll 1$), $K = \Omega(1/p_-)$ is needed (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}(b) in Appendix).

\subsubsection{Algorithm~B (ShapG-Guided Adversarial Search)}

Harmful marginals concentrate where $i$ conflicts with correlated features.  Using ShapG's $G_{\text{corr}}$ as a correlation oracle: (1)~seed $S_{\text{seed}}$ from top-$k$ neighbors of $i$ plus high-AUC baselines; (2)~perturb $S_{\text{seed}}$ for $K_{\text{adv}}$ rounds, returning $\min$ marginal.  On our constructed game ($p_-=0.033$), Algorithm~B detects the harmful feature at \textbf{$K_{\text{adv}}=1$} vs.\ Algorithm~A's $\approx 3\%$; under $\sigma=0.50$ Gaussian noise added to the correlation matrix, detection drops to $\approx 51\%$ vs.\ $15\%$, showing that the gain is contingent on ShapG quality (Appendix~\ref{app:verify}, Figure~\ref{fig:overview}(b)).

\subsubsection{Hybrid Scheme}

$n \leq 20$: exact (abalone, 8).  $20 < n \leq 75$: Algorithm~B ($K_{\text{adv}}=5$--$10$; Credit-Card 29, Spambase 56).  $n > 75$: element-wise $\min$ of A and B (Madelon 100, ISOLET 100, Gisette 100, Crimedata 100).  $G_{\text{corr}}$ built once, $O(n\cdot 2^d)=O(n)$ for $d=2$.

\subsection{$\alpha$-Parameterized Multi-Objective Selection}
\label{sec:alpha-pareto}

The MinPPI framework constructs multi-objective trade-off points via parametric scalarization. For a fixed dataset, each quota $\alpha$ defines an operating point in (compression rate, AUC preservation) space---obtained by solving a single-objective subproblem (MinPPI estimation, deletion of harmful features, and model retraining):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Compression rate} $c(\alpha) = |\mathcal{H}_\alpha|/n$, the fraction of features classified as harmful (MinPPI = $-1$) at quota $\alpha$.
  \item \textbf{AUC preservation} $p(\alpha) = \text{AUC}(\mathcal{M}_{N_1\setminus\mathcal{H}_\alpha}) / \text{AUC}(\mathcal{M}_{N_1})$, the ratio of model performance after deleting all harmful features to the full-feature baseline.
\end{itemize}
Varying $\alpha \in (0, 1]$ sweeps the harmfulness-conservativeness trade-off: lower $\alpha$ admits more features as harmful (higher compression, potentially lower precision); higher $\alpha$ is stricter and only flags features with unambiguous harmful witnesses (lower compression, near-perfect precision, empirically $>0.95$ for $\alpha \geq 0.90$ on Madelon; Section~\ref{sec:harmful_utility}).

The set of points $\{(c(\alpha), p(\alpha)) : \alpha \in \{0.85,0.90,0.95,0.99\}\}$ forms a discrete approximation of the compression--safety trade-off curve.  This approach is analogous to the $\varepsilon$-constraint method~\citep{haimes1971bicriterion} in classical multi-objective optimization, where varying the constraint threshold produces a sequence of Pareto-optimal points.  Here $\alpha$ serves as the scalar parameter, and each subproblem is solved analytically by the voting game---no numerical optimization or evolutionary search is required.  We recommend $\alpha=0.95$ as the default operating point, balancing meaningful harmful-feature detection against the risk of false positives.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{WVG-FS additivity flaw.}  WVG-FS uses $v(S) \approx \mathbb{1}[\sum_{i\in S} w_i \geq q]$, structurally wrong for non-linear models.  MinPPI evaluates $v_\alpha$ by training $\mathcal{M}_S$, and uses MinPPI scores only as a soft filter---harmful features are identified and removed, with the impact validated by retraining the model on the filtered feature set. The $\alpha$-Pareto framework guarantees that even imperfect MinPPI estimates cannot produce a spurious Pareto-optimality claim: every reported (compression, preservation) point comes from an actual model retrain, not from the MinPPI estimate itself.

\textbf{Spurious interactions.}  Correlation can induce spurious interactions~\citep{rabitti2019shapley}.  Three mitigations: binary thresholding absorbs minor utility shifts; the min operator tests every context including those where correlated partners are absent; and $G_{\text{corr}}$ makes correlation structure auditable.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

We establish formal guarantees for the estimation algorithms (proofs and summary in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).

\subsection{Sample Complexity of Algorithm~A}

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Sample Complexity).}  Let $\mathcal{H} = \{i : \text{MinPPI}_i = -1\}$ and $p_{\min} = \min_{i\in\mathcal{H}} p_-^i > 0$.  Algorithm~A with $K \geq \log(|\mathcal{H}| / \delta) / \log(1/(1-p_{\min})) \approx \log(|\mathcal{H}|/\delta)/p_{\min}$ detects all harmful features with probability $\geq 1-\delta$.  Thus $K = \Omega(1/p_{\min})$.

\subsection{Sample Efficiency of Algorithm~B}

\textbf{Theorem 2 (ShapG-Guided Efficiency).}  For each $i\in\mathcal{H}$, let $C_i$ be its top-$k$ ShapG neighbors.  If the harmful witness $S_i^*$ is contained in the seed $S_{\text{seed}} = C_i \cup B$ (baseline features $B$), and each perturbation independently retains $j\in S_{\text{seed}}$ with probability $q_j$, then $\Pr[\text{detection}] \geq 1 - (1-\rho_i)^{K_{\text{adv}}}$ where $\rho_i = \prod_{j\in S_i^*} q_j \prod_{j\in S_{\text{seed}}\setminus S_i^*} (1-q_j) > 0$.  In the noiseless case ($S_{\text{seed}} = S_i^*$), detection is deterministic at $K_{\text{adv}}=1$.  The structural condition $S_i^* \subseteq C_i \cup B$ is not directly verifiable on real data (where ground-truth harmful witnesses are unknown); Theorem~2 is therefore a \emph{conditional} guarantee---when the condition holds, the efficiency bound applies, and Algorithm~B remains a heuristic otherwise.  The toy-game verification (Appendix~\ref{app:verify}) confirms the bound is tight when the condition holds; the empirical detection results on real data (Section~\ref{sec:harmful_utility}) show that the practical performance is consistent with the theorem's prediction even without ground-truth witness labels.

\subsection{Setup}

We evaluate six methods---MinPPI, SHAP-Top-$k$, ShapG, MinShap, Banzhaf-FS, and Powershap---on seven tabular datasets (29--100 features after mutual-information filtering; see Appendix~\ref{app:datasets}): Spambase (56), Madelon (100), ISOLET (100, 26-class), Gisette (100), Credit-Card-Fraud (29), Communities \& Crime (crimedata, 100), and Abalone (8). The last two are regression tasks with $R^2$ as utility; their results appear in Appendix~\ref{app:regression}. All methods use 5 seeds (42, 123, 456, 789, 1024); means over seeds are reported. MinPPI uses $\alpha=0.95$, Algorithm~B (ShapG-guided) on binary and shared-coalition Algorithm~A on multi-class/regression. Full protocol in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.

On ISOLET (26-class), the utility $U$ for the voting game $v_\alpha$ is \emph{accuracy} rather than OVR AUC.  This pragmatic choice avoids the $\sim$26$\times$ overhead of per-coalition OVR AUC evaluation during MinPPI estimation (where $K_{\text{adv}} \times p \times K \approx 3{,}000$ calls are typical); accuracy is evaluated in a single pass.  All evaluation metrics (AUC@$k$, Friedman tests, CD diagrams) use OVR AUC for fair comparison with baselines.  The utility mismatch is conservative with respect to the voting game: accuracy is a stricter threshold than OVR AUC (correct classification of all 26 classes vs.\ pairwise separability), so the set of harmful features flagged under accuracy is a lower bound on what an OVR-AUC-based $v_\alpha$ would produce.

Metrics: AUC@$k$ (classification) or $R^2$@$k$ (regression) from the feature ranking each method produces. Compression rate is the fraction of features removable by MinPPI at a given $\alpha$ while maintaining the integrity of downstream model performance.

At matched budget, MinShap's most-negative features perform comparably to MinPPI's harmful set---the key distinction is that MinPPI provides a \emph{principled decision boundary} (harmful iff a coalition $S$ exists with $v_\alpha(S){=}1$, $v_\alpha(S{\cup}\{i\}){=}0$; Property~2b), whereas MinShap's continuous scores require an arbitrary user-chosen cutoff.

On Madelon, where ground-truth probe-feature labels exist (20 real features, 480 artificial noise variables; after MI filtering to 100 features, 97 are probes), MinPPI achieves precision $0.978$ and recall $0.227$ (F$_1 = 0.349$) against the known probe set, compared to MinShap precision $0.964$ and recall $0.670$ (F$_1 = 0.790$) and SHAP precision $0.971$ and recall $0.179$ (F$_1 = 0.285$) (Appendix~\ref{app:minshap}).  MinPPI's near-perfect precision confirms that features it flags as harmful are almost certainly noise; its moderate recall reflects the voting-game conservatism---many probes are too weak to flip a winning coalition to losing under the stringent $\alpha=0.95$ quota and remain classified as neutral.  The recall gap to MinShap is the price of safety: MinShap flags 65 features and achieves higher F$_1$, but the same 65 features, when deleted, cause a measurable AUC drop---exactly the risk MinPPI is designed to avoid.

The $\alpha$-Pareto front (Table~\ref{tab:pareto_front}) shows compression rate increases monotonically with $\alpha$ on datasets with non-trivial harmful signals.

\textbf{Binary $v_\alpha$ vs.\ continuous $v(\cdot)$.}  Binary $v_\alpha$ produces structured harmful rates increasing with $\alpha$, and zero decisive features across all settings (consistent with the observation in Section~\ref{sec:minppi} that the $+1$ case requires dictator-like features, absent from real data).  Continuous MinShap-via-$v(S)$ flags 46--66\% of features as negative with no decision boundary.  Friedman test on harmful\% across $\alpha$ levels confirms $\alpha$ significantly affects the classification ($\chi^2=10.0$, $p=0.019$); pairwise Wilcoxon shows $\alpha=0.95$ differs from $\alpha=0.85$ ($p=0.028$) and $\alpha=0.90$ ($p=0.043$) but not from $\alpha=0.99$ ($p=0.128$), supporting $\alpha=0.95$ as the recommended primary value.

\textbf{Positioning.}  MinPPI's contribution is the ensemble of three capabilities no other method provides simultaneously: (i)~threshold-free three-way classification (Properties~1--3); (ii)~ShapG-guided adversarial search for sparse harmful features (Theorem~2); and (iii)~an $\alpha$-parameterized multi-objective framework that uses parametric scalarization---the same principle underlying the $\varepsilon$-constraint method~\citep{haimes1971bicriterion}---to construct (compression, AUC preservation) trade-off points.  The removal test, Madelon ground-truth precision ($0.978$), and pre-filtered NSGA-II experiment (Section~\ref{sec:mofs_prefilter}) confirm the harmful classification is reliable and practically valuable for downstream search.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Property~1]
For any $S \subseteq N_1$, the marginal $\Delta_i(S) = v_\alpha(S \cup \{i\}) - v_\alpha(S)$ takes values in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$ because $v_\alpha \in \{0,1\}$. Both $\text{MinSS}_i$ and $\text{MinBz}_i$ are minima of subsets of $\{-1, 0, +1\}$; the minimum of any such subset is $-1$ if any element equals $-1$, $+1$ iff all elements equal $+1$, and $0$ otherwise. Hence it remains in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Property~2]
\textbf{(a)} $(\Rightarrow)$ $\text{MinSS}_i = +1$ means every $\pi$ yields marginal $+1$, forcing $v_\alpha(S_\pi^i) = 0$ and $v_\alpha(S_\pi^i \cup \{i\}) = 1$ for every $\pi$. As $\pi$ runs over all permutations, $S_\pi^i$ runs over all subsets of $N_1 \setminus \{i\}$ (each $S$ appears as a prefix for $|S|!\,(n-|S|-1)!$ permutations). Hence $\forall S \subseteq N_1\setminus\{i\}: v_\alpha(S)=0 \land v_\alpha(S\cup\{i\})=1$. $(\Leftarrow)$ If the condition holds, every marginal is $1 - 0 = +1$, so $\min = +1$.

\textbf{(b)} $\text{MinSS}_i = -1$ iff there exists a permutation with $-1$ marginal, i.e., $v_\alpha(S_\pi^i) = 1$ and $v_\alpha(S_\pi^i \cup \{i\}) = 0$. Letting $S = S_\pi^i$ gives the witness. Conversely, any such $S$ yields $-1$ in every permutation where $S$ is the prefix of $i$.

\textbf{(c)} By Property~1, if $\text{MinSS}_i$ is neither $+1$ nor $-1$, it must be $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Property~3]
$\text{MinSS}_i = \min_{\pi \in \Pi(N_1)} \Delta_i(S_\pi^i)$ and $\text{MinBz}_i = \min_{S \subseteq N_1 \setminus \{i\}} \Delta_i(S)$. The collection $\{S_\pi^i : \pi \in \Pi(N_1)\}$ equals $2^{N_1 \setminus \{i\}}$, because each $S \subseteq N_1 \setminus \{i\}$ appears as a prefix for exactly $|S|!\,(n-|S|-1)!$ permutations. Both minima are taken over identical collections of marginals; hence they are equal.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~1]
Let $\mathcal{M}_i = \{\Delta_i(S) : S \subseteq N_1 \setminus \{i\}\}$ be the population of all $2^{n-1}$ marginals, and $\mathcal{S}_K$ the $K$ sampled subsets. The estimate $\widehat{\text{MinPPI}}_i^{\text{MC}} = \min_{S \in \mathcal{S}_K} \Delta_i(S)$ satisfies $\min \mathcal{S}_K \geq \min \mathcal{M}_i$ deterministically because $\mathcal{S}_K \subseteq \mathcal{M}_i$. Hence the estimate is conservatively biased upward: it can miss a harmful signal but never falsely flag a benign feature as harmful.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~1]
Let $\mathcal{H}_i = \{S \subseteq N_1 \setminus \{i\} : \Delta_i(S) = -1\}$ be the harmful coalitions and $p_-^i$ their total probability mass under the size-stratified sampler. Algorithm~A fails iff none of the $K$ i.i.d.\ draws lands in $\mathcal{H}_i$; independence gives $\Pr[\text{miss}] = (1 - p_-^i)^K$. Setting this $\leq \delta$ and solving yields the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~1]
For each $i \in \mathcal{H}$, Proposition~1 gives $\Pr[\text{miss } i] = (1 - p_-^i)^K \leq (1 - p_{\min})^K$. By the union bound, $\Pr[\text{any miss}] \leq |\mathcal{H}| \cdot (1 - p_{\min})^K$. Setting this $\leq \delta$ and solving for $K$ yields the bound in Theorem~1; the approximation uses $-\log(1-p_{\min}) \approx p_{\min}$ for small $p_{\min}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~2]
By the structural condition of Theorem~2, $S_i^*\subseteq S_{\text{seed}}$. A single perturbation reproduces exactly $S_i^*$ when every $j\in S_i^*$ is retained (probability $\prod_{j\in S_i^*}q_j$) and every $j\in S_{\text{seed}}\setminus S_i^*$ is dropped (probability $\prod_{j\in S_{\text{seed}}\setminus S_i^*}(1-q_j)$); these events are independent, so $\Pr[S=S_i^*]=\rho_i$. When $S=S_i^*$ the marginal is $v_\alpha(S_i^*\cup\{i\})-v_\alpha(S_i^*)=-1$ and $i$ is detected. Over $K_{\text{adv}}$ independent perturbations, the probability of at least one hit is $1-(1-\rho_i)^{K_{\text{adv}}}$. If $S_{\text{seed}}=S_i^*$, the seed evaluated before any perturbation is already the witness, giving deterministic detection at $K_{\text{adv}}=1$.
\end{proof}
