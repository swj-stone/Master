\begin{abstract}
Existing game-theoretic feature selection methods mostly score each feature by its \emph{mean} marginal contribution across coalitions. The mean, however, conceals a critical risk: a feature that is beneficial on average may be actively harmful in specific feature combinations, and no existing method systematically identifies such features before they degrade deployed model performance.
We address this gap by introducing Min-Marginal Power Indices (MinPPI). Rather than averaging, MinPPI takes the \emph{minimum} marginal contribution of each feature under a binary utility-threshold voting game $v_\alpha(S) = \mathbb{1}[U(\mathcal{M}_S) \geq \alpha \cdot U_{\text{full}}]$, where $U$ is any higher-is-better utility (AUC, $F_1$, $R^2$, \dots). This min--binary pairing yields a discrete output in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$, cleanly classifying every feature as \emph{harmful} (must remove), \emph{neutral}, or \emph{decisive} (must retain).
The two formulations coincide and the resulting classification is parameter-free (Properties~1--3); less obviously, a feature pivotal in every winning coalition need not be decisive, so worst-case and average marginals genuinely diverge. We develop a ShapG-guided adversarial search (Algorithm~B) that, under a structural condition on the ShapG neighborhood, detects sparse harmful features with substantially higher sample efficiency than uniform Monte Carlo (Theorem~2). Embedding MinPPI into the mutation and crossover operators of NSGA-II yields the MOFS-MinPPI algorithm. Selection is always deferred to the true training-based objective, so MinPPI error cannot create spurious Pareto dominance: the returned front of any finite run is internally non-dominated (Proposition~2a), and it converges almost surely to the true Pareto front as the generation budget increases (Proposition~2b).
On five tabular datasets (29--200 features), MOFS-MinPPI is competitive with seven baselines---SHAP, ShapG, MinShap, Banzhaf, Powershap, MOFS-SHAP, and NSGA2-uniform---on top-$k$ AUC, while its Pareto-front hypervolume and compression rate at $1\%/3\%/5\%$ AUC-loss thresholds match or exceed the strongest NSGA-II baselines. Ablation confirms that the min operator yields better top-$k$ predictive performance than the mean, that binary $v_\alpha$ produces a principled three-way classification absent from continuous $v(\cdot)$, and that MinPPI guidance ($\beta>0$) improves Pareto-front quality over unguided search.
These findings suggest that replacing the mean with the minimum over a thresholded value function offers a broadly applicable principle for robust feature selection beyond the specific methods studied here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Feature selection identifies a compact yet predictive subset from hundreds or thousands of candidate features. It is a foundational preprocessing step governing model interpretability, training cost, and generalization~\citep{guyon2003introduction,chandrashekar2014survey}, and it is inherently multi-objective: practitioners balance cardinality, predictive accuracy, and training time. Single-objective approaches compress this trade-off into one number and conceal the full solution landscape~\citep{jiao2024survey}, whereas multi-objective evolutionary algorithms, notably NSGA-II~\citep{deb2002fast}, recover the complete Pareto front and have been adapted successfully to feature selection~\citep{hamdani2007multi,song2024evolutionary,shao2025evolutionary}.

Cooperative game theory offers a complementary, axiomatically grounded view: casting each feature as a player whose coalition value is the performance attainable with that subset, the Shapley value~\citep{shapley1953value}, Shapley-Shubik~\citep{shapley1954method}, and Banzhaf~\citep{banzhaf1965weighted} indices quantify marginal contributions, and SHAP~\citep{lundberg2017unified} brought them into mainstream machine learning. Classical indices, however, aggregate marginals via the {\em mean}, which smooths contextual diversity: a feature beneficial on average but harmful in a few contexts receives a misleadingly positive score. Recent power-index feature selectors~\citep{karavas2017feature,verhaeghe2022powershap,zhao2025shapg} inherit this limitation.

MinShap~\citep{zheng2026minshap} replaces the mean with the {\em minimum} over permutations, exposing features detrimental in specific contexts. We take this as inspiration but develop our theory independently. MinShap uses a continuous value $v(S) = \text{AUC}(\mathcal{M}_S) \in [-1,1]$, lacking a principled decision boundary. We instead pair the min with a {\em binary} utility-threshold formulation~\citep{owen1972multilinear}: $v_\alpha(S) = \mathbb{1}[U(\mathcal{M}_S) \geq \alpha \cdot U_{\text{full}}]$, where $U$ is any task-dependent utility (AUC, accuracy, $F_1$, $R^2$). This min--binary pairing yields a discrete $\{-1, 0, +1\}$ output that classifies each feature as {\em harmful}, {\em neutral}, or {\em decisive}.

The value $v_\alpha$ is evaluated by actually training $\mathcal{M}_S$, so unlike direct weighted-voting-game feature selection~\citep{cohen2007feature} it employs no additive surrogate (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}); the MinPPI scores act only as soft guidance for NSGA-II, with Pareto-optimality decided by true model fitness. Synthesizing power indices, the min-marginal operator, and multi-objective search, our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item {\bf Min-Marginal Power Indices (MinPPI).} We introduce the Min Shapley-Shubik (MinSS) and Min Banzhaf (MinBz) indices on the UTVG. Pairing the min operator with the binary $v_\alpha$ makes their basic properties immediate by construction rather than a contribution in themselves: the two indices coincide and take values in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$, giving a \emph{parameter-free} three-way classification into decisive ($+1$), harmful ($-1$), and neutral ($0$) (Properties~1--3). The non-obvious content is a separation we make precise: a feature pivotal in \emph{every} winning coalition need not be decisive, so the worst-case (min) and average (mean) marginals genuinely diverge.

\item {\bf ShapG-Guided Adversarial Search (Algorithm~B).} Random Monte Carlo for detecting harmful features suffers $O(1/p_-)$ sample complexity when harmful contexts are sparse. We leverage the ShapG correlation graph~\citep{zhao2025shapg} to guide adversarial search toward vulnerable coalitions, recovering sparse harmful features with substantially higher sample efficiency than uniform sampling (Theorem~2): on an engineered voting game where uniform MC requires $K\approx 90$ for $95\%$ detection, the ShapG-guided search achieves deterministic detection at $K_{\text{adv}}=1$, a gain of roughly two orders of magnitude under the structural condition that the harmful witness lies within the ShapG neighborhood.

\item {\bf MOFS-MinPPI Algorithm.} We embed MinPPI into NSGA-II operators: harmful features receive elevated mutation probability biased toward removal; decisive features are protected. Guidance strength is controlled by a single parameter~$\beta$, with $\beta = 0$ recovering the unguided baseline.

\item {\bf Comprehensive Empirical Validation.} We evaluate MinPPI-MOFS against seven baselines on five tabular datasets ($29$--$200$ features, five seeds each). Performance is measured via Pareto-front hypervolume (HV), compression rate at $1\%/3\%/5\%$ AUC-loss thresholds, and training-time speedup. Ablation isolates the min vs.\ mean operator, binary vs.\ continuous $v(\cdot)$, and guidance strength~$\beta$, with statistical significance via the Friedman--Nemenyi test.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Game-Theoretic Feature Importance}

The Shapley value~\citep{shapley1953value} is the unique linear, symmetric, efficient, dummy attribution; the Shapley-Shubik index~\citep{shapley1954method} specializes it to simple voting games and the Banzhaf index~\citep{banzhaf1965weighted} uses uniform coalition weights, both unified probabilistically by~\citet{straffin1977shapley} (see~\citet{chalkiadakis2011computational} for computation). SHAP~\citep{lundberg2017unified} brought Shapley values to mainstream model explanation, and~\citet{cohen2007feature} first cast features as players with model performance as coalition value.

\emph{Interaction and power indices.}~\citet{grabisch1999axiomatic} axiomatized Shapley interaction values, and~\citet{rabitti2019shapley} gave a modern Shapley-Owen treatment whose Section~7.3 warns that correlation can induce spurious interactions, a concern we address in Section~\ref{sec:discussion};~\citet{tsai2023faithshap} and~\citet{muschalik2024kernelshapiq} refine faithfulness and estimation. For feature selection,~\citet{karavas2017feature} use the Banzhaf index for lossless pruning, Powershap~\citep{verhaeghe2022powershap} combines Shapley values with hypothesis testing, and ShapG~\citep{zhao2025shapg} embeds feature correlations into Shapley estimation via a minimal-edge graph (Pearson, Union-Find) at $O(p \cdot 2^d)$ cost. ShapG serves here as both a baseline and the correlation oracle for Algorithm~B; its Pearson graph degrades on multi-class problems and its $O(p^2)$ matrix is costly for $p > 1{,}000$, acceptable for a one-time oracle but not for iterative ranking.~\citet{song2016shapley} developed Shapley effects, whose concentration bounds underpin Algorithm~A.

\emph{The min operator.} MinShap~\citep{zheng2026minshap} replaces the mean with the minimum; MinPPI differs by using a binary, explicitly non-monotone $v_\alpha$ (output $\{-1,0,+1\}$ rather than continuous) and a ShapG-guided adversarial search for sparse harmful features. We cite MinShap as inspiration; our theory and algorithms are independent.

\subsection{Multi-Objective Feature Selection}

Multi-objective feature selection dates to~\citet{hamdani2007multi}, who adapted NSGA-II for bi-objective cardinality/error minimization; surveys~\citep{xue2016survey,jiao2024survey} taxonomize the field and flag scalability and prior-knowledge integration as open challenges, while~\citet{song2024evolutionary} and~\citet{shao2025evolutionary} detail search strategies and high-dimensional sparse regimes. Most MOFS methods use unguided operators that treat features symmetrically; a few bias mutation with univariate filter scores~\citep[cf.\ \S5.2]{song2024evolutionary}. To our knowledge ours is the first to guide MOFS operators with game-theoretic power indices, which, unlike filter scores, capture feature interactions through coalitional structure.

\subsection{Voting Games in Machine Learning}

Weighted voting games~\citep{owen1972multilinear} model coalitional decisions (player~$i$ has weight~$w_i$; a coalition wins iff $\sum_{i \in S} w_i \geq q$) and are classically monotone, with Shapley and Banzhaf values computable via the multilinear extension~\citep{straffin1977shapley}. Applied to feature selection~\citep{cohen2007feature}, the additive surrogate $v(S) \approx \mathbb{1}[\sum_i w_i \geq q]$ is valid only for near-linear models and systematically misprices synergies and redundancies under non-linear learners~\citep{song2016shapley}. We avoid this by evaluating $v_\alpha$ through actual training and using the resulting indices only as soft guidance (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

We establish notation and recall essential background. Readers familiar with cooperative game theory, NSGA-II, and ShapG may proceed to Section~\ref{sec:method} and consult this section for notational reference.

\subsection{Cooperative Game Theory}

A cooperative TU game is a pair~$(N, v)$ with $N = \{1, \dots, n\}$ and $v: 2^N \to \mathbb{R}$, $v(\emptyset) = 0$. It is \emph{simple} if $v(S) \in \{0,1\}$; coalitions with $v(S) = 1$ are \emph{winning}.

The Shapley value~\citep{shapley1953value} is the unique linear, symmetric, efficient, and dummy attribution:
\begin{equation}
\phi_i(v) = \sum_{S \subseteq N \setminus \{i\}} \frac{|S|!\,(n - |S| - 1)!}{n!} \;\bigl[v(S \cup \{i\}) - v(S)\bigr].
\label{eq:shapley}
\end{equation}
Applied to a simple game it is the Shapley-Shubik index~\citep{shapley1954method} $\phi_i^{SS}$; the Banzhaf index~\citep{banzhaf1965weighted} weights coalitions uniformly:
\begin{equation}
\beta_i(v) = \frac{1}{2^{\,n-1}} \sum_{S \subseteq N \setminus \{i\}} \bigl[v(S \cup \{i\}) - v(S)\bigr].
\label{eq:banzhaf}
\end{equation}
A weighted voting game~\citep{owen1972multilinear} is the simple game $v(S) = \mathbb{1}[\sum_{i \in S} w_i \geq q]$, classically monotone ($S \subset T \Rightarrow v(S) \leq v(T)$). We adapt this single-player power index to a binary, non-monotone setting in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{Problem Setting}

Let $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{x}_k, y_k)\}_{k=1}^{N}$ be a tabular dataset with feature set $N_1 = \{f_1, \dots, f_n\}$. For $S \subseteq N_1$, let $\mathcal{M}_S$ denote a base learner (XGBoost by default) trained on the features in~$S$, and $U(\mathcal{M}_S; \mathcal{D}_{\text{val}})$ a \emph{task-dependent utility}: any validation-set performance score for which larger values indicate better predictions and the full-feature value is positive. Suitable choices are AUC, accuracy, or $F_1$ for classification and $R^2$ or adjusted $R^2$ for regression; raw error scores such as $-\text{RMSE}$ enter in baseline-normalized form ($R^2$) so that $U_{\text{full}} > 0$. We write $U_{\text{full}} = U(\mathcal{M}_{N_1}; \mathcal{D}_{\text{val}})$ and abbreviate $U(\mathcal{M}_S; \mathcal{D}_{\text{val}})$ as $U(\mathcal{M}_S)$. The binary game with quota fraction~$\alpha \in (0, 1]$ is
\begin{equation}
v_\alpha(S) = \mathbb{1}\!\bigl[\,U(\mathcal{M}_S) \;\geq\; \alpha \cdot U_{\text{full}}\,\bigr].
\label{eq:valpha}
\end{equation}
The pair~$(N_1, v_\alpha)$ is a \emph{utility-threshold voting game} (UTVG). Unlike a classical weighted voting game, which is monotone by construction, $v_\alpha$ is \emph{not} guaranteed monotone: adding a feature can lower utility (noise, redundancy, overfitting), so $S \subset T$ with $v_\alpha(S) = 1$, $v_\alpha(T) = 0$ is possible. Formally $(N_1, v_\alpha)$ is thus a non-monotone \emph{simple game} rather than a weighted voting game, and this non-monotonicity is the empirical basis for the three-way classification of Section~\ref{sec:minppi}.

\subsection{NSGA-II Background}

Multi-objective optimization minimizes $\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}) = (f_1, \dots, f_m)$ under Pareto dominance ($\mathbf{x} \prec \mathbf{x}'$ iff $f_j(\mathbf{x}) \leq f_j(\mathbf{x}')$ for all $j$, strict for some), whose image is the Pareto front. NSGA-II~\citep{deb2002fast} combines non-dominated sorting (fronts $\mathcal{F}_1, \mathcal{F}_2, \dots$) with crowding distance for within-front diversity. Encoding a subset as a binary mask $\mathbf{m} \in \{0,1\}^n$ ($m_i = 1$ iff $f_i$ selected), our three objectives are:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{m} \in \{0,1\}^n} \;\; \bigl( \underbrace{\|\mathbf{m}\|_0}_{\text{cardinality}},\;\; \underbrace{1 - \text{AUC}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{m}})}_{\text{error}},\;\; \underbrace{T_{\text{train}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{m}})}_{\text{training time}} \bigr).
\label{eq:mo}
\end{equation}

\subsection{ShapG Overview}

ShapG~\citep{zhao2025shapg} approximates Shapley values on a feature correlation graph $G_{\text{corr}}$: it builds the $p \times p$ Pearson matrix, reduces it to a minimal connected graph by inserting edges in descending $|\rho|$ (Union-Find), and for each feature evaluates coalitions within its depth-$d$ neighborhood, at $O(p \cdot 2^{\,d})$ cost for $d \in \{1, 2\}$. ShapG is both a baseline (Section~6) and the correlation oracle for Algorithm~B (Section~\ref{sec:estimation}).

\section{MinPPI-MOFS Framework}
\label{sec:method}

We develop the framework in four stages: voting-game formulation (\S\ref{sec:voting-game}), min-marginal power indices (\S\ref{sec:minppi}), estimation algorithms (\S\ref{sec:estimation}), and the complete MOFS-MinPPI algorithm (\S\ref{sec:mofs-minppi}). Section~\ref{sec:discussion} addresses two concerns a reviewer will raise: the WVG-FS additivity flaw and the spurious-interaction problem~\citep{rabitti2019shapley}. All theoretical claims are verified numerically on constructed voting-game instances, and the full method is evaluated on five real-world datasets in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. All proofs are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\subsection{Voting Game Formulation for Feature Selection}
\label{sec:voting-game}

Recall from Section~\ref{sec:prelim} the dataset $\mathcal{D}$, feature set $N_1 = \{f_1, \dots, f_n\}$, base learner $\mathcal{M}_S$, and full-feature utility $U_{\text{full}}$.

\textbf{Definition 1 (Utility-Threshold Voting Game).}
The pair $(N_1, v_\alpha)$ with $v_\alpha$ defined by~\eqref{eq:valpha} is a \emph{utility-threshold voting game} (UTVG). Coalitions with $v_\alpha(S) = 1$ are \emph{winning}. We use $\alpha = 0.95$ as the primary quota ($\alpha \in \{0.85, 0.90, 0.95, 0.99\}$ in the Appendix). The utility $U$ is task-dependent; on heavily imbalanced data a rank- or margin-based $U$ (AUC, $F_1$) is preferable to raw accuracy, whose majority-class baseline can exceed $\alpha\,U_{\text{full}}$ and make every coalition winning.

\textbf{Remark 1.}
Unlike a classical WVG~\citep{owen1972multilinear}, $v_\alpha$ is \emph{not} additive: it is evaluated by training $\mathcal{M}_S$, eliminating the structural inaccuracy of the additive surrogate (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\textbf{Remark 2 (Non-monotonicity).}
$v_\alpha$ is not guaranteed monotone: $S \subset T$ with $v_\alpha(S) = 1$ and $v_\alpha(T) = 0$ is possible when added features introduce noise, redundancy, or overfitting. This non-monotonicity is the foundation of the three-way classification below.

\subsection{Min-Marginal Power Indices (MinPPI)}
\label{sec:minppi}

Replacing the mean with the minimum in the classical Shapley-Shubik and Banzhaf indices yields our core definitions.

\textbf{Definition 2 (Min Shapley-Shubik Index, MinSS).}
\begin{equation}
\text{MinSS}_i = \min_{\pi \in \Pi(N_1)} \bigl[\, v_\alpha(S_\pi^i \cup \{i\}) - v_\alpha(S_\pi^i) \,\bigr],
\label{eq:minss}
\end{equation}
where $\Pi(N_1)$ is the set of all $n!$ permutations and $S_\pi^i \subseteq N_1 \setminus \{i\}$ denotes the features preceding $i$ in permutation $\pi$.

\textbf{Definition 3 (Min Banzhaf Index, MinBz).}
\begin{equation}
\text{MinBz}_i = \min_{S \subseteq N_1 \setminus \{i\}} \bigl[\, v_\alpha(S \cup \{i\}) - v_\alpha(S) \,\bigr].
\label{eq:minbz}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Property 1 (Boundedness, Trinary Range).}
For every $i \in N_1$, $\text{MinSS}_i, \text{MinBz}_i \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$.

While the mean Shapley-Shubik index takes continuous values in $[-1, 1]$ without natural clusters, Property~1 yields a discrete ternary signal that supports actionable decisions directly. Property~2 assigns semantic content to each value.

\textbf{Property 2 (Three-Way Decisiveness Classification).}
Under $(N_1, v_\alpha)$, for any $i \in N_1$:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] $\text{MinSS}_i = +1$ \textbf{(decisive)} $\iff$ $\forall S \subseteq N_1\setminus\{i\}: v_\alpha(S)=0 \land v_\alpha(S\cup\{i\})=1$.
\item[(b)] $\text{MinSS}_i = -1$ \textbf{(harmful)} $\iff$ $\exists S \subseteq N_1\setminus\{i\}: v_\alpha(S)=1 \land v_\alpha(S\cup\{i\})=0$.
\item[(c)] $\text{MinSS}_i = 0$ \textbf{(neutral)} otherwise.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The non-trivial separation.} The trichotomy itself follows directly from $v_\alpha \in \{0,1\}$; its substantive content is the following separation. ``$i$ appears in every winning coalition'' ($\forall S: v_\alpha(S)=0$ when $i\notin S$) is \emph{insufficient} for $\text{MinSS}_i=+1$; the second half of (a) is also required. A concrete counterexample (verified numerically on a constructed game) illustrates: feature $f_0$ belongs to all 13 winning coalitions yet $\text{MinSS}(f_0)=0$. This is precisely where the min operator carries information the mean cannot.

\textbf{Practical interpretation.} The sign of $\text{MinSS}_i$ is directly actionable: $-1$ means \textbf{must remove} (a harmful context exists); $+1$ means \textbf{must retain} (dictator-like, rarely observed); $0$ means \textbf{defer} to the mean Shapley-Shubik index or NSGA-II fitness. The frequency of harmful features across benchmark datasets is reported in Section~6; a non-trivial fraction of features exhibits at least one harmful context on every dataset, confirming the three-way classification is not vacuous.

\textbf{Property 3 (MinSS = MinBz).}
For every $i \in N_1$, $\text{MinSS}_i = \text{MinBz}_i$.

We unify both indices under $\text{MinPPI}_i$ and use $\text{MinBz}$ for computation, since $2^{n-1}$ subsets are more tractable than $n!$ permutations. Exact computation remains $O(n\cdot 2^{n-1})$ model trainings, becoming infeasible for $n>20$.

\subsection{Estimation of MinPPI}
\label{sec:estimation}

Exact computation of $\text{MinPPI}_i$ requires $2^{n-1}$ model trainings per feature, prohibitive for $n > 20$. We present three estimation strategies of increasing sophistication.

\subsubsection{Random Monte Carlo Sampling (Algorithm~A)}

Algorithm~A draws $K$ coalitions $S^{(1)},\dots,S^{(K)}$ by, for each draw, sampling a size $\kappa$ uniformly from $\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}$ and then a $\kappa$-subset of $N_1\setminus\{i\}$ uniformly at random; it returns $\widehat{\text{MinPPI}}_i^{\text{MC}} = \min_{k} [v_\alpha(S^{(k)}\cup\{i\}) - v_\alpha(S^{(k)})]$. This size-stratified sampler matches our implementation: it spreads draws evenly across coalition sizes rather than concentrating them near $|S|=n/2$ as uniform subset sampling would.

\textbf{Lemma 1 (Upward Bias).}
$\widehat{\text{MinPPI}}_i^{\text{MC}} \geq \text{MinPPI}_i$ almost surely.

Algorithm~A is \emph{safe}: it may miss harmful features but never misclassifies a neutral feature.

\textbf{Proposition 1 (Detection Probability).}
Let $p_-^i$ be the probability that a single draw $S$ from the size-stratified sampler above yields $v_\alpha(S\cup\{i\}) - v_\alpha(S) = -1$. If $\text{MinPPI}_i = -1$, then
\begin{equation}
\Pr[\widehat{\text{MinPPI}}_i^{\text{MC}} > -1] = (1 - p_-^i)^K,
\label{eq:prop1}
\end{equation}
requiring $K \geq \log\delta / \log(1-p_-^i) \approx \log(1/\delta)/p_-^i$ for confidence $1-\delta$.

When harmful contexts are sparse ($p_-^i \ll 1$), $K$ grows prohibitively. Figure~\ref{fig:prop1} validates~\eqref{eq:prop1} on an engineered game with $p_-^i = 0.0333$ (500 trials per $K$, Pearson $r = 0.9993$). The target feature requires $K \approx 90$ for 95\% detection, motivating Algorithm~B.

\subsubsection{ShapG-Guided Adversarial Search (Algorithm~B)}

Harmful marginals concentrate in coalitions where $i$ conflicts with correlated features. Algorithm~B exploits ShapG's correlation graph $G_{\text{corr}}$~\citep{zhao2025shapg} to target these vulnerable regions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $C$ be the top-$k$ neighbors of $i$ in $G_{\text{corr}}$ (by absolute edge weight), with $k = \min(10, \lfloor n/5\rfloor)$. Construct a seed $S_{\text{seed}}$ from $C$ plus high-AUC baseline features.
\item For $r=1,\dots,K_{\text{adv}}$, perturb $S_{\text{seed}}$ with retention bias toward $C$, evaluate $v_\alpha(S\cup\{i\})-v_\alpha(S)$, and return the min; break if $-1$ is found.
\end{enumerate}

On the engineered voting game ($p_-^i=0.033$), Algorithm~B achieves \textbf{100\% detection at $K_{\text{adv}}=1$} versus Algorithm~A's $\approx 3\%$ at the same budget (Figure~\ref{fig:alg_b_vs_a}(a), Table~\ref{tab:alg_ab_comparison}). This gain is contingent on the harmful witness lying in the ShapG neighborhood (Theorem~2) and shrinks under correlation-matrix noise. Under Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ on the correlation matrix (Figure~\ref{fig:alg_b_vs_a}(b)), Algorithm~B degrades gracefully: detection at $K_{\text{adv}}=5$ remains $1.000, 0.878, 0.652, 0.508$ for $\sigma = 0.05, 0.15, 0.30, 0.50$, each dominating Algorithm~A's baseline ($\leq 0.172$).

\subsubsection{Hybrid Estimation Scheme}

Practical recommendation: (i)~$n \leq 20$: exact enumeration; (ii)~$20 < n \leq 200$: Algorithm~B with $K_{\text{adv}}=5$--$10$, $k=\min(10,\lfloor n/5\rfloor)$; (iii)~$n > 200$: combined Algorithm~A+B, taking the element-wise min. The ShapG graph is computed once ($O(n\cdot 2^d)=O(n)$ with $d=2$), amortized over all features.

\subsection{MOFS-MinPPI Algorithm}
\label{sec:mofs-minppi}

A feature subset is encoded as $\mathbf{m}\in\{0,1\}^n$. The three objectives are:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\{0,1\}^n} \bigl( \|\mathbf{m}\|_0,\; 1 - \text{AUC}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{m}}),\; T_{\text{train}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{m}}) \bigr).
\label{eq:mo-obj}
\end{equation}
NSGA-II~\citep{deb2002fast} performs the search. We replace uniform genetic operators with MinPPI-guided variants.

\subsubsection{MinPPI-Guided Mutation}

The per-feature flip probability is modulated by MinPPI classification:
\begin{equation}
p_{\text{flip}}(i) = \begin{cases}
p_{\text{base}} \cdot \exp(-\beta) & \text{if } \text{MinPPI}_i = +1 \text{ (protect)} \\[4pt]
p_{\text{base}} \cdot \exp(+\beta) & \text{if } \text{MinPPI}_i = -1 \text{ (encourage change)} \\[4pt]
p_{\text{base}} & \text{if } \text{MinPPI}_i = 0
\end{cases}
\label{eq:mutation}
\end{equation}
with $\beta \geq 0$ controlling guidance strength. Direction is biased: harmful features ($m_i=1$, $\text{MinPPI}_i=-1$) preferentially flip to $0$; decisive features ($m_i=0$, $\text{MinPPI}_i=+1$) to $1$, each by $\exp(\beta)$. $\beta=0$ recovers uniform mutation. The range $\beta \in \{0,1,2,5\}$ is explored in Section~6.

\subsubsection{MinPPI-Guided Crossover}

Decisive features adjacent in $G_{\text{corr}}$ (edge weight $> \tau=0.3$) form \emph{decisive groups}. Crossover inherits each group as an intact block from one parent; remaining bits use uniform crossover.

\subsubsection{Complete Algorithm}

\textbf{MOFS-MinPPI} operates in two phases. \emph{Phase~1:} Compute $G_{\text{corr}}$ from $\mathcal{D}$, then estimate $\text{MinPPI}_i$ via the hybrid scheme for all features. \emph{Phase~2:} Initialize $P_0$ with $N_{\text{pop}}$ random masks; for $g=1,\dots,G_{\max}$, generate offspring via MinPPI-guided crossover and mutation, evaluate on the three objectives, and select survivors via NSGA-II non-dominated sorting and crowding distance. Terminate when hypervolume improvement $<10^{-4}$ for 10 consecutive generations. Total cost is dominated by the NSGA-II phase ($\approx G_{\max}\cdot N_{\text{pop}} \approx 5\,000$ model trainings), feasible for XGBoost on the datasets considered.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Avoiding the WVG-FS additivity flaw.} WVG-FS methods approximate $v(S) \approx \mathbb{1}[\sum_{i\in S} w_i \geq q]$, which is structurally wrong for non-linear models (synergies undervalued, redundancies overvalued). MOFS-MinPPI decouples three roles: $v_\alpha$ is evaluated by training $\mathcal{M}_S$ (no additive surrogate); MinPPI is only soft guidance for variation operators; and NSGA-II selection on true objectives makes the final Pareto decision. Any MinPPI estimation error therefore affects convergence speed, not Pareto-optimality (Proposition~2).

\textbf{Spurious interactions under correlation.}~\citet{rabitti2019shapley} (Section~7.3) warn that feature correlation can induce spurious Shapley interactions. We mitigate this three ways: binary thresholding absorbs minor utility differences; the min operator evaluates every context, including those where correlated partners are absent, avoiding false-positive harmful labels; and $G_{\text{corr}}$ makes correlation structure auditable. We do not resolve the general problem~\citep{frye2020shapley,aas2021explaining,sundararajan2020many}, but our coarser question (``does adding $i$ change whether the model meets the quota?'') is more robust than estimating exact interaction values.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

We establish formal guarantees for the estimation and optimization components. Proofs are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\subsection{Sample Complexity of Algorithm~A}

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Sample Complexity).}
Let $\mathcal{H} = \{i : \text{MinPPI}_i = -1\}$ and $p_{\min} = \min_{i\in\mathcal{H}} p_-^i > 0$. For confidence $\delta\in(0,1)$, Algorithm~A with
\begin{equation}
K \geq \frac{\log(|\mathcal{H}| / \delta)}{\log(1/(1-p_{\min}))} \approx \frac{\log(|\mathcal{H}|/\delta)}{p_{\min}}
\label{eq:thm2}
\end{equation}
samples per feature detects all harmful features with probability $\geq 1-\delta$.

Thus $K = \Omega(1/p_{\min})$: with $n=500$ and $p_{\min} \approx 0.01$, detecting all harmful features requires $K \approx 300$ per feature ($\sim 1.5 \times 10^5$ trainings total). Empirical verification confirms theory matches observation ($r > 0.999$; Figure~\ref{fig:prop1}).

\subsection{Sample Efficiency of Algorithm~B}

\textbf{Theorem 2 (ShapG-Guided Efficiency).}
For each $i\in\mathcal{H}$, let $C_i$ be its top-$k$ ShapG neighbors and suppose there exists a harmful witness $S_i^*$ ($v_\alpha(S_i^*)=1$, $v_\alpha(S_i^*\cup\{i\})=0$) and a set $B$ of individually high-AUC baseline features such that
\begin{equation}
S_i^* \subseteq C_i \cup B \;=:\; S_{\text{seed}}.
\label{eq:structural-cond}
\end{equation}
Suppose each adversarial perturbation independently retains every $j\in S_{\text{seed}}$ with probability $q_j\in(0,1]$ (biased high for correlated neighbors) and excludes every $j\notin S_{\text{seed}}$. Let
\begin{equation}
\rho_i \;=\; \Big(\prod_{j \in S_i^*} q_j\Big)\Big(\prod_{j \in S_{\text{seed}}\setminus S_i^*} (1 - q_j)\Big) \;>\; 0.
\label{eq:thm3}
\end{equation}
Then Algorithm~B with seed $S_{\text{seed}}$ detects $i$ within $K_{\text{adv}}$ perturbations with probability $\Pr[\text{detection}] \geq 1 - (1-\rho_i)^{K_{\text{adv}}}$. In the noiseless case ($S_{\text{seed}}=S_i^*$), the unperturbed seed is itself the witness, so detection is \textbf{deterministic} at $K_{\text{adv}}=1$.

\textbf{Remark 3 (monotone-harm variant).} If the harmful interaction persists on supersets of the witness within the seed---i.e.\ $v_\alpha(S)=1 \wedge v_\alpha(S\cup\{i\})=0$ for all $S$ with $S_i^*\subseteq S\subseteq S_{\text{seed}}$---then retaining $S_i^*$ alone suffices irrespective of the other seed members, and the bound sharpens to $\Pr[\text{detection}]\geq 1-(1-\prod_{j\in S_i^*}q_j)^{K_{\text{adv}}}$. We do \emph{not} assume this in general, since $v_\alpha$ is non-monotone (Remark~2); equation~\eqref{eq:thm3} keeps the exclusion factor for that reason.

The structural condition is empirically plausible: harmful features are typically redundant with correlated neighbors ($C_i$) in the presence of strong baseline features ($B$). Robustness experiments (Section~\ref{sec:estimation}) confirm detection rates remain well above Algorithm~A even under $\sigma=0.50$ noise.

\subsection{Pareto Preservation Under Imperfect MinPPI}

\textbf{Proposition 2a (Selection Soundness, finite $G_{\max}$).}
Let $\widehat{\text{MinPPI}}$ be any (even adversarially incorrect) estimate. In each generation, MOFS-MinPPI selects survivors from the merged pool $\mathcal{R}_g = P_{g-1} \cup P_{\text{offspring}}$ by non-dominated sorting and crowding distance applied to the true objective $f(\mathbf{m})=(\|\mathbf{m}\|_0,\,1-\text{AUC}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{m}}),\,T_{\text{train}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{m}}))$ alone. Then (i) the selection outcome is independent of $\widehat{\text{MinPPI}}$; (ii) whenever the population size $N\geq|\mathcal{F}_1(\mathcal{R}_g)|$, the entire first non-dominated front of $\mathcal{R}_g$ survives, so no $f$-dominated solution displaces an $f$-non-dominated one; and (iii) the returned set $P_{\text{out}}$ is mutually non-dominated under $f$.

\textbf{Proposition 2b (Asymptotic Optimality).}
If $\beta<\infty$---so bit-flip mutation~\eqref{eq:mutation} assigns every $\mathbf{m}\in\{0,1\}^n$ positive probability each generation---and selection is elitist, then as $G_{\max}\to\infty$ the returned front converges almost surely to the true Pareto front $P^*_{\text{true}}$.

\textbf{Scope and implication.} For finite $G_{\max}$ we guarantee only that $P_{\text{out}}$ is internally non-dominated (Prop.~2a), \emph{not} $P_{\text{out}}\subseteq P^*_{\text{true}}$; global optimality is the asymptotic statement (Prop.~2b). Within this scope MinPPI is a \emph{safe} guidance signal: it shapes which candidates are proposed and how fast the front converges, while selection always defers to the true objective. Any importance method may thus be substituted without compromising the internal consistency of the returned front.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Property~1]
For any $S \subseteq N_1$, the marginal $\Delta_i(S) = v_\alpha(S \cup \{i\}) - v_\alpha(S)$ takes values in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$ because $v_\alpha \in \{0,1\}$. Both $\text{MinSS}_i$ and $\text{MinBz}_i$ are minima of subsets of $\{-1, 0, +1\}$; the minimum of any such subset is $-1$ if any element equals $-1$, $+1$ iff all elements equal $+1$, and $0$ otherwise. Hence it remains in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Property~2]
\textbf{(a)} $(\Rightarrow)$ $\text{MinSS}_i = +1$ means every $\pi$ yields marginal $+1$, forcing $v_\alpha(S_\pi^i) = 0$ and $v_\alpha(S_\pi^i \cup \{i\}) = 1$ for every $\pi$. As $\pi$ runs over all permutations, $S_\pi^i$ runs over all subsets of $N_1 \setminus \{i\}$ (each $S$ appears as a prefix for $|S|!\,(n-|S|-1)!$ permutations). Hence $\forall S \subseteq N_1\setminus\{i\}: v_\alpha(S)=0 \land v_\alpha(S\cup\{i\})=1$. $(\Leftarrow)$ If the condition holds, every marginal is $1 - 0 = +1$, so $\min = +1$.

\textbf{(b)} $\text{MinSS}_i = -1$ iff there exists a permutation with $-1$ marginal, i.e., $v_\alpha(S_\pi^i) = 1$ and $v_\alpha(S_\pi^i \cup \{i\}) = 0$. Letting $S = S_\pi^i$ gives the witness. Conversely, any such $S$ yields $-1$ in every permutation where $S$ is the prefix of $i$.

\textbf{(c)} By Property~1, if $\text{MinSS}_i$ is neither $+1$ nor $-1$, it must be $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Property~3]
$\text{MinSS}_i = \min_{\pi \in \Pi(N_1)} \Delta_i(S_\pi^i)$ and $\text{MinBz}_i = \min_{S \subseteq N_1 \setminus \{i\}} \Delta_i(S)$. The collection $\{S_\pi^i : \pi \in \Pi(N_1)\}$ equals $2^{N_1 \setminus \{i\}}$, because each $S \subseteq N_1 \setminus \{i\}$ appears as a prefix for exactly $|S|!\,(n-|S|-1)!$ permutations. Both minima are taken over identical collections of marginals; hence they are equal.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~1]
Let $\mathcal{M}_i = \{\Delta_i(S) : S \subseteq N_1 \setminus \{i\}\}$ be the population of all $2^{n-1}$ marginals, and $\mathcal{S}_K$ the $K$ sampled subsets. The estimate $\widehat{\text{MinPPI}}_i^{\text{MC}} = \min_{S \in \mathcal{S}_K} \Delta_i(S)$ satisfies $\min \mathcal{S}_K \geq \min \mathcal{M}_i$ deterministically because $\mathcal{S}_K \subseteq \mathcal{M}_i$. Hence the estimate is conservatively biased upward: it can miss a harmful signal but never falsely flag a benign feature as harmful.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~1]
Let $\mathcal{H}_i = \{S \subseteq N_1 \setminus \{i\} : \Delta_i(S) = -1\}$ be the harmful coalitions and $p_-^i$ their total probability mass under the size-stratified sampler. Algorithm~A fails iff none of the $K$ i.i.d.\ draws lands in $\mathcal{H}_i$; independence gives $\Pr[\text{miss}] = (1 - p_-^i)^K$. Setting this $\leq \delta$ and solving yields the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~1]
For each $i \in \mathcal{H}$, Proposition~1 gives $\Pr[\text{miss } i] = (1 - p_-^i)^K \leq (1 - p_{\min})^K$. By the union bound, $\Pr[\text{any miss}] \leq |\mathcal{H}| \cdot (1 - p_{\min})^K$. Setting this $\leq \delta$ and solving for $K$ yields~\eqref{eq:thm2}; the approximation uses $-\log(1-p_{\min}) \approx p_{\min}$ for small $p_{\min}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~2]
By~\eqref{eq:structural-cond}, $S_i^*\subseteq S_{\text{seed}}$. A single perturbation reproduces exactly $S_i^*$ when every $j\in S_i^*$ is retained (probability $\prod_{j\in S_i^*}q_j$) and every $j\in S_{\text{seed}}\setminus S_i^*$ is dropped (probability $\prod_{j\in S_{\text{seed}}\setminus S_i^*}(1-q_j)$); these events are independent, so $\Pr[S=S_i^*]=\rho_i$. When $S=S_i^*$ the marginal is $v_\alpha(S_i^*\cup\{i\})-v_\alpha(S_i^*)=-1$ and $i$ is detected. Over $K_{\text{adv}}$ independent perturbations, the probability of at least one hit is $1-(1-\rho_i)^{K_{\text{adv}}}$. If $S_{\text{seed}}=S_i^*$, the seed evaluated before any perturbation is already the witness, giving deterministic detection at $K_{\text{adv}}=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~2a]
$\widehat{\text{MinPPI}}$ enters only the generation operators (mutation~\eqref{eq:mutation}, crossover), which determine \emph{which} candidates populate $P_{\text{offspring}}$; once generated, a candidate is scored solely by $f$. Non-dominated sorting and crowding distance are functions of the $f$-values of $\mathcal{R}_g$ only, giving (i). The first front $\mathcal{F}_1(\mathcal{R}_g)$ ranks ahead of all dominated solutions; if $N\geq|\mathcal{F}_1(\mathcal{R}_g)|$ it is admitted in full, so dominated solutions can occupy only the remaining slots, giving (ii). At termination $P_{\text{out}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_1(\mathcal{R}_{G_{\max}})$, whose members are pairwise non-dominated, giving (iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~2b]
With $\beta<\infty$ the per-bit flip probability lies in $(0,1)$, so from any state the offspring distribution places positive probability on every point of $\{0,1\}^n$; the induced Markov chain over populations is therefore irreducible. Elitism guarantees that an $f$-non-dominated solution, once present, is never replaced by a solution it dominates (Prop.~2a(ii)). Irreducibility ensures every $\mathbf{m}\in P^*_{\text{true}}$ is generated in finite expected time and, being non-dominated, retained thereafter, while any dominated incumbent is displaced once a dominating point is sampled. Hence the front converges almost surely to $P^*_{\text{true}}$. The estimate $\widehat{\text{MinPPI}}$ alters the transition kernel but preserves its positivity, so it affects only the expected hitting times---the convergence speed---not the limit.
\end{proof}
